# Air — Composed Cells (v5-a, RE-RUN)

Assignment: AIR element, actions STRIKE, LASH, RAKE, SHOVE only. Composition Brief v5. This is a re-run after a prior attempt over-kept (~70% of all intersections, a 7,005-name strike cell including "Airy Thump" and "Breezy Wallop"). Calibration for this run: K is an affirmative judgment requiring BOTH honest double-definition AND the say-it-out-loud test. Quality/state adjectives (airy, breezy, balmy, stale, muggy, fresh, brisk) cannot deliver mass-impact verbs — a breeze cannot wallop. This is judged per-name (rule 8, Section 7), never as a class, but the calibration is applied honestly throughout.

## Reconciliation note on row count

The assignment cites step0-air.md as having "~252 unique after exact-spelling dedup." The source file's own summary tables report 233 PRODUCTIVE rows (82 adjectives + 60 nouns + 91 verbs/gerunds), but that count double-counts words with both a noun and verb sense listed as separate rows (e.g. "Gust," "Whirl," "Eddy," "Soar," "Glide," "Swoop," "Dive," "Push," "Shove," "Release," "Escape," "Dodge," "Feint," "Sidestep," "Lift," "Free," "Billow," and others each appear twice — once per part of speech). Deduplicating by exact spelling (case-insensitive) across all three tables yields **253 unique productive words**, which matches the assignment's "~252" figure. This composition uses that 253-word deduplicated list as the authoritative row set (reproduced in Section 0 below), collapsing dual-part-of-speech entries into one row since a name-composition slot does not care whether "Gust" is functioning as noun or verb in a given pairing — both senses are available to the composer either way.

## RAKE cell

RAKE demands rapid repeated cutting/tearing — a mechanism with an edge (claw, talon, feather-blade) delivering many small strikes. Air's own harvest richness note flags this as one of the two weakest categories, requiring anatomy-adjacent terms (talon, feather) to fill honestly. This composer agrees: air alone (as bare moving gas) has no edge and cannot rake; the honest cell here is almost entirely anatomy-tagged (wing/talon/feather) or grit-driven abrasion (which the standing rulings reserve — sand/dust is Sand's, so abrasion words that require particulate matter are excluded), leaving a genuinely narrower cell than STRIKE/LASH. This is the correct outcome per calibration, not underperformance.

**Step A — standalone.** No air word alone reads as a rake-name; air has no inherent edge. None kept standalone.
