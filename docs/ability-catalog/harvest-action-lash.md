# Harvest: Action Field — LASH ("a sweeping arc hitting what it passes")

Covers whip/flail vocabulary, mowing/scything, and general sweeping-motion words. Scope: the ARC MOTION itself, element-neutral where possible, applicable to any element medium delivering a whipping/sweeping/scything/flailing strike.

## 1. Nouns

- Lash — a single sweeping stroke, especially of something flexible
- Whip — a flexible striking implement or the sweeping stroke it makes
- Whiplash — the sudden violent snap-back of a sweeping strike
- Flail — a swinging, uncontrolled sweeping strike
- Swipe — a broad sweeping stroke, often one-handed
- Sweep — the broad arcing motion itself
- Scythe — a long curved blade swept low in an arc; also used verbally
- Scything — the mowing motion of a scythe-like sweep
- Arc — the curved path of a sweeping strike
- Swing — the pendulum motion preceding a sweeping impact
- Slash — a sweeping cut, usually with an edge
- Crosscut — a sweeping stroke that crosses the body or target laterally
- Backswing — the return arc of a sweeping motion
- Sidesweep — a lateral arcing strike
- Cleave — a sweeping strike that splits or divides
- Reap — the sweeping harvest-motion of cutting down in an arc
- Mow — the sweeping, ground-level cutting motion
- Thrash — a violent whipping, sweeping series of blows
- Lashing — the act or result of striking with a whip-like sweep
- Wallop — a broad swinging blow (informal but standard English, not slang)
- Cartwheel strike — descriptive compound for a full rotational sweep (borderline; verify naturalness)
- Coil-lash — a sweep preceded by a winding coil (anatomy-implying, see section 4)
- Tail-sweep — a sweep delivered by a tail-like appendage
- Sickle sweep — a curved, hooking sweeping strike
- Flick — a short, quick whip-like snap
- Snap — the fast terminal motion of a whip's arc
- Crack — the whip-like report/motion at the end of a lash
- Riposte-sweep — not natural, exclude
- Windmill — a full circular sweeping motion
- Roundhouse — a wide circular sweeping strike
- Fan strike — a sweep that spreads wide like an opening fan
- Scourge — a whip or lashing instrument, also the act of lashing repeatedly

## 2. Verbs / gerunds

- Lash / lashing — to strike with a sudden sweeping, whip-like motion
- Whip / whipping — to strike with a fast flexible sweeping stroke
- Flail / flailing — to swing wildly and repeatedly in uncontrolled arcs
- Sweep / sweeping — to move broadly through an arc, catching everything in the path
- Swipe / swiping — to strike with a quick broad sweeping motion
- Scythe / scything — to cut down in a low sweeping arc, as with a scythe
- Slash / slashing — to cut with a broad sweeping stroke
- Reap / reaping — to sweep through and cut down in one motion
- Mow / mowing — to cut down in a sweeping, ground-level pass
- Thrash / thrashing — to strike repeatedly with violent whipping motion
- Scourge / scourging — to lash repeatedly and punishingly
- Crack / cracking — to snap a whip-like limb through the air with force
- Snap / snapping — to close an arc suddenly at its terminal point
- Coil / coiling — to wind before releasing into a sweeping strike (anatomy-implying)
- Uncoil / uncoiling — to release a wound body part into a sweeping arc
- Wheel / wheeling — to sweep in a wide circular motion
- Arc / arcing — to travel along a curved sweeping path
- Fan / fanning — to sweep in a widening arc
- Rake — reserved for the RAKE action (rapid repeated cuts); exclude from lash to avoid overlap
- Scoop / scooping — a low rising sweep (borderline; usually SHOVE domain, exclude here)
- Cartwheel / cartwheeling — to rotate through a full sweeping circle
- Swat / swatting — a short sweeping strike, usually downward or lateral
- Buffet / buffeting — to strike repeatedly with sweeping blows (also weather-adjacent, usable)
- Scud — to sweep low and fast (mostly weather; borderline, see exclusions)

## 6. Honest richness assessment

The LASH field is very rich. English has an unusually deep and distinct vocabulary for "flexible or curved implement swept through an arc" — whip, lash, flail, scourge, scythe, sickle, sweep, swipe, cleave, reap, mow — each carrying a slightly different connotation (punishing vs. harvesting vs. wild vs. controlled). The sub-concept of "coiled limb releasing into a strike" (coil/uncoil) is especially strong and gives a natural anatomy hook distinct from other actions. The "harvest" register (scythe, reap, mow, scything) is a genuinely rich and underused sub-field that reads well as element-neutral. The "whip crack" sub-field (crack, snap, whiplash) gives good onomatopoeic/sonic candidates. The weakest sub-concept is fully circular/rotational sweeps (windmill, cartwheel, roundhouse) — these words exist but skew toward informal or sport registers and needed care to keep natural; roundhouse in particular has fighting-game connotations worth flagging for curation review even though it's a real English word (not itself a borrowed move name, but adjacent). Overall: strong field, no shortage of neutral candidates, clean separation achievable from RAKE (repeated cuts, not a single arc) and CRUSH (compression, not motion).

## Excluded as other-domain / adjacent-action boundaries

- Rake, raking — reserved for the RAKE action (rapid repeated cuts); a single lash is one arc, rake is many rapid strikes, so excluded here despite similar imagery
- Scoop, scooping — leans SHOVE (displacing/lifting a target) rather than a sweeping arc that "hits what it passes"
- Buffet, buffeting — primarily weather/impact domain (wind or blows battering something); included cautiously in verbs as it can describe a sweeping series of strikes, but flagged as borderline
- Scud — primarily meteorological (clouds/rain sweeping low and fast); excluded as other-domain (weather), not an action-of-striking word
- Cartwheel strike — borderline naturalness as a two-word collocation; kept in nouns for reference but not promoted to a clean candidate name
