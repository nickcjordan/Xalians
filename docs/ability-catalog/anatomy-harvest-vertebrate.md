# Anatomy Harvest — Terrestrial Vertebrates (Mammals, Reptiles, Amphibians)

Domain assignment: heads (jaws, fangs, tusks, horns, antlers, beaks on non-birds, trunks, proboscises), limbs (claws, paws, hooves, forelimbs as bludgeons), tails (clubs, whips, prehensile), body armor and display (shells, plates, spines, quills, crests, sails, manes), amphibian/reptile specialties (tongue-lash, constricting coils, venom delivery).

---

## Head / jaw / bite group

### `fangs`
- **What it is:** Elongated, sharp piercing teeth, typically paired, often hollow or grooved for venom delivery.
- **Actions enabled:** strike, crush, drain, ambush
- **Example creatures:** viper, cobra, saber-toothed cat, vampire bat
- **Notes:** Sibling to `jaws` (fangs are a specialization of the bite, not the whole mechanism). Overlaps with `venom-glands`/`venom-delivery` when hollow. Distinct from `tusks` (fangs are for piercing/gripping flesh; tusks are for digging/goring, usually not retractable into a bite).

### `jaws`
- **What it is:** The full biting mechanism — upper and lower mandible with a full tooth row, capable of clamping, shearing, or crushing.
- **Actions enabled:** crush, strike, rake, shove
- **Example creatures:** crocodile, wolf, bear, snapping turtle
- **Notes:** Broader/parent concept to `fangs`, `tusks`, `beak`. A creature might have only `jaws` (generalized bite) without a more specialized fang/tusk/beak entry.

### `tusks`
- **What it is:** Enlarged, usually permanently exposed teeth (often modified canines or incisors) projecting outside the mouth, used for goring, digging, or display.
- **Actions enabled:** strike, shove, rake, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** elephant, wild boar, walrus, warthog
- **Notes:** Distinct from `fangs` — tusks are external/visible even when mouth closed, more blunt-to-goring than piercing-to-envenomate. Overlaps with `horns` in the "goring" action role but is a dental structure, not a cranial outgrowth.

### `horns`
- **What it is:** Permanent, unbranched bony/keratinous cranial projections (true horns, keeping a bony core covered in keratin sheath).
- **Actions enabled:** strike, shove, crush, terrorize, ward
- **Example creatures:** rhinoceros, bull, ram, triceratops-type reptile
- **Notes:** Distinct from `antlers` (horns are permanent and unbranched typically; antlers are shed annually and branched). `ward` applies when horns are framed defensively (e.g., lowered head to block/deter).

### `antlers`
- **What it is:** Branched, bony cranial growths, shed and regrown annually, used in sparring and display.
- **Actions enabled:** strike, shove, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** deer, elk, moose, caribou
- **Notes:** Sibling/variant of `horns`; keep separate because branching + shedding + "sparring rack" connotation differs meaningfully in flavor even if mechanically similar. Also has a strong display-only reading, hence `terrorize`.

### `beak` (non-bird / reptile-adjacent)
- **What it is:** A hardened, keratinous sheath over the jaws forming a hooked or shearing cutting edge, without exposed teeth.
- **Actions enabled:** strike, crush, rake
- **Example creatures:** snapping turtle, tortoise, platypus (bill), parrot-beaked dinosaur-type reptile
- **Notes:** Explicitly called out in brief as relevant even on non-birds. Distinct from `jaws` by material (keratin sheath vs. teeth) and shape (hooked cutting edge).

### `trunk` / `proboscis`
- **What it is:** A long, muscular, prehensile nasal extension used for grasping, striking, and manipulating objects.
- **Actions enabled:** lash, strike, shove, snare, spray
- **Example creatures:** elephant, tapir (short proboscis), star-nosed mole (sensory proboscis variant)
- **Notes:** `spray` applies via a trunk's ability to draft and blast water/dust. Dual-role: primarily manipulation/action, but also highly sensory (smell/touch) — call out per brief's dual-role rule, similar to antennae/whiskers.

### `tongue-lash` (amphibian/reptile specialty)
- **What it is:** An extremely elongated, sticky, or rapidly-extending tongue used to snare prey at range.
- **Actions enabled:** lash, snare, drain
- **Example creatures:** chameleon, toad, frog, giant salamander
- **Notes:** Explicitly requested amphibian/reptile specialty. Distinct action profile from other lash-type parts (whip-tail, tentacle) in that it's specifically prey-capture-at-range with adhesion, not blunt impact.

### `crushing-bite` — considered and folded into `jaws`
- Not a separate key; represented by `jaws` with high crush weighting. Noting here per exhaustive-sweep instruction so it isn't silently dropped.

## Limb / claw / paw group

### `claws`
- **What it is:** Sharp, curved keratinous nails on digit tips, used for gripping, tearing, and climbing.
- **Actions enabled:** rake, strike, crush, shove
- **Example creatures:** bear, wolverine, komodo dragon, big cat
- **Notes:** Sibling/variant of `talons` (bird domain) — talons are typically the raptor/bird-of-prey grasping-and-piercing variant; `claws` here is the general mammal/reptile rake-and-grip variant. Keep both as separate keys per brief's own example.

### `paws`
- **What it is:** Soft-padded feet, often clawed, used for batting, swatting, and pinning.
- **Actions enabled:** strike, shove, crush
- **Example creatures:** bear, big cat, dog, badger
- **Notes:** Overlaps heavily with `claws` (paws are the platform, claws are the point); consider merging paws into claws during curation, or keep paws for the blunt "swat/pin" reading distinct from the piercing "rake" reading.

### `hooves`
- **What it is:** Hardened keratinous coverings over the toes of ungulates, used for striking and trampling.
- **Actions enabled:** strike, crush, shove
- **Example creatures:** horse, deer, bull, rhinoceros
- **Notes:** Distinct from `claws`/`paws` — blunt, percussive, no piercing/raking capacity. Strongly associated with rearing/kicking action.

### `forelimbs-as-bludgeon`
- **What it is:** Heavily muscled or clubbed forelegs/forearms used to strike or slam rather than grip.
- **Actions enabled:** strike, crush, shove
- **Example creatures:** kangaroo (kick/punch), gorilla (forearm slam), bear (forelimb swipe), giant ground sloth
- **Notes:** Explicitly requested in brief. Overlaps with `paws` and `claws` when the limb terminates in those structures, but this key isolates the "whole limb as a club" reading (e.g., a kangaroo's tail-braced kick, a gorilla's overhand slam) distinct from a discrete hand/paw action.

### `digging-claws` / `talons-for-digging` — considered
- Folded into `claws` with a digging-context note; not a separate key. Mentioned for completeness (e.g., mole, armadillo, pangolin foreclaws).

### `wing-limbs` (bat) — out of core assignment
- Bats have clawed wing-thumbs; noted but treated as covered by `claws` + a wing/flight key likely owned by a different (avian/aerial) harvest agent. Flagging cross-domain overlap rather than claiming it.

## Tail group

### `tail-club`
- **What it is:** A tail terminating in a heavy, bony, or fused mass used as a blunt weapon.
- **Actions enabled:** strike, crush, shove
- **Example creatures:** ankylosaur-type reptile, glyptodont, armored lizard
- **Notes:** Explicitly requested. Distinct from `whip-tail` by delivering blunt trauma rather than a fast lash; distinct from `prehensile-tail` by rigidity (a club tail is not used for grasping).

### `whip-tail`
- **What it is:** A long, flexible, muscular tail used to lash at speed, sometimes tipped with spikes.
- **Actions enabled:** lash, strike, rake
- **Example creatures:** monitor lizard, crocodile, stegosaur-type reptile (spiked variant), iguana
- **Notes:** Explicitly requested ("tails... whips"). Spiked variant overlaps with `spines`/`quills` at the tip — note as a compound if the schema allows combining parts, otherwise keep whip-tail as the dominant key and treat spikes as flavor.

### `prehensile-tail`
- **What it is:** A grasping tail capable of wrapping around objects or opponents, used to snare, anchor, or restrain.
- **Actions enabled:** snare, shove, ward
- **Example creatures:** opossum, chameleon, monkey, some skinks
- **Notes:** Explicitly requested. Distinct from `constricting-coils` (body-length constriction) by scale — this is a single grasping limb-equivalent, not full-body wrap.

### `constricting-coils` (reptile specialty)
- **What it is:** A long, muscular body capable of wrapping around and tightening on prey to restrain or crush.
- **Actions enabled:** crush, snare, drain
- **Example creatures:** large constricting snake, giant salamander (grip-and-roll), moray-adjacent reptile
- **Notes:** Explicitly requested amphibian/reptile specialty. `drain` applies thematically (constriction cutting off circulation/breath, read as "draining" vitality) even without literal blood-drain anatomy — flag for schema-writer judgment on whether `drain` should require a literal siphoning part instead.

## Body armor / display group

### `shell`
- **What it is:** A rigid, fused carapace/plastron enclosing all or part of the body, providing passive protection and sometimes an active retreat/ram.
- **Actions enabled:** ward, crush, shove
- **Example creatures:** tortoise, turtle, armadillo (shell-like banding), pangolin (scaled variant, see below)
- **Notes:** `crush`/`shove` apply when the shell itself is used offensively (ramming, body-slam) rather than purely passive. Sibling to `plates` — shell is one continuous fused structure; plates are discrete overlapping segments.

### `plates`
- **What it is:** Discrete, overlapping or segmented bony/keratinous armor covering the body, flexible at the joints between segments.
- **Actions enabled:** ward, shove
- **Example creatures:** armadillo, pangolin, crocodile (osteoderms), ankylosaur-type reptile
- **Notes:** Sibling/variant of `shell` — see above. Also relates to `tail-club` in armored reptiles that have both.

### `spines` / `quills`
- **What it is:** Sharp, rigid keratinous or bony projections covering part of the body, defensive by default but able to be driven into an attacker.
- **Actions enabled:** ward, strike, rake
- **Example creatures:** porcupine, hedgehog, spiny lizard, horned toad
- **Notes:** `quills` (mammal, detachable/barbed, e.g. porcupine) and `spines` (reptile/amphibian, fixed, e.g. horned lizard) are close variants; keep as one merged key or two siblings depending on curation preference — flagging the ambiguity rather than deciding.

### `crest`
- **What it is:** A ridge of erectile skin, scales, or bony frill along the head/back/neck, raised for display or intimidation.
- **Actions enabled:** terrorize, ward
- **Example creatures:** frilled lizard, iguana, basilisk lizard, ceratopsian-type reptile (bony frill)
- **Notes:** Primarily a display part; only marginal action verbs apply (no direct strike). Overlaps with `sail` in some crested lizards — keep separate: crest is head/neck-localized, sail is dorsal/spinal.

### `sail`
- **What it is:** A tall membrane of skin stretched over elongated spinal projections along the back, used for display and thermoregulation-reading intimidation.
- **Actions enabled:** terrorize, ward
- **Example creatures:** spinosaur-type reptile, sailfin lizard, sail-backed amphibian
- **Notes:** Almost purely a display/terrorize part in an action taxonomy; very little direct combat application. Distinct from `crest` (localized) and `spines` (individual sharp points) by being one continuous membrane.

### `mane`
- **What it is:** A thick ruff of long hair or fur around the head/neck, used for intimidation display and providing incidental protection to the neck.
- **Actions enabled:** terrorize, ward
- **Example creatures:** lion, hyena (mane bristle), maned wolf, baboon
- **Notes:** `ward` applies narrowly (a mane can cushion neck bites); primary role is `terrorize` via bristling/display. Low action-density part, include for completeness per exhaustive-first instruction.

### `dewlap` / `throat-fan` — considered
- **What it is:** Loose, often colorful skin flap under the throat, extended for display.
- **Actions enabled:** terrorize
- **Example creatures:** anole lizard, bullfrog (vocal sac variant), iguana
- **Notes:** Marginal — single-action, pure display part. Flagging as a candidate but likely a cut during curation (very thin action support, mostly a "sense/display" organ the brief warns against over-including). Included for exhaustiveness.

### `venom-delivery` (spurs/glands, cross-cutting)
- **What it is:** A dedicated venom-injecting structure separate from fangs — e.g., a hollow spur or glandular barb.
- **Actions enabled:** strike, drain, ambush
- **Example creatures:** platypus (hind-leg spur), Gila monster (grooved teeth + venom, overlaps fangs), some salamanders (skin-toxin secretion, passive)
- **Notes:** Where venom is delivered via teeth, fold into `fangs`; this key is for the cases where the delivery structure is NOT the mouth (leg spur) or is passive (toxic skin secretion — see `toxic-skin` below). Flag for curator: may not need a separate key if `fangs` note covers "venom-capable variant" and skin-toxin is rare enough to fold into a general `venom-delivery` catch-all.

### `toxic-skin`
- **What it is:** Skin or glandular secretions that poison on contact or when the creature is bitten/eaten.
- **Actions enabled:** ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** poison dart frog, toad (parotoid glands), fire salamander
- **Notes:** Passive defensive part, not an "acts with" instrument in the strike sense — mostly `ward`/`terrorize`. Borderline against the brief's "external functional parts relevant to action" test; included because it is genuinely defense-active (predator that bites is poisoned) rather than purely passive coloration. Flag for curator judgment — could be ruled a "trait" rather than an "anatomy" part.

## Summary

Harvested 24 candidates across four sub-groups (head/jaw, limb/claw, tail, armor/display) for terrestrial vertebrates (mammals, reptiles, amphibians): `fangs`, `jaws`, `tusks`, `horns`, `antlers`, `beak`, `trunk`/`proboscis`, `tongue-lash`, `claws`, `paws`, `hooves`, `forelimbs-as-bludgeon`, `tail-club`, `whip-tail`, `prehensile-tail`, `constricting-coils`, `shell`, `plates`, `spines`/`quills`, `crest`, `sail`, `mane`, `dewlap`/`throat-fan`, `venom-delivery`, `toxic-skin` (25 counting the spines/quills pairing as one, or 26 if split). Several pairs are flagged as likely merge candidates for central curation (`paws` into `claws`, `spines` and `quills`, `shell` and `plates` as siblings not synonyms), and a few marginal display-only parts (`dewlap`, `mane`, `sail`, `toxic-skin`) are flagged as possible cuts since their action-verb support is thin, but were kept per the brief's "prefer over-inclusive" instruction. Amphibian/reptile specialties explicitly requested — tongue-lash, constricting-coils, venom-delivery — are all covered.
