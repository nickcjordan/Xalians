## 6. Honest richness assessment

The core fear/dread vocabulary (nouns and verbs: dread, terror, horror, cow, daunt, menace, unnerve, quail, cower, wither) is extremely rich in English — this is one of the deepest semantic fields in the language, with strong coverage across formal, poetic, and archaic registers. Element-neutral naming is correspondingly easy: glare/roar/howl/presence/aura combine cleanly with dread-adjectives without pulling toward any one element.

The intimidation-display sub-field (animal threat behavior: hackles, bristling, crests, ruffs, baring fangs) is moderately rich but anatomically narrow — English has good verbs (bristle, flare, puff, bare) but a small closed set of anatomy nouns (hackles, ruff, crest, quills, mane), all reusable across many species since they name generic body parts rather than specific creatures.

The omen/foreboding sub-field (portent, presage, augur, forebode) is rich but skews literary/archaic; it pairs well with ghost and dark elements naturally, which is why so much of it drifted to section 5 rather than staying neutral — "doom," "omen," and "portent" inherently carry a dark/fate connotation that a fire or water creature's ability name would read oddly using. This confirms the brief's warning: the dread field leans structurally toward ghost and dark, and true neutral candidates cluster around physical/sensory dread (glare, roar, chill, presence, aura, stare) rather than fate-language (doom, omen, portent), which I kept element-leaning even when not obviously "spectral."

Weakest sub-area: fire- and water-flavored terror names are thin, since dread vocabulary doesn't naturally carry heat or wetness connotations — I could only honestly produce a few (searing glare, blazing wrath) by adjective-pairing rather than finding dread words that are inherently fire/water-flavored. This is expected and consistent with the brief's note that this field leans ghost/dark; other elements should likely draw TERRORIZE names sparingly or via cross-field adjective pairing rather than expecting rich native vocabulary here.
