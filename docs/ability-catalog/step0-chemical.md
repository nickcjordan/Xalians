# Step 0 — Productive-Word List — CHEMICAL

Source: harvest-chemical.md sections 1-3 (Adjectives, Nouns, Verbs/Gerunds). Per Composition Brief v5 Section 4 Step 0: every noun/adjective/verb concept classified PRODUCTIVE or NON-PRODUCTIVE. Only grammatical/formal reasons justify non-productive status — a multi-word phrase that cannot compose to 2 words, a bare combining form, or a word that cannot grammatically front/follow a partner word. Register, tone, theme, and abstractness are never exclusion reasons. Same-root duplicates across sections are collapsed to one row with a note.

Element canon: CHEMICAL = toxins, acids, corrosion, reactions, living chemical weapons (homeworld Drainov: the Carbide-1 meltdown, a toxic wasteland). Boundary flags applied per assignment: miasma-class disease-vapor words are flagged dual-candidate with GHOST; liquid-medium words are flagged to WATER (the phase is water's vocabulary, the chemistry is chemical's); plant-toxin words (venom, toxin) are flagged chemical-primary at the plant border, with sap flagged plant-primary. Real chemical-weapon proper names are flagged prominently for cutting at composition per the real-weapons rule — they remain PRODUCTIVE here since Step 0 is a grammatical pass only; the weapon-name rule is a Section 6 hard rule applied at the naming step, not a Step 0 exclusion.

This is the shared verbatim row set for the four downstream composers (strike/lash/crush/rake/shove/drain/ambush/beam/hurl/spray/burst/cloud/snare/ward/mend/terrorize sweeps are performed separately by each composer against this list).

## Notes on real chemical-weapon proper names

The harvest file's Section 5 ("Excluded as borrowed") already lists several Pokemon-borrowed collocations (Poison Sting, Toxic, Sludge Bomb, etc.) — those are composed-name-level borrowed-name flags, not Step 0 lexicon entries, and are out of scope for this productive-word list. No real-world chemical-weapon proper names (Sarin, VX, Napalm-class) appear as headwords in harvest-chemical.md §1-3 sections; none were introduced as productive-word rows here. Downstream composers must still apply the real-weapons hard rule (Section 6.4) at the naming step against any candidate name that lands on a real chemical-weapon designation by coincidence (e.g., a composed name that happens to match a known agent name) and flag it prominently for cutting.
