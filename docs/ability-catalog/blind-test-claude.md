Instead its capability profile clusters around swim (52), burrow (31), climb (18), and especially manipulation (44), the highest non-swim physical score. Combined with an "instrument": "pincers" appearing in three of its four abilities, the read is a heavy-bodied ambush predator with grasping claws as its primary tool, comfortable in water and able to dig in or grip terrain, but slow and non-agile on land (agility: 14 is the second-lowest attribute in the whole record, beaten only by charisma).

Three of its four named abilities use "medium": "dark" and one uses "ghost", and the two highest-intensity abilities (Point of No Return at 74, and the signature one) are both dark. The name "graviclaw" and the ability descriptions ("Collapses gravitational waves into a point inside its open claw," "Event Horizon," "Wraith Vise") paint dark, in this system, not as shadow/stealth but as something closer to gravity/void manipulation — a black-hole conceit. "Point of No Return" (signature, intensity 74) snares via gravitational collapse; "Event Horizon" (intensity 41) is a "ward" — plausibly a defensive gravity field around itself; "Wraith Vise" (intensity 48, ghost-medium, action "crush") suggests once something is pulled in, a spectral/intangible-force finishing blow; "Null Grasp" (intensity 33, instrument "mind" rather than pincers) is the odd one out — a psychic-flavored dark snare that doesn't use the claws at all, hinting the creature's gravitational power isn't purely mechanical but partly a mental/psionic projection.

Intelligence is mid (42) and charisma is the lowest attribute in the sheet (19), suggesting a solitary, non-social, not-especially-clever animal running on instinct and raw physical/elemental force rather than cunning or pack coordination. Temperament confirms solitary and aggressive-but-not-manic: boldness 81, aggression 66, but sociability only 22 and energy only 25 — bold and willing to fight but low-energy (ambush-appropriate: conserve energy, wait, strike) and antisocial.

The archetype key "stalwart" favoring "resilience" and "willpower" is a direct label match to its two highest attributes, so the system appears to derive or at least validate a role/build classification from the raw numbers — this is a tank/bruiser archetype, not a skirmisher or glass cannon.

"Armored" matches the chitin covering; "anchored" most plausibly refers to its low mobility being a fixed trait of the species — it holds ground rather than moves — which coheres with the burrow/anchor/grip profile (possibly it roots or braces itself, resisting being moved, which would also make sense of "Event Horizon" as a defensive stance ability). "Stealthy" as a rolled (i.e., not guaranteed, individual-variation) trait sits awkwardly against a 372kg armored creature with the lowest agility score in the sheet — see Internal Tensions below.

Appearance is reduced to a single field, "finish": "gleam" — presumably describing how its chitin/exoskeleton catches light (a glossy, polished carapace rather than matte, dull, or rough), the only aesthetic detail in the entire record.

## 4. Internal tensions

- **"Stealthy" rolled trait vs. the rest of the physical profile.** A 372kg, 198cm, chitin-armored creature with a "gleam" finish (implying a light-catching, presumably conspicuous surface) and the lowest agility score in the record (14) is a poor fit for stealth in any conventional reading. Either "stealthy" means something narrower here (e.g., bypasses detection via tremorsense-adjacent ambush rather than true concealment, or it's about scent/sound suppression rather than visual concealment), or this is a case where the trait-roll system is decoupled from the physiology fields and can produce combinations that don't cohere narratively.
- **"Anchored" trait vs. "swim: 52" and "burrow: 31".** If "anchored" means low mobility / rootedness (the most natural reading given agility 14, sprint 12, leap 4), it sits oddly next to genuinely competent swim and burrow scores — those are still *movement* capabilities, just not overland-chase movement. The record doesn't resolve whether "anchored" describes a literal combat mechanic (cannot be forcibly moved / knocked back) versus a general mobility character (slow, sedentary) — these are compatible but distinct claims, and the record supports both simultaneously without picking one.
- **High willpower/instinct (78/71) vs. low intelligence (42) and the "mind"-instrument ability.** "Null Grasp" uses "instrument: mind," implying a deliberate mental/psychic act, yet the attribute most associated with cognition (intelligence) is middling while willpower and instinct — arguably non-cognitive, "gut"/drive attributes — are high. This is plausible (a creature that acts on raw instinctual force rather than calculated intelligence) but it is a tension the record doesn't explicitly resolve: is "Null Grasp" a conscious tactical choice or an involuntary instinctual reflex?
- **Low energy (25) and low sprint/leap vs. high aggression (66) and high boldness (81).** An animal that is bold and aggressive but has low energy and near-zero mobility stats implies a very specific behavioral niche (ambush predator that commits fully once it engages, rather than a roaming hunter) — internally consistent under that one reading, but the record never states it; a different reader could just as easily see "aggressive but low-energy" as contradictory (why would a low-energy animal pick fights readily?) without the ambush-predator inference bridging it.
- **`element.affinities.dark: 100` (maximal) coexisting with a "ghost"-medium ability (Wraith Vise) that has no corresponding "ghost" entry beyond affinity 44.** If affinities gate which abilities an individual can roll, a ghost affinity of only 44 (well under the dark 100) sitting alongside a full ghost-medium ability with respectable intensity (48) suggests either affinity doesn't gate ability medium as tightly as it might appear, or the relationship between affinity score and ability access is nonlinear/threshold-based rather than proportional — the record has no second data point to tell which.
- **`respiration: amphibious` and `climateTolerance` topping out at 20C** — amphibious creatures (able to function in water and air) are often associated with warmer/wetter climates in general biological intuition, yet this creature's tolerance band (-15C to 20C) skews cold. Not strictly contradictory, but it's a combination the record asserts without any supporting detail (e.g., no mention of geothermal habitat, ice-water burrows, or similar) to make the cold-amphibious pairing feel motivated rather than arbitrary.
