1. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The closed media enum is founded on a category error: `gas` and `liquid` are phases, but `vacuum` is an absence of matter, and the three are not exhaustive even for game-relevant physics (plasma, supercritical fluid, granular or porous surroundings, and multiphase media do not fit). Calling the enum exhaustive makes later extension a breaking correction rather than an ordinary vocabulary addition. **Concrete suggestion:** Define a versioned environmental-medium registry with normative categories and an explicit extension policy; either model vacuum separately as `requiresAmbientMatter: false` or state exactly how exotic and mixed media map to the available keys.

2. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The drowning rule contradicts the explicit meaning of `breathes: []`. A creature that does not breathe has every ambient medium absent from `breathes`, so the stated derivation makes a breathless creature drown whenever submerged in a tolerated liquid. **Concrete suggestion:** Define drowning only for creatures with a respiratory requirement, e.g. `breathes.length > 0 && currentMedium == liquid && liquid not in breathes`; separately define respiratory failure in gases and vacuum, and publish truth-table fixtures for all combinations.

3. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The relationship between `breathes` and `environmentalTolerance.ambientMedia` is unspecified. A record can say that a creature breathes liquid but cannot safely be surrounded by liquid, or tolerates gas while being unable to breathe in it. The first may be invalid or may mean “can exchange but is poisoned”; the second may mean breath-holding, asphyxiation, or no respiratory need. Third-party games cannot distinguish these cases. **Concrete suggestion:** State whether `breathes` must be a subset of `ambientMedia`, define any allowed exception semantically, and provide a complete decision table for safe exposure, respiratory failure, drowning, and breathless immunity.

4. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Elemental type is not an adequate chemistry schema. “Chemical-element and unharmed” collapses composition, concentration, dose, pH, solvent behavior, and mixtures into a combat-style elemental identity; a Chemical native being immune to every chemical swamp does not follow from either affinity or phase. The universal 14x14 effectiveness matrix also introduces a shared survival mechanic despite the rule that games derive mechanics from creature facts. **Concrete suggestion:** Store environmental exposure tags and creature tolerances as nature data with explicit semantics, or sharply limit the element matrix to a documented abstract exposure class; do not infer universal immunity merely from matching primary element.

5. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** `ambientMedia` claims to answer what can safely surround the body while omitting pressure, mechanical support, humidity, and composition. Phase alone cannot establish safe normal activity: a deep-sea body may tolerate liquid only within a pressure band, and a large aquatic body may be unable to function in gas even if the gas itself is harmless. Deferring these dimensions silently turns “not modeled” into “safe at every value.” **Concrete suggestion:** Make the claim explicitly partial (`toleratedMediaAtReferenceConditions`) and define those reference conditions now, or add pressure/support constraints before any record relies on the block; represent unmodeled dimensions as unknown, not unlimited tolerance.

6. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** One Celsius range is applied across all media even though safe temperature depends strongly on medium and pressure, and “ambient temperature” in vacuum is not a single thermodynamic quantity. The schema also omits endpoint inclusivity, precision, allowed extrema, and `min <= max` validation. **Concrete suggestion:** Define a reference measurement protocol and numeric constraints, and key ranges by medium (with a radiant-equilibrium convention for vacuum) before claiming cross-game meaning.

7. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The explicit-none principle is not a field-level contract. It names five universal dimensions, but other apparently universal fields—`corporeality`, `composition.primary`, `anatomy`, `bodyPlan`, dimensions, environmental tolerance, capabilities, base senses, guaranteed traits, and the traits object itself—have no required/optional ruling. Implementers still cannot tell “known none,” “not applicable,” “not rolled,” and “schema did not contain this field” apart. **Concrete suggestion:** Publish a schema table for every field giving requiredness, null prohibition, empty representation, applicability conditions, defaults (preferably none), and behavior when reading older schema versions; enforce it with JSON Schema and fixtures.

8. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The revised element contract remains internally inconsistent. The original says `affinities` deliberately contains the primary at 100, while the new explicit-none rule says absent affinities are omitted. If the primary remains duplicated, `affinities` is never absent; if “absent” means no secondary, the field name and cardinality do not communicate that. **Concrete suggestion:** Keep `primary` separate and rename the optional map to `secondaryAffinities` with cardinality exactly zero or one, or require an all-affinities map in every record; do not mix the two representations.

9. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Secondary-affinity generation is still underspecified after anomalies were removed. The brief does not define graph directionality, edge weights, empty-neighbor behavior, the 1–99 strength distribution, whether species can override the 75/25 odds, or whether graph and odds changes are independently versioned. **Concrete suggestion:** Specify the exact candidate construction and weighted draw, strength distribution, fallback for zero candidates, template override rules, and conformance vectors for representative graphs.

10. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Several “none” sentinels are semantically forced rather than honest. `covering: "bare"`, `lifespan: "ageless"`, and a required composition/body plan may not describe a non-corporeal creature; an empty `ambientMedia` plus a numeric temperature range is also incoherent, while omitting the block loses the distinction between not applicable and unknown. **Concrete suggestion:** Define applicability by `corporeality` and add explicit registry values such as `none`/`not-applicable` where they are real facts; do not reuse `bare` or `ageless` as generic N/A markers.

11. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Anatomy validation crosses species and specimen scopes. Species declare instruments, but `physiology.anatomy` is an individual-record field. If anatomy can vary, a species-level declaration can be valid for the template while an individual lacks the required part; if anatomy cannot vary, duplicating it in every specimen needs an explicit invariant. **Concrete suggestion:** Declare anatomy as fixed or rolled. For fixed anatomy, validate the species template and require exact record inheritance; for rolled anatomy, make ability eligibility conditional on the realized specimen anatomy and validate every issued record.

12. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The physical-instrument rule cannot be implemented while the design still has an open decision between one vocabulary and two linked vocabularies. There is also no normative `physical` classification or mapping for group terms and synonyms, so a key can evade validation merely by being unclassified or mapped loosely. **Concrete suggestion:** Ratify registries before schema freeze: each instrument key must declare `kind`, allowed actions, and exact required anatomy predicates; reject unknown or unmapped keys closed rather than treating them as non-physical.

13. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** “Non-physical instruments validate against senses/traits instead” has no predicate, and the relevant trait may be rolled after species instruments are declared. A species can declare `mind`, generate an individual without `telekinetic` or an appropriate sense, and still need an ability from that instrument. Retry behavior could then bias trait rarity or consume RNG unpredictably. **Concrete suggestion:** Put a machine-readable eligibility predicate on every non-physical instrument, distinguish template potential from specimen eligibility, and define deterministic fallback behavior when no declared instrument is eligible.

14. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** A flat anatomy key list establishes neither count nor configuration. It cannot prove that a plural or paired instrument is present in sufficient quantity, distinguish a pincer from “pincers,” or express that a part exists but cannot perform a particular action. Presence alone therefore authorizes abilities the body may not support. **Concrete suggestion:** Give anatomy entries stable part IDs plus count/configuration or expose capability predicates such as `can-grip`, `can-lash`, and `can-project`; make instruments validate against predicates rather than spelling coincidence.

15. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Outcome-based `manipulation` can contradict its alleged means. It is rolled before the `telekinetic` trait, yet telekinesis is later tilted by manipulation. An individual with no manipulating anatomy can roll high manipulation and then fail to roll telekinetic, leaving no means for the stored outcome; conversely the later trait can create a means whose contribution was never included in the earlier score. **Concrete suggestion:** Either generate means before the outcome and derive/roll manipulation conditionally, or constrain manipulation bands by guaranteed means and add whole-record invariants covering physical-only, telekinetic-only, both, and neither.

16. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** “Breathing mechanism is never record data” conflicts with calling `anatomy` a comprehensive list of functional parts. If respiratory organs are excluded, the field is really ability-relevant external anatomy; if included, the environmental contract leaks breathing mechanism after explicitly forbidding it. **Concrete suggestion:** Define the anatomy field’s scope narrowly and rename it if needed, or permit functional organ data consistently; add inclusion/exclusion rules to the parts registry.

17. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Weighted trait draws, downward tilts, and species-authored floors do not have a coherent probability contract. In a fixed-count weighted sample, increasing one trait necessarily lowers other traits even if their raw weights are unchanged; without replacement, exclusion and deduplication alter every marginal again. “Never below floors” cannot be guaranteed without saying whether floors apply to weights, per-draw chances, or final inclusion probabilities. **Concrete suggestion:** Specify the sampling model mathematically, define floors on final marginal probability, and use a constrained normalization algorithm with tests proving all floors and exclusions hold.

18. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The three tilt tiers are described by arrows rather than functions. Mass and height are not comparable across species, “size” has no source field, sense averaging does not say how zeros or special senses participate, and raw 0–100 attributes produce different effects when species bands have different widths. Simulation tuning cannot make an unspecified transform interoperable. **Concrete suggestion:** Define every driver, normalization domain (prefer within-species percentiles where intended), curve, cap, rounding rule, interaction order, and fixed-point arithmetic; publish conditional distribution acceptance ranges per species and version.

19. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The accepted rich-get-richer effect creates an exploitable rarity gradient, not merely visual coherence. High attributes increase access to desirable positive-only traits, so already strong specimens receive extra trait count/value; if seeds can be chosen or previewed, grinding targets these correlated tails. No global rarity metric or issuance policy accounts for the conditional probability. **Concrete suggestion:** Measure joint outcome rarity and power, set per-species tail limits, disclose whether trait draws change count or only identity, and use committed/server-issued seeds or an explicit anti-grinding policy where scarcity matters.

20. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** The phasing tilt is ambiguous for Ghost-primary creatures because the old representation puts primary affinity 100 in `affinities`, while the new text could mean only a rolled secondary. “Rolled-on-corporeal path only” also does not say how non-corporeal phasing is represented or whether corporeal Ghost-primary specimens are nearly guaranteed the trait. **Concrete suggestion:** Define the exact affinity input (primary included or secondary only), the corporeality eligibility predicate, and whether non-corporeal phasing is guaranteed, inapplicable, or a different fact.

21. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Guaranteed and rolled trait interaction remains undefined. `armored` may be guaranteed by covering and also weighted by mass “where rolled”; no rule says whether it is removed before normalization, redrawn, duplicated, or allowed to consume a rolled slot. The same issue applies to exclusions and prerequisites. **Concrete suggestion:** Give traits set semantics, remove guaranteed and ineligible keys before drawing, define whether removal changes count or weights, and validate prerequisites, exclusions, maximum count, and retry limits without unbounded rejection.

22. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Requiring chirality to ship in `generatorVersion 1.0.0` can violate the immutable snapshot promise. If any 1.0.0 generator, fixture, or record already exists without the field, adding a mandatory RNG-consuming draw changes both its schema and all downstream seeded results under the same version. `schemaVersion` does not repair reused generator semantics. **Concrete suggestion:** Freeze 1.0.0 before issuance; if it has ever emitted normative output, introduce the field only in a new generator version and use a domain-separated chirality RNG stream so it cannot perturb other rolls.

23. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Chirality is called a dimorphism marker while being deliberately undisplayed and denied any phenotype, ordering, or gender mapping. That leaves third parties with a binary label but no normative legitimate meaning, making it an easy de facto sex/gender or rank proxy despite platform policy. “The platform” also does not bind independent games. **Concrete suggestion:** State the permitted biological/derivation semantics in the registry and conformance terms, make non-discrimination a third-party contract if required, or remove the field until it has a nature-level use that is not merely a hidden binary partition.

24. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** The chirality distribution contract omits whether 50/50 is an independent Bernoulli probability or an exact issuance balance, and whether any non-`achiral` species may fix or weight a chirality. It also lacks validation tying `achiral` exclusively to a species declaration. **Concrete suggestion:** Define the template schema as either `{mode: achiral}` or `{mode: rolled, weights: ...}`, forbid illegal specimen values, and publish deterministic roll vectors and population-level statistical tests.

25. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The lifespan labels have no operational anchors. “Short” or “enduring” could mean relative to mass, species, Xalians globally, real time, battles, or generator cycles; without years or a reference exposure/use profile, separate games cannot derive even ordinally comparable wear. Calling wear-out “lifespan” also invites age mechanics that canon rejects. **Concrete suggestion:** Define each class against a canonical wear budget or dimensionless reference duty cycle with ordered boundaries, rename the field to `wearLongevity` if appropriate, and explicitly forbid age-stage inference.

26. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** The authoring rubric is neither reproducible nor stable. Mass, metabolic intensity, composition, and home-world harshness have no scoring function, and home-world harshness makes the result appear environmental even though the stored enum is intrinsic and follows the specimen off-world. **Concrete suggestion:** Publish a scored rubric with tie-breakers and review tests, and state whether the value means intrinsic durability under reference conditions or expected survival on the home world; store separate facts if both are desired.

27. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Seed plus `generatorVersion` still does not ensure reproducibility. There is no canonical PRNG, byte order, table ordering, weighted-choice algorithm, floating/fixed-point rule, retry consumption order, or domain separation; the new chirality and conditional anatomy/trait logic make downstream RNG drift even more likely. **Concrete suggestion:** Publish a bit-exact algorithm with per-field domain-separated streams and golden complete-record fixtures across boundary and fallback cases.

28. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Provenance remains self-asserted. A user can fabricate a favorable record, backdate `generatedAt`, choose a desirable origin/serial, or grind seeds and present the output as legitimately issued. Immutability does not establish authenticity. **Concrete suggestion:** Canonically serialize and hash records, sign them with a generator identity, define serial namespaces and seed-issuance rules, and expose key rotation/revocation verification.

29. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** `generatorVersion` still promises an entire content snapshot without a content digest, immutable manifest, archival locator, or compatibility contract between generator and schema versions. Years later, identical version strings can resolve to missing or replaced tables. **Concrete suggestion:** Make each version a content-addressed manifest hashing the algorithm and every registry/table, guarantee permanent retrieval, and specify which schema versions can encode its outputs.

30. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Ordinary ability generation remains incomplete. Count, whether the authored signature is included in that count, instrument/action/medium/intensity distributions, duplicate rules, zero-candidate fallback, and retry limits are all unspecified; conditional anatomy and trait eligibility adds more failure paths. **Concrete suggestion:** Specify the full probability tree and a terminating, deterministic fallback order, then provide fixtures for empty catalog cells, no eligible instruments, and duplicate draws.

31. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The name-catalog schema still cannot encode “heavier names go to heavier intensity.” `minIntensity` is only a threshold, not a weight curve, and changing intensity from 1–10 to 0–100 makes any implicit ten-tier assumption obsolete. **Concrete suggestion:** Store an explicit heft score plus a normative weighting function, or a versioned piecewise weight curve, and test name frequencies at representative intensities including 0 and 100.

32. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Allowing ability intensity 0 is semantically unresolved. A zero-intensity `strike`, `mend`, or signature can still receive a dramatic catalog name and may count toward coverage even though it performs no graded act; omitting it would conflict with the rule that graded zero is explicit. **Concrete suggestion:** Decide whether 0 means dormant/present, minimum possible active effect, or absence. If active abilities must be nonzero, constrain their intensity to 1–100 while retaining 0 for universal capability fields where “none” is meaningful.

33. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Signature abilities and catalog entries still lack stable machine identities, while bespoke behavior is keyed “to the signature.” Display names cannot safely serve as keys, and collision checking only current content permits future catalogs to collide with historical signatures embedded in pinned snapshots. **Concrete suggestion:** Add immutable `abilityId`/`signatureId`, keep display names non-authoritative, and maintain an append-only normalized namespace with tombstones across every released version.

34. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Coverage is no longer a simple reachable `(instrument × action × medium)` product. Physical instruments may depend on rolled anatomy, non-physical instruments on rolled traits/senses, secondary media occur only 25% of the time, and intensity filters can empty a nominally populated cell. A checker that uses only species declarations can both overstate and understate usable coverage. **Concrete suggestion:** Compute coverage over realizable specimen states and intensity ranges, require a valid deterministic name for every generated state, and report probability-weighted thinness in addition to a raw floor.

35. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The archetype invariant remains mathematically impossible: 16 archetypes with two favors create 32 assignments, while ten attributes favored by exactly three archetypes create 30. The new explicit `favors: []` example for `balanced` suggests a fix but directly contradicts the original statement that every archetype has two. **Concrete suggestion:** Ratify `balanced` as the explicit zero-favor exception (leaving 30 assignments) or change the counts, then enforce both per-archetype cardinality and global totals in validation.

36. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Favored-only archetype tilts remain unequal boosts. Their expected value depends on species band width and headroom, and they also feed trait tilts through the boosted attributes, so archetype choice now changes both stats and positive-trait probabilities. “Bounded” does not establish comparable power or tail behavior. **Concrete suggestion:** Apply boosts in percentile space with a fixed expected budget and test joint stat-plus-trait distributions by species/archetype; document any intended power differences rather than treating the archetypes as equivalent labels.

37. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Core distributions are still prose, not contract: archetype tilt, attribute bands, temperament weighting, affinity strength, trait draw count, trait tilt, finish draws, and name selection omit exact formulas, clipping, rounding, and correlation behavior. Simulation batches cannot validate implementations without normative targets. **Concrete suggestion:** Specify fixed-point formulas and random draw algorithms, publish per-species Monte Carlo acceptance bands and conditional tail requirements, and make statistical conformance part of generator release gating.

38. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** The nature-versus-rules boundary is weakened further by the environmental matrix. Actions such as `mend`, `terrorize`, and `ward`, passive/reactive trait classes, intensity, and now “unharmed” typed exposure already assert a universal gameplay ontology. Third-party games cannot tell which assertions are facts they must honor and which are merely suggested derivations. **Concrete suggestion:** Classify every field as observation, authored semantic capability, or game derivation input, give each class normative limits, and keep damage, immunity, timing, targets, and other outcomes out of the canonical record layer.

39. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** One-line registry descriptions remain insufficient for interoperability, especially now that validation depends on them. Terms such as `vibration` versus `vocal`, `display`, `telepathic`, `bare`, lifespan classes, physical versus non-physical instruments, and capability axes need normative distinctions, not display glosses. **Concrete suggestion:** Give every key machine-readable relationships, applicability and exclusion rules, units/anchors, and conformance examples; specify arrays as sets or sequences and define duplicate/order canonicalization.

40. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Renaming trait keys risks breaking immutable historical records and third-party code if role-nouns were ever emitted or used in fixtures. Version pinning preserves the old vocabulary but does not tell a game whether `healer` and `healing` are the same semantic trait across versions, and reusing retired keys later would be worse. **Concrete suggestion:** Separate stable trait IDs from display labels, publish explicit aliases/migrations for old IDs, and permanently reserve retired keys across registries and generator versions.

41. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Immutable records still lack a validity and errata channel. Broken templates, impossible combinations, exploitable seed handling, compromised signing keys, or later-discovered semantic errors cannot be repaired by per-game balance patches, and silently changing a pinned registry is forbidden. **Concrete suggestion:** Add an external signed status/errata registry keyed by record hash and content manifest, supporting invalidation, superseding interpretation notes, and signing-key revocation without mutating the record.

42. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** The catalog production pipeline remains difficult to verify. “Exhaustive” harvesting has no required source provenance, borrowed-name detection depends on agent recognition, and a single orchestrator is a consistency bottleneck across 30 semantic fields and later planet-specific evaluations. **Concrete suggestion:** Store source/corpus provenance and per-tag justifications, maintain a competitor-name corpus with automated normalized/fuzzy checks, use blind second review on thin or disputed cells, and make the final machine-generated coverage report a release artifact.

43. **Severity: low. Specific hole:** Mechanical constraints are still described as review rules rather than mandatory gates. At this scale, word count, American spelling, vocabulary membership, action compatibility, anatomy predicates, duplicate normalization, affinity cardinality, min/max ranges, explicit-none rules, and forbidden concepts will drift. **Concrete suggestion:** Run a schema validator and catalog linter on every intermediate and release artifact, reject unresolved warnings, and preserve the exact validator version and report in the generator manifest.
