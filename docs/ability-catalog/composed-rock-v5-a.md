STRIKE landed as the widest cell by a large margin, consistent with the assignment's canon note that it is rock's signature action — the falling-stone/seismic-blow register (boulder, granite, bedrock, slab, monolith, seismic, tectonic) crossed productively against nearly the entire impact-vocabulary synonym pool, and the cell would have been wider still if not for a deliberate, per-name-justified exclusion of state-adjectives (petrified, fossilized, ossified, ancient, weathered, riven, cleaved) as bare strike-manner modifiers — those words survive instead as anatomy-tagged fist/claw/ram compounds, which is definition-honest but narrower. LASH came in exactly as thin as the canon predicted: stone genuinely does not whip, and every survivor required either a tail-anatomy tag or a phenomenon-level reading of a rockslide's leading edge as an "arc" — this is the correct outcome for the element, not an under-effort one; the disposition table and matrix both show the LASH cross was attempted in full against the same word list as the other three actions and failed honestly, not that it was skipped. RAKE landed as a solid mid-size cell built almost entirely on edged/bladed stone types (flint, shale, slate, quartz, obsidian, chert) crossed against the cutting/scraping/abrasion vocabulary, with rock's genuine ownership of the abrasion sub-register (grate, rasp, scour, furrow) giving it several names no other element could claim as cleanly. SHOVE landed as the second-widest cell, built on rock's mass/weight adjectives (colossal, titanic, hulking, massive, ponderous) and the seismic-displacement register (upheaval, subduction, tectonic, fault) crossed against the ram/charge/push vocabulary — with two genuine Step A standalones (Upheaval, Subduction) that needed no partner word at all, unique among the four cells in this run. Across all four cells, the most common rejection code was D (definition dishonest), driven overwhelmingly by two recurring failure patterns: (1) architectural/defensive nouns (rampart, bastion, fortress, keystone, vault, wall) that describe structures built to withstand force, not deliver it, cut consistently across STRIKE and SHOVE wherever tried; and (2) immobile/static landform and state words (bedrock, crag, cairn, scree as a bare unit, ancient, riven, cleaved) that cannot themselves act as the moving mass or instrument a STRIKE/LASH/SHOVE name requires, though many of the same words pass cleanly in RAKE (where the required property is an edge or texture, not motion or mass) or as anatomy-tagged compounds (fist, claw, ram) where the state-adjective describes the instrument's material rather than the blow's manner.
