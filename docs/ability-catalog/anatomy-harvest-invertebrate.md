# Anatomy Harvest — Invertebrate Domain (arthropods, mollusks, cnidarians, annelids, echinoderms)

Per ANATOMY-HARVEST-BRIEF.md. Exhaustive sweep, over-inclusive by design; consolidation happens centrally.

---

## Arthropods

### 1. `pincers`
- **What it is:** Paired grasping claws terminating in opposable points, as on a crab or scorpion (chelae).
- **Actions:** crush, shove, snare, ward
- **Example creatures:** crab, lobster, scorpion, giant fictional crustacean-monster
- **Notes:** Near-synonym of `claws` but specifically the two-part opposing-jaw grip (chela), not a single hooked digit. Overlaps `chelicerae` (below) which are smaller mouth-pincers rather than limb pincers.

### 2. `chelicerae`
- **What it is:** Small fanged or pincer-like mouthparts in front of the mouth, as in spiders and scorpions, often paired with venom delivery.
- **Actions:** strike, drain, ambush
- **Example creatures:** spider, scorpion, tick
- **Notes:** Distinct from `pincers` (walking-leg claws) and from `fangs` (vertebrate domain) — arachnid-specific mouth structure; frequently venom-linked, overlaps `venom-fangs` concept if that key exists elsewhere.

### 3. `mandibles`
- **What it is:** Paired horizontal jaw-parts that bite, shear, or grip, as in ants, beetles, and crustaceans.
- **Actions:** strike, crush, rake, shove
- **Example creatures:** ant, stag beetle, dragonfly nymph, giant fictional insectoid
- **Notes:** Insect/crustacean equivalent of jaws; some (stag beetle) are oversized display-and-combat structures overlapping with `tusks` in function.

### 4. `stinger`
- **What it is:** A sharp, often barbed, venom-injecting tail or abdominal spike.
- **Actions:** strike, drain, ambush, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** scorpion, wasp, bee, stingray-adjacent (though stingray is vertebrate)
- **Notes:** Core entry; likely already in central list given prominence. Overlaps `barbed-tail` / `venom-spur` variants.

### 5. `spinnerets`
- **What it is:** Silk-producing organs, usually at the rear of the abdomen, that extrude and direct threads for webs, wraps, or tethers.
- **Actions:** snare, ward, mend
- **Example creatures:** spider, silkworm moth larva, some fictional giant spiders
- **Notes:** "Mend" applies loosely (web repair/construction); primarily a snare/trap tool. Could pair with a `silk-glands` variant if internal-vs-external distinction matters.

### 6. `antennae`
- **What it is:** Paired, jointed, whip-like sensory feelers on the head that also probe, lash, or manipulate at close range.
- **Actions:** strike, lash, snare
- **Example creatures:** ant, moth, lobster, crayfish
- **Notes:** Dual-role (sense + act) per brief's exception clause. Long crustacean antennae can physically lash or sweep; short insect antennae are more purely sensory — note the size/role split when curating.

### 7. `carapace`
- **What it is:** A single-piece hardened shield covering the back/thorax, as in crabs, lobsters, and beetles.
- **Actions:** ward
- **Example creatures:** crab, lobster, horseshoe crab, tortoise beetle
- **Notes:** Overlaps `exoskeleton-plating` (below) as the general case; carapace is the specific dorsal-shield term. Also overlaps `shell` (mollusk domain) conceptually — different structural origin but same defensive function.

### 8. `exoskeleton-plating`
- **What it is:** Segmented, hardened outer-body armor covering limbs and trunk, as in beetles and armored crustaceans.
- **Actions:** ward, shove
- **Example creatures:** rhinoceros beetle, armadillo-adjacent isopod (roly-poly), armored crustacean
- **Notes:** Broader than `carapace` (whole-body vs. dorsal-only); "shove" applies when plating is used as a battering surface (rhino beetle horn-adjacent body-charges). Consider merging with `carapace` centrally or keeping both as scale-variants.

### 9. `horn` (beetle horn)
- **What it is:** A rigid, often forked, chitinous projection from the head or thorax used in shoving contests.
- **Actions:** shove, crush, strike
- **Example creatures:** rhinoceros beetle, stag beetle (horn variant), unicorn beetle
- **Notes:** Likely consolidates with a general `horns` key from other domains; noting arthropod-specific example set (chitin, not keratin/bone).

### 10. `raptorial-forelegs`
- **What it is:** Enlarged, spined front legs folded for a rapid grab-and-hold strike, as in a mantis.
- **Actions:** strike, ambush, snare, crush
- **Example creatures:** praying mantis, mantis shrimp (forelimb variant), assassin bug
- **Notes:** Distinct action signature (ambush strike + hold) from generic `pincers`; worth keeping separate because the verb set (ambush+snare together) is unusual.

### 11. `smashing-claw` (mantis shrimp club)
- **What it is:** A club-like raptorial appendage that delivers an extremely fast, blunt-force strike.
- **Actions:** strike, crush
- **Example creatures:** mantis shrimp
- **Notes:** Narrow/exotic example; could be folded into `pincers` or `raptorial-forelegs` centrally, but the strike mechanism (blunt smash, not grip) is functionally distinct — flag for curator judgment.

### 12. `barbed-legs` / `spined-legs`
- **What it is:** Walking or jumping legs lined with rigid spines or barbs, usable defensively or to grip prey.
- **Actions:** rake, snare, ward
- **Example creatures:** stick insect, tarantula, praying mantis (leg spines), thorny devil katydid
- **Notes:** Overlaps `raptorial-forelegs` when on forelimbs specifically; this entry covers the general spined-limb case including mid/hind legs.

### 13. `urticating-hairs`
- **What it is:** Fine, barbed, irritant-coated hairs or bristles that detach into the air or onto a threat on contact.
- **Actions:** cloud, terrorize, ward
- **Example creatures:** tarantula, some caterpillars (io moth, saddleback caterpillar)
- **Notes:** Explicitly named in the assignment. "Cloud" fits well (airborne irritant dispersal on tarantulas kicking hairs); caterpillar version is more contact-based (ward/terrorize).

### 14. `venom-bristles` / `spined-bristles`
- **What it is:** Rigid, hollow or grooved body spines that inject irritant or toxin on contact, distinct from the fine urticating hair type.
- **Actions:** strike, terrorize, ward
- **Example creatures:** saddleback caterpillar, puss caterpillar, sea urchin (cross-reference echinoderm section)
- **Notes:** Sits between `urticating-hairs` (fine, airborne) and `spines` (rigid, mechanical-only); curator may merge with one or the other.

### 15. `wing-cases` (elytra)
- **What it is:** Hardened forewings that fold over and protect the flight wings and body, as in beetles.
- **Actions:** ward
- **Example creatures:** beetle (any), ladybug
- **Notes:** Purely defensive plating variant; low action-verb count, borderline for inclusion — flag as a possible cut.

### 16. `stridulating-limbs` (non-wing sound organs)
- **What it is:** Ridged leg or body segments rubbed together to produce a loud warning sound.
- **Actions:** terrorize, ward
- **Example creatures:** cricket (leg-on-wing variant), some grasshoppers
- **Notes:** Borderline "action-relevant" — mostly deterrence/communication. Include as terrorize-focused entry; curator may cut as too niche or merge into a general sound-organ key if one exists.

### 17. `pedipalps`
- **What it is:** Paired sensory-and-grasping appendages near the mouth, smaller than pincers/chelicerae, used to manipulate prey or (in scorpions) as an additional pinching pair.
- **Actions:** strike, snare
- **Example creatures:** scorpion, spider, whip scorpion
- **Notes:** In scorpions these ARE the large pincers (overlaps `pincers` entry #1 — scorpion pedipalps are commonly just called pincers/claws). In spiders they're small and manipulative only. Flag for merge-or-split decision.

### 18. `whip-tail` (uropygid/vinegaroon tail)
- **What it is:** A thin, non-stinging tail-like appendage that sprays defensive acid/acetic compound from the base.
- **Actions:** spray, ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** vinegaroon, whip scorpion
- **Notes:** Not a stinger (no venom injection) — functions as an aiming/spray organ. Distinct enough from `stinger` to keep separate; overlaps `spray` mechanism seen in mollusk ink below.

### 19. `book-lungs-spiracles` — EXCLUDED
- Internal/respiratory; excluded per brief (no internal organs). Noted only to confirm it was considered and cut.

### 20. `compound-eyes` — EXCLUDED (sensory-only)
- Noted and cut; sensory organ without independent action capability, unlike antennae.

### 21. `swarm-node` / `hive-body` (colonial/swarm anatomy)
- **What it is:** Not a single creature's limb but the functional concept of a colony body — many small individuals (a swarm) acting as one distributed "part," able to engulf, disperse, or reform.
- **Actions:** cloud, swarm-specific: burst (scatter), snare (engulf), terrorize
- **Example creatures:** locust swarm, army ant colony, bee swarm, wasp nest defenders
- **Notes:** This is conceptually different from the other entries — it's a colonial-scale anatomy concept rather than a body part. Flag explicitly for the brief's "colonial/swarm-relevant parts" ask. Might not fit the per-creature `anatomy` field at all if the schema is single-organism; include for curator to decide whether swarm-scale entries belong in this field or a different one (e.g., a "form" or "swarm" trait).

### 22. `mandible-pincer-hybrid` (stag beetle jaws)
- **What it is:** Grossly enlarged mandibles shaped and used like external pincers for wrestling/shoving rivals.
- **Actions:** shove, crush, strike
- **Example creatures:** stag beetle, some rhinoceros beetles
- **Notes:** Likely a merge candidate with `mandibles` (#3) or `pincers` (#1) — flagged separately because the display/wrestling function (shove-dominant) differs from the shearing function of standard mandibles.

## Mollusks

### 23. `tentacles`
- **What it is:** Long, flexible, muscular limbs, often suckered, used for grasping, pulling, and manipulation.
- **Actions:** lash, snare, crush, drain, shove
- **Example creatures:** octopus, squid, giant squid, cuttlefish
- **Notes:** Core entry, near-certain to already exist centrally. Overlaps `tendrils` (thinner/more whip-like) and `suckers` (the gripping surface on the tentacle, often modeled as a separate sub-part).

### 24. `tendrils`
- **What it is:** Thin, whip-like, often more numerous or finer appendages than tentacles, used for probing and light snaring rather than heavy grappling.
- **Actions:** lash, snare, ward
- **Example creatures:** jellyfish (cross-ref cnidarian), some squid (feeding tentacles vs. arms distinction), fictional eldritch creature limbs
- **Notes:** Explicitly named in assignment. In cephalopods, squid have 8 thicker "arms" plus 2 longer thinner "tentacles" for feeding strikes — that feeding-tentacle is arguably this key rather than #23. Curator should decide whether `tentacles` vs `tendrils` splits on thickness/count or on function (grip vs. probe/strike).

### 25. `feeding-tentacles` (squid strike-tentacles)
- **What it is:** A pair of extra-long, elastic tentacles tipped with suckers that shoot out to snatch prey at range.
- **Actions:** strike, snare, ambush
- **Example creatures:** squid, cuttlefish
- **Notes:** Specific mechanism (ballistic extension strike) distinct from the general grasping `tentacles`/`arms`. Strong candidate to keep separate given the unique "strike at range" action profile.

### 26. `suckers`
- **What it is:** Round, muscular adhesive discs lining the underside of tentacles/arms, used to grip and hold fast to surfaces or prey.
- **Actions:** snare, drain, ward
- **Example creatures:** octopus, squid, cuttlefish
- **Notes:** Explicitly named in assignment. Usually modeled as a modifier/sub-feature of tentacles rather than standalone, but the brief lists it separately — include as its own candidate for curator to decide merge-or-split. "Drain" fits weakly (grip-and-hold enabling other draining action) — flag as a soft fit.

### 27. `radula` (rasping mouthparts, plain language)
- **What it is:** A tongue-like ribbon of tiny hooked teeth used to scrape, rasp, or drill through surfaces and flesh.
- **Actions:** rake, drain, crush
- **Example creatures:** snail, slug, cone snail, limpet
- **Notes:** Brief explicitly asks for this "as rasping mouthparts in plain words" — recommend a plain-language key like `rasping-tongue` or `rasp-mouth` rather than the Latin `radula`, per the brief's "plain-language, most universally known word" rule. Listing both forms here for curator naming choice.

### 28. `venom-proboscis` (cone snail harpoon)
- **What it is:** An extensible, needle-tipped tube that fires a single venomous harpoon-like tooth into prey.
- **Actions:** strike, drain, ambush
- **Example creatures:** cone snail
- **Notes:** Distinct mechanism (single-shot projectile harpoon) rather than a rasp — do not merge with `radula`/rasping-tongue; overlaps `stinger` in function but delivery mechanism (fired dart vs. fixed spike) differs.

### 29. `shell`
- **What it is:** A single-piece or hinged calcified external casing that the animal can withdraw into or use as a shield/ram.
- **Actions:** ward, shove
- **Example creatures:** snail, clam, giant clam, nautilus, hermit-crab-adopted shell (crustacean use)
- **Notes:** Overlaps `carapace` (arthropod domain) as the mollusk-structural equivalent; "shove" applies to species that ram or clamp (giant clam closing, conch kicking with its shell).

### 30. `siphon`
- **What it is:** A tubular muscular organ used to forcefully expel water, ink, or waste, and to enable jet propulsion.
- **Actions:** spray, burst, hurl
- **Example creatures:** squid, octopus, clam, scallop
- **Notes:** Explicitly named in assignment ("siphons/jets"). Dual mechanical role: propulsion (burst-move, not an attack verb per se, but "burst" fits a sudden jet-powered escape/charge) and expulsion (spray of water or ink).

### 31. `ink-sac-jet` (functional, not the internal sac itself)
- **What it is:** The externally-directed release of a thick ink or dye cloud through the siphon, used to obscure and confuse.
- **Actions:** cloud, ward
- **Example creatures:** octopus, squid, cuttlefish, sea hare
- **Notes:** The ink SAC itself is internal (excluded), but the expelled cloud/jet action is external and action-relevant — recommend keying this as an action of the `siphon` entry rather than a standalone part, but including here since ink-clouding is a very recognizable, distinct action set (cloud) worth flagging explicitly.

### 32. `beak` (cephalopod beak)
- **What it is:** A hard, sharp, parrot-like biting structure at the center of the arms, used to crush and tear.
- **Actions:** strike, crush, rake
- **Example creatures:** octopus, squid
- **Notes:** Overlaps a generic `beak` key from other domains (birds) — same word, convergent structure; cephalopod version is chitinous rather than keratin, hidden at the center of the arm mass rather than a facial feature.

### 33. `arms` (octopus arms, as distinct from tentacles)
- **What it is:** Eight thick, tapering, fully-suckered limbs used for crawling, manipulating, wrapping, and prying.
- **Actions:** lash, crush, snare, shove, drain
- **Example creatures:** octopus
- **Notes:** Functionally near-identical to `tentacles` (#23); cephalopod biology technically distinguishes "arms" (8, fully suckered) from "tentacles" (2, suckered only at the tip, extensible) — flag this precise distinction for the curator, since a game schema likely wants only one of the two as the general key with the other as a variant/note.

### 34. `mantle-clamp`
- **What it is:** The muscular, expandable mantle wall that can be flexed to clap shut, squeeze, or slam against a threat.
- **Actions:** shove, crush, ward
- **Example creatures:** clam, scallop (mantle/shell-clap swimming and defense), octopus (mantle contraction)
- **Notes:** Scallops famously "clap" their shells to swim away and can pinch; this is a lower-confidence/niche entry — flag as possible cut, but included for completeness of the "compound armor" and "coils" sweep since mantle motion is part of the shell-defense system.

## Cnidarians

### 35. `stinging-tentacles` (nematocyst tentacles)
- **What it is:** Long, trailing tentacles studded with microscopic venom-injecting capsules (nematocysts) that fire on contact.
- **Actions:** strike, drain, snare, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** jellyfish, sea anemone, Portuguese man o' war, coral polyp
- **Notes:** This is the cnidarian-specific variant of `tentacles`/`tendrils` — the defining feature is the venom-on-contact-without-grip mechanism (fire-and-forget stinging cells), distinct from cephalopod tentacles which grip via suckers. Recommend keeping distinct from mollusk tentacles or noting the nematocyst mechanism as a modifier.

### 36. `nematocyst-cloud` / `stinging-mesh`
- **What it is:** A broad trailing curtain or mat of fine stinging filaments (rather than discrete tentacles) that a creature drifts through prey.
- **Actions:** cloud, snare, terrorize, ward
- **Example creatures:** Portuguese man o' war (tentacle mat), box jellyfish, lion's mane jellyfish
- **Notes:** Overlaps `stinging-tentacles` but the "broad drifting curtain" version reads more as an area/cloud effect than a targeted limb — worth keeping as a separate flavor entry since the action verb "cloud" fits it better than a discrete tentacle.

### 37. `oral-arms`
- **What it is:** Frilled, often thick arm-like extensions around a jellyfish's mouth, used to gather and sting prey and pull it toward the mouth.
- **Actions:** snare, drain, strike
- **Example creatures:** jellyfish (true jellyfish/Scyphozoa), moon jelly
- **Notes:** Distinct from the outer ring of stinging tentacles — oral arms are shorter, thicker, and closer to the body/mouth. Likely a merge candidate with `stinging-tentacles` centrally, but the "gather and feed" function is more manipulative than the whip-like outer tentacles.

### 38. `polyp-colony-mat` (colonial/swarm-relevant)
- **What it is:** A shared, connected colonial structure of many small stinging polyps (as in coral or a siphonophore) acting as a single functional surface.
- **Actions:** ward, terrorize, snare
- **Example creatures:** coral, fire coral, Portuguese man o' war (itself a colonial organism, not a single animal)
- **Notes:** Colonial/swarm-relevant per assignment. Similar flag to the arthropod `swarm-node` entry — this is a structural/colonial concept rather than a discrete limb; the Portuguese man o' war is technically a colony of specialized zooids (float, tentacles, feeding, reproductive parts each separate organisms) which could itself justify a distinct "colonial-body" schema note.

### 39. `nettle-cells` (generalized cnidocytes on the body surface, not just tentacles)
- **What it is:** Stinging cells embedded directly in the body surface or bell, not just on trailing tentacles, that deliver a sting on any contact.
- **Actions:** strike, ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** fire coral, some anemones
- **Notes:** Distinguishing "whole-body sting" from "tentacle sting" may be over-granular for the schema — flag as likely merge into `stinging-tentacles` or a general `venomous-skin` key if one exists in another domain (e.g., amphibian).

## Annelids

### 40. `bristles` (chaetae/setae)
- **What it is:** Stiff, hair-like bristles projecting in rows along a segmented worm's body, used for grip, traction, and light defense.
- **Actions:** rake, ward, shove
- **Example creatures:** earthworm, bristleworm, sea mouse
- **Notes:** Explicitly relevant to the assignment's "urticating hairs/bristles" sweep, though most annelid bristles are mechanical (traction) rather than venomous — see #41 for the venomous variant.

### 41. `fireworm-bristles` (venomous/urticating annelid bristles)
- **What it is:** Detachable, hollow, venom-coated bristles that break off in a threat's skin on contact, causing intense irritation.
- **Actions:** cloud, terrorize, ward
- **Example creatures:** bearded fireworm, bristleworm
- **Notes:** This is the annelid-specific instance of the "urticating hairs/bristles" sweep item named directly in the assignment — mechanically parallel to the arthropod `urticating-hairs` entry (#13); likely a central merge of the two into one general `urticating-bristles` key with cross-domain examples.

### 42. `jaws` (polychaete eversible jaws)
- **What it is:** A muscular, retractable throat structure that can turn inside-out to thrust forward and bite with hooked jaws.
- **Actions:** strike, ambush, crush
- **Example creatures:** bobbit worm, bristleworm
- **Notes:** The "eversible pharynx with jaws" mechanism gives a very distinct ambush-strike profile (explosive strike from concealment) — the bobbit worm is a strong reference point for an "ambush from below" gameplay archetype. Recommend keeping distinct from generic vertebrate `jaws` due to the ballistic-eversion delivery.

### 43. `parapodia`
- **What it is:** Paired, paddle-like fleshy lobes along each body segment, fringed with bristles, used for crawling, swimming, and grip.
- **Actions:** shove, rake, ward
- **Example creatures:** ragworm, sea mouse, bristleworm
- **Notes:** Functions partly as a limb, partly as armor-fringe; low individual distinctiveness — likely mergeable into `bristles` (#40) or cut, included for completeness of the systematic sweep.

### 44. `sucker-mouth` (leech)
- **What it is:** A round, muscular, adhesive oral disc ringed with tiny teeth or blades, used to clamp onto a host and feed.
- **Actions:** drain, snare
- **Example creatures:** leech
- **Notes:** Distinct "drain" specialist entry — strongly thematic for a drain-focused creature. Overlaps `suckers` (mollusk, #26) in mechanism (adhesion) but combines it with a biting/feeding function, closer to a mouth-part than a limb.

### 45. `proboscis` (annelid/leech eversible feeding tube)
- **What it is:** A thin, extensible piercing tube everted from the mouth to penetrate a host and draw fluid.
- **Actions:** strike, drain
- **Example creatures:** leech (proboscis-feeding species), some marine worms
- **Notes:** Alternate feeding mechanism to `sucker-mouth`; some leeches use jaws+sucker, others use a needle-like proboscis instead — keep as a variant note rather than necessarily a separate top-level key.

## Echinoderms

### 46. `spines`
- **What it is:** Rigid, sharp, calcified projections covering the body, ranging from short defensive studs to long venomous needles.
- **Actions:** ward, strike, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** sea urchin, crown-of-thorns starfish, sea star (short-spine varieties)
- **Notes:** Core defensive entry; overlaps `urticating-hairs`/`venom-bristles` (arthropod section) in the venomous long-spine variant (e.g., flower urchin) — the mechanism (rigid calcite spike vs. flexible hollow hair) differs enough to keep separate.

### 47. `pedicellariae`
- **What it is:** Tiny, pincer-like jawed organs scattered across the body surface between the spines, used to grip, pinch, and in some species inject venom.
- **Actions:** strike, snare, ward
- **Example creatures:** sea urchin, sea star
- **Notes:** Very small-scale but genuinely distinct structure from the main spines — miniature pincers covering the whole body surface rather than limb-tip claws. Niche; flag as possible-cut but included per exhaustive-sweep instruction.

### 48. `tube-feet`
- **What it is:** Hundreds of small, hydraulic, suction-tipped tentacle-like feet on the underside, used for slow locomotion, gripping, and prying open prey (e.g., forcing open a clamshell).
- **Actions:** snare, crush, shove
- **Example creatures:** starfish/sea star, sea urchin, sea cucumber
- **Notes:** "Crush" fits the starfish's well-known technique of using coordinated tube-feet tension to pry bivalve shells apart; a distinctive mechanic (many small parts acting as one sustained pulling force) worth keeping as its own entry rather than folding into generic `suckers`.

### 49. `arm` (regenerating/detachable starfish arm)
- **What it is:** A flexible, spine-studded radiating limb that can grip, wrap, and — notably — detach and regenerate if lost.
- **Actions:** snare, lash, ward
- **Example creatures:** starfish/sea star, brittle star
- **Notes:** The self-detaching/regenerating property is more a trait/ability than an anatomy shape; flag for the curator on whether "can be shed and regrown" belongs as a note on this key or as a separate mechanical trait elsewhere in the schema. Brittle stars additionally use arms for rapid whip-like locomotion (lash fits well there).

### 50. `eversible-stomach` — EXCLUDED
- Starfish evert their stomach through their mouth to digest prey externally; considered but excluded as it reads as an internal organ turned inside-out rather than a true external limb/instrument. Flagged for curator in case the schema wants a "digestive weapon" exception similar to the sensory dual-role clause, but recommend cutting per the internal-organs constraint.

### 51. `cuvierian-tubules` (sea cucumber defense)
- **What it is:** Sticky, often toxic, thread-like tubules violently ejected from the rear of the body to entangle a threat.
- **Actions:** hurl, snare, cloud, ward
- **Example creatures:** sea cucumber
- **Notes:** A striking, very distinctive defensive mechanism (self-evisceration of sticky threads) — strong candidate to keep as its own entry given the unusual and vivid action profile (hurl+snare+cloud together is rare). Related to `spray`/`siphon` mechanisms in mollusks by ejection-force logic but the payload (sticky adhesive threads, not liquid/ink) differs.

### 52. `spiny-skin` / `dermal-ossicles`
- **What it is:** A leathery skin embedded with small calcified plates (ossicles), giving a rough, semi-armored surface without full rigid plating.
- **Actions:** ward
- **Example creatures:** sea cucumber, sea star (skin between spines)
- **Notes:** Weak/low-action entry, mostly a texture note rather than a distinct instrument — flag as likely cut or fold into `spines`/general armor language.

## Cross-cutting / colonial-swarm notes

Per the assignment's explicit call-out to include "colonial/swarm-relevant parts," three candidates above address this directly and are worth surfacing together for the curator:

- `swarm-node` / `hive-body` (#21, arthropod) — insect swarm/colony acting as one distributed body.
- `polyp-colony-mat` (#38, cnidarian) — coral/siphonophore colonial structure, including the Portuguese man o' war's true multi-organism colony.
- Tube-feet (#48) and pedicellariae (#47) are "many-small-parts-acting-as-one" at the individual-organism scale, which is a related but distinct concept (distributed micro-instruments on ONE creature, vs. a colony of many creatures/zooids acting as one).

These four represent a spectrum from "one creature with many tiny cooperating instruments" to "many creatures/zooids forming one functional body," and the schema may want to handle them differently (an `anatomy` entry vs. a `colonial` flag/trait elsewhere). Recommend the central curator make an explicit call here rather than silently merging or dropping them.

## Summary

Harvested 52 candidates across five invertebrate phyla groupings (arthropods: 22, mollusks: 12, cnidarians: 5, annelids: 6, echinoderms: 7), explicitly covering every part class named in the assignment — pincers, mandibles, stingers, spinnerets, tentacles/tendrils, suckers, radula (as plain-language rasping mouthparts), antennae, compound armor/carapace/exoskeleton plating, urticating hairs/bristles, siphons/jets, barbed limbs, coils (echinoderm arm-lash and cephalopod arm-wrap cover this loosely; no true "coiling" limb exists in this domain the way it does in snakes, so noted as a gap rather than forced), and colonial/swarm-relevant parts. Several entries are flagged as likely merge candidates (pincers/chelicerae/pedipalps; tentacles/arms/tendrils; carapace/exoskeleton-plating/shell; urticating-hairs/venom-bristles/fireworm-bristles) or possible cuts (wing-cases, stridulating-limbs, mantle-clamp, parapodia, pedicellariae, spiny-skin) for the central curation pass, and three colonial/swarm concepts are called out together since they may not fit a single-creature `anatomy` field cleanly. All findings are recorded in this file; nothing exists only in this summary.
