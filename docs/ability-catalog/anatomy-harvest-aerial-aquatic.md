# Anatomy Harvest — Aerial and Aquatic

Domain: birds, bats, flying insects (wing mechanics only, not covered elsewhere), fish, marine mammals, deep-sea life. Method: exhaustive sweep per brief, curation happens centrally later.

---

## 1. feathered-wings

- **what it is** — a bird's flight limb: rigid leading edge of primary/secondary flight feathers over a foldable arm-and-hand skeleton, used for powered flight, gliding, and as a striking/shielding surface when folded.
- **actions it enables** — strike, lash, shove, ward, hurl (wind gust off a wingbeat), snare (wing-trapping prey against the body)
- **example creatures** — eagle, swan, ostrich (flightless variant), owl, roc/thunderbird-type fictional giant birds
- **notes** — sibling to `membranous-wings` (bat/pterosaur-style) and `buzzing-wings` (insect); a swan's wing-strike and an eagle's wing-shove are both real defensive/offensive behaviors. "Wing-shield" (raptors mantling over prey/kills) could be a sub-note under `ward`.

## 2. membranous-wings

- **what it is** — a skin membrane (patagium) stretched between elongated finger bones (bat) or a single rod-like digit (pterosaur-style), used for flight and, folded, as a wrap/shroud.
- **actions it enables** — strike, lash, ward (wrapping/shrouding self or ally), snare (enveloping smaller prey), hurl (downdraft)
- **example creatures** — bat, flying fox, pterosaur-type fictional creature, flying-squirrel-type glider (patagium without true powered flight)
- **notes** — overlaps `feathered-wings` in function but distinct material/texture for description purposes; gliding membranes (flying squirrel, flying fish's pectoral fins used aerially) are a lesser variant worth a note rather than a separate key.

## 3. buzzing-wings

- **what it is** — paired or two-paired thin insect wings beaten at high frequency, producing lift, a loud drone/whine, and turbulent micro-gusts at close range.
- **actions it enables** — cloud (wing-generated dust/spore stirring), lash (wing-buffet at close range), terrorize (the drone/whine itself), hurl (minor)
- **example creatures** — dragonfly, wasp, cicada, giant-insect-type fictional creature
- **notes** — most insect offense comes from other parts (stinger, mandibles); this key is specifically the wing action itself (buffet/noise), not the insect's other weapons, which belong to other domains' harvests.

## 4. talons

- **what it is** — large, strongly curved, sharp raptorial claws on a bird's (or bird-like creature's) feet, built for a high-force grip-and-puncture strike.
- **actions it enables** — strike, rake, crush (grip strength), snare (binding/carrying off prey)
- **example creatures** — eagle, hawk, owl, roc-type fictional giant bird
- **notes** — variant of general `claws`/`talons` key used elsewhere in the catalog for ground predators; keep as the same key if a unified `talons` entry exists, otherwise this is the avian-specific instance. Overlaps with `beak` for a bird's total kit.

## 5. beak

- **what it is** — a hard keratinous bill forming a bird's jaws, shaped from short and crushing (finch) to long and probing (heron, hummingbird) to hooked and tearing (raptor).
- **actions it enables** — strike, crush, rake (tearing), drain (probing/piercing types, e.g., hummingbird-style), ambush (spear-strike, heron-style)
- **example creatures** — eagle, heron, toucan, hummingbird, pelican
- **notes** — a pelican's pouch (see `gular-pouch` below) is a distinct sub-feature worth its own entry if the catalog wants storage/capture actions. Overlaps with `bill` (interchangeable term; keep one key, note the synonym).

## 6. bill

- **what it is** — synonym/variant of beak, typically used for flatter or broader-jawed birds (duck, platypus, spoonbill) built more for filtering, dabbling, or shoveling than for tearing.
- **actions it enables** — crush, spray (dabbling/filtering water), snare (scoop-catching)
- **example creatures** — duck, platypus, spoonbill, pelican
- **notes** — strongly consider merging into `beak` as a single key with a "sub-type: filtering/dabbling" note rather than two separate registry entries.

## 7. gular-pouch

- **what it is** — an expandable throat sac beneath the beak used to scoop, hold, and drain water while straining out prey.
- **actions it enables** — snare, drain, hurl (regurgitation), spray
- **example creatures** — pelican, frigatebird (display variant), cormorant
- **notes** — dual-purpose: capture tool and (in frigatebirds) a display organ; the display use is not action-relevant and should be excluded, keep only the scoop/strain function.

## 8. crest

- **what it is** — erectile feathers or a bony/skin ridge atop a bird's (or bird-like creature's) head, raised for display and intimidation.
- **actions it enables** — terrorize, ward (bluff display)
- **example creatures** — cockatoo, cassowary (casque variant, bony), peacock
- **notes** — a cassowary's casque is a hard bony helmet, arguably closer to a `horns`/`crest` hybrid — worth flagging to the horn/crest harvest domain if it exists, since this is borderline aerial vs. terrestrial. Include here as the bird-adjacent variant.

## 9. spurs (wing or leg)

- **what it is** — a sharp bony or keratinous spike on a bird's wing joint (jacana, screamer) or leg (rooster, cassowary), used as a stabbing weapon in territorial combat.
- **actions it enables** — strike, rake
- **example creatures** — rooster, cassowary, screamer (wing-spurred bird), spur-winged goose
- **notes** — cassowaries also have a large dagger-like inner toe claw functioning similarly; note as a variant rather than separate key. Overlaps with generic `spurs` if defined elsewhere for mammals (platypus venom spur is a different mechanism, see `venom-spur` under injection-type parts if that domain exists — flag as cross-domain).

## 10. dorsal-fin

- **what it is** — a rigid or semi-rigid vertical fin on a fish's or marine mammal's back, primarily for stabilization but usable as a slashing or ramming edge in some species.
- **actions it enables** — strike, rake (in spined variants), ward (surfacing/threat display)
- **example creatures** — shark, dolphin, sailfish, lionfish (venomous-spined variant)
- **notes** — the lionfish/stonefish variant carries venomous spines and should cross-reference an injection/venom key if one exists elsewhere in the catalog; the plain stabilizer version is mostly non-action, include mainly for the spined/sailfish ramming variant.

## 11. pectoral-fins

- **what it is** — paired fins on a fish's sides used for steering, braking, and in some species launching the body out of water or delivering a slap.
- **actions it enables** — lash, shove, hurl (leaping via fin-launch, flying-fish style), strike (slap)
- **example creatures** — flying fish, manta ray (modified into "wings"), grouper
- **notes** — manta/stingray pectoral fins are so enlarged they function as underwater wings; consider noting them as a bridge concept to `membranous-wings` for aquatic flight. Ray's fins overlap with `barb`/`stinger-tail` entries for offense, which live under a different part.

## 12. caudal-fin (tail-fin)

- **what it is** — the fish's terminal tail fin, the primary thrust generator, usable as a powerful lateral striking slap.
- **actions it enables** — strike, lash, shove, hurl (splash/wave generation)
- **example creatures** — shark, tuna, swordfish, eel
- **notes** — distinct from `fluke` (marine mammal horizontal tail) — fish tails are vertical-beating, mammal flukes are horizontal-beating; keep as separate keys since orientation changes the description even if action set overlaps heavily.

## 13. fluke

- **what it is** — the broad, horizontal, paired tail fin of a cetacean or sirenian, powered by up-and-down spinal undulation, capable of an immensely forceful slap.
- **actions it enables** — strike, crush, lash, shove, ward (tail-slap warning/display, sperm-whale-style)
- **example creatures** — whale, dolphin, manatee, dugong
- **notes** — real whale tail slaps are documented as capable of killing/stunning prey and repelling orca attacks; strongest "crush" case among fin-type parts. Sibling to `caudal-fin`.

## 14. pectoral-flipper

- **what it is** — a paired, paddle-shaped forelimb on a marine mammal, used for steering and also as a striking/slapping or grappling limb.
- **actions it enables** — strike, shove, lash, ward (slap on water surface as a threat display)
- **example creatures** — dolphin, seal, sea lion, whale
- **notes** — overlaps functionally with `fluke` for propulsion-derived strikes but is a forelimb, not the tail; keep distinct since a creature could plausibly have one without the other (e.g., a serpentine sea creature with flukes but vestigial flippers).

## 15. flippers (pinniped hind)

- **what it is** — the fused, webbed hind limbs of seals/sea lions, used primarily for aquatic propulsion, and on land dragged or used to shove/brace the body.
- **actions it enables** — shove, crush (body-slam via flipper-braced lunge)
- **example creatures** — seal, sea lion, walrus
- **notes** — largely a locomotion part; include narrowly for the land-based body-shove/lunge behavior seals exhibit when defending territory. Likely mergeable with `pectoral-flipper` into one general `flippers` key with a note distinguishing fore/hind.

## 16. tusks (marine)

- **what it is** — greatly elongated canine (walrus) or single spiraled incisor (narwhal) teeth projecting outside the mouth, used for hooking, jabbing, and combat.
- **actions it enables** — strike, rake, crush (hauling out on ice via tusk-hooking), ambush (narwhal fencing/stunning behavior)
- **example creatures** — walrus, narwhal
- **notes** — narwhal tusks are now understood to also have a sensory (electroreceptor-like) function — note the dual role per the brief's antennae/whiskers/barbels exception, though it's a borderline case since the sensory role is minor compared to a true barbel. Cross-reference a general `tusks` key if terrestrial tusks (elephant, boar) are cataloged elsewhere — likely the same key.

## 17. baleen

- **what it is** — rows of fringed keratin plates hanging from the upper jaw of baleen whales, used to filter and strain enormous volumes of water for prey, and swung/gulped in feeding lunges.
- **actions it enables** — snare (filter-catching), drain (bulk filter-feeding), shove (lunge-feeding water displacement)
- **example creatures** — humpback whale, blue whale, right whale
- **notes** — a strainer, not a weapon in the conventional sense, but the brief explicitly calls for "baleen-like strainers" — keep the snare/drain framing minimal and clearly non-violent, matching real baleen behavior.

## 18. barbels

- **what it is** — slender, whisker-like sensory-and-tactile feelers near the mouth of certain fish, used to detect food by touch and taste, occasionally to lure or probe.
- **actions it enables** — ambush (probing for buried prey), snare (minor, guiding prey toward mouth)
- **example creatures** — catfish, carp, sturgeon, goatfish
- **notes** — explicit dual-role part per the brief (sense + act) — include and flag the dual role clearly. Weak action profile; mostly a flavor/utility part rather than a combat one.

## 19. lure (illicium)

- **what it is** — a modified fin ray tipped with a fleshy, sometimes bioluminescent lobe, dangled in front of the mouth to attract prey within striking range.
- **actions it enables** — ambush, snare, beam (if the lure glows — bioluminescent variant)
- **example creatures** — anglerfish, frogfish
- **notes** — the classic "anglerfish-style" part called out directly in the assignment; the bioluminescent variant bridges to a light/beam-type part if the catalog has a dedicated luminous-organ key — cross-reference `photophores` below.

## 20. photophores

- **what it is** — clusters of light-producing cells or organs studding the skin of deep-sea creatures, used for counter-illumination camouflage, communication, startling, or attracting/repelling.
- **actions it enables** — beam, terrorize (sudden flash), cloud (light-based confusion burst, squid/some fish variant), ward (startle-flash defense)
- **example creatures** — anglerfish, lanternfish, deep-sea squid, viperfish
- **notes** — distinct from the anglerfish's single dangling `lure`; this is body-wide luminescence used defensively or as a startle/communication flash. Overlaps thematically with any generic "bioluminescence" key if the catalog already has one elsewhere — likely the same concept, keep one.

## 21. suction-discs (remora/octopus-style)

- **what it is** — round adhesive pads (on a modified dorsal fin for remoras, or lining the arms for cephalopods) that create strong vacuum grip on surfaces or prey.
- **actions it enables** — snare, crush (gripping constriction in cephalopod arms), drain (attaching to a host, remora-style, though this is closer to parasitism than combat)
- **example creatures** — remora, octopus, squid
- **notes** — the octopus/squid arm-sucker version is much more action-relevant (grappling, holding prey) than the remora hitchhiking version; consider whether remora's version belongs at all, or should be folded as a minor note under a broader `suckers` key shared with cephalopod tentacles (which likely belong to a separate "tentacle" harvest domain — flag as cross-domain overlap).

## 22. electric-organ

- **what it is** — modified muscle tissue capable of generating a strong electric discharge, delivered on contact or through water to stun prey or deter predators.
- **actions it enables** — strike, burst (shock discharge), ward
- **example creatures** — electric eel, electric ray (torpedo ray), stargazer fish
- **notes** — the brief asks for "external expression in plain words" — describe as "a shock delivered through touch or water" rather than any voltage/amperage specifics. This is a genuinely external, action-relevant organ (unlike a heart/lung), so it belongs despite sounding internal.

## 23. ink-sac-expulsion (jet/ink cloud)

- **what it is** — an expelled cloud of dark ink or a jet of water forcefully expelled from a siphon, used to obscure vision, distract, or propel the creature in a burst of speed.
- **actions it enables** — cloud, ambush (escape-then-strike), hurl (jet propulsion as a shove/ram)
- **example creatures** — octopus, squid, cuttlefish, sea hare (uses ink defensively)
- **notes** — two related but distinct mechanisms worth separating in notes: (1) ink cloud = pure `cloud`/concealment; (2) water-jet propulsion (mantle siphon) = sudden burst movement that could enable a ramming `hurl`/`shove`. Consider whether these should be two keys (`ink-cloud` and `jet-propulsion`) rather than one — recommend splitting at curation.

## 24. siphon (jet-propulsion)

- **what it is** — a muscular funnel-shaped organ that expels a forceful water jet, the primary swimming mechanism of cephalopods, usable for a sudden burst of speed or a directed water-blast at a target.
- **actions it enables** — hurl, shove, spray, burst
- **example creatures** — octopus, squid, cuttlefish, nautilus
- **notes** — split candidate from `ink-sac-expulsion` above; if curation keeps ink and jet as one key, this note-set should merge into it. If split, this key covers the pure hydraulic-force actions (spray/shove/burst) while ink-cloud covers concealment (cloud/ambush).

## 25. rostrum (bill/sword)

- **what it is** — an elongated, rigid, often blade- or spear-like extension of the upper jaw or snout, used to slash through prey schools or impale/stun.
- **actions it enables** — strike, rake, ambush, crush (ramming impact)
- **example creatures** — swordfish, marlin, sawfish, paddlefish (blunt filter-feeding variant)
- **notes** — the sawfish variant additionally has lateral teeth along the rostrum, functioning like a serrated blade — could note as a sub-variant ("saw-rostrum") rather than a new key. Distinct from `beak`/`bill` (bird) despite the naming overlap; keep separate keys, note the homonym risk.

## 26. gill-cover-spines (opercular spines)

- **what it is** — hardened, sometimes barbed points on the bony plate covering a fish's gills, which can be flared and used to jab or lodge painfully in a predator's mouth.
- **actions it enables** — strike, ward, snare (lodging/anchoring to resist swallowing)
- **example creatures** — catfish, many spiny-finned fish, lionfish (also has separate venomous fin spines)
- **notes** — the brief says gill covers are only relevant if action-relevant — this is the specific action-relevant case (defensive flaring/jabbing), not the gill itself (which stays excluded as respiratory). Keep narrowly scoped to the spine, not the gill function.

## 27. fin-spines (venomous or non-venomous)

- **what it is** — hardened, sharp rays at the leading edge of dorsal, pectoral, or anal fins, some hollow and venom-delivering, used defensively when the creature is threatened or trodden on.
- **actions it enables** — strike, ward, rake
- **example creatures** — lionfish, stonefish, stingray-adjacent spined fish, catfish (pectoral spine lock)
- **notes** — heavy overlap with `dorsal-fin` note above; recommend consolidating into one `fin-spines` key covering all venom/non-venom spined-fin cases rather than repeating under dorsal-fin. Venom delivery specifics should cross-reference a general venom/injection key if the catalog has one.

## 28. stinger-tail (ray barb)

- **what it is** — one or more serrated, venomous barbs near the base of a stingray's tail, whipped upward and forward in a defensive strike.
- **actions it enables** — strike, rake, ward
- **example creatures** — stingray, some skates
- **notes** — distinct mechanism from `caudal-fin`/`fluke` (this is a rigid barb near the tail base, not the fin itself); cross-reference venom/injection key if separate from raw physical damage.

## 29. mantle (cephalopod body-flare)

- **what it is** — the muscular sac forming a cephalopod's main body, capable of rapid color/texture change and sudden expansion/flaring to look larger.
- **actions it enables** — terrorize, ward
- **example creatures** — octopus, cuttlefish, squid
- **notes** — mostly a display/bluff part rather than a striking one; borderline whether it clears "action-relevant" bar on its own, include for the terrorize/ward display function (cuttlefish "flamboyant display" and octopus dramatic color-flash before attacking/fleeing are well documented).

## 30. wing-claws (juvenile/atavistic)

- **what it is** — small clawed digits retained at the leading edge of an otherwise feathered or membranous wing, usable for climbing or gripping and, secondarily, as a hooking strike.
- **actions it enables** — rake, snare
- **example creatures** — hoatzin chick (real-world claw-winged bird), generic "wyvern/gargoyle-type" fictional flyer with clawed wingtips
- **notes** — niche/edge-case entry; likely a note under `feathered-wings` or `membranous-wings` ("clawed variant") rather than a standalone key, flagging for curation to decide.

## 31. air-bladder-burst (defensive puff-up)

- **what it is** — a rapidly water- or air-inflatable body (stomach or specialized sac) that swells the creature to several times its size, sometimes lined with spines that erect upon inflation.
- **actions it enables** — ward, terrorize, burst (the inflation itself as a sudden defensive event)
- **example creatures** — pufferfish, porcupinefish, blowfish
- **notes** — borderline internal-organ exclusion (the brief excludes stomachs), but the *behavior* (external size-doubling plus spine erection) is the action-relevant, externally visible part — recommend keying it to the spine/skin expansion result rather than the internal bladder itself; consider folding into a generic `spines` key elsewhere in the catalog with an inflatable-variant note instead of a standalone key here.

## 32. slime-coat (hagfish-style)

- **what it is** — glands along a creature's flanks that exude copious thick slime on contact or threat, clogging a predator's gills or mouth.
- **actions it enables** — ward, snare (fouling/immobilizing an attacker's mouth), cloud (murk-generation, loosely)
- **example creatures** — hagfish, some eels
- **notes** — arguably a skin secretion rather than a discrete "part," but it is externally expressed and action-relevant defensively; flag for curation as to whether it belongs here or in a dedicated secretions/toxin harvest domain if one exists.

---

## Summary

Harvested 32 candidates spanning avian flight and feeding anatomy (feathered wings, talons, beak/bill, gular pouch, crest, spurs), membranous and insect wing variants, fish locomotion and offense (dorsal fin, pectoral fins, caudal fin, rostrum, gill-cover spines, fin-spines, stinger-tail), marine-mammal limbs (fluke, pectoral flipper, hind flippers, tusks, baleen), deep-sea specialized organs (lure, photophores, electric organ, barbels), and cephalopod mechanisms (ink expulsion, jet-propulsion siphon, suction discs, mantle-flare), plus two edge-case entries (wing-claws, air-bladder-burst) and one skin-secretion edge case (slime-coat) flagged for possible relocation to a different harvest domain. Several explicit merge/split recommendations are noted inline for central curation: beak/bill likely one key, dorsal-fin's venomous-spine note should fold into a unified fin-spines key, ink-expulsion and jet-propulsion are proposed as a split rather than one entry, and fore/hind marine-mammal flippers may collapse into one flippers key.
