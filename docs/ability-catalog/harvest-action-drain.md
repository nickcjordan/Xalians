## 6. Honest richness assessment

The DRAIN field is very rich in verbs and abstract nouns — English has an unusually dense vocabulary for gradual, parasitic, or forced loss of vitality/fluid/strength (siphon, sap, leech-as-verb, enervate, debilitate, wither, wick, milk, wring), giving strong element-neutral naming options built on "touch / grasp / grip / clutch / bite" combined with a vitality-noun (essence, vitality, vigor, marrow, reserve). This is the field's strongest sub-concept: parasitic-contact naming (Draining Touch, Sapping Grasp, etc.) is deep and produces many natural, non-repetitive two-word names.

Weaker sub-concept: pure noun variety for the "thing taken" is good (essence, vitality, vigor, marrow, reserves, strength, lifeforce) but starts to repeat in combination — many candidate names are near-synonyms of each other (Vital Drain / Vitality Drain / Life Drain / Essence Drain), so curation will need to prune duplicates rather than lack for raw material.

The anatomy/mechanism table is thinner than for a field like CRUSH or RAKE — drain-associated anatomy centers almost entirely on tubular/adhesive/piercing feeding structures (siphon, proboscis-analog, suckers, clamping jaws) rather than a wide range of distinct body plans. This is expected: draining is functionally about the point of contact plus a passive/active pull, not a varied strike mechanism.

Element-leaning candidates are moderately rich for Ghost and Psychic (soul/spectral/mind vocabulary pairs very naturally with draining) and for Water (tidal/undertow imagery is a strong metaphorical fit for a pulling force). Fire, Ice, Electric, Metal, Plant, Sand, and Chemical all produced usable but thinner options — mostly a single element-flavor noun (ember, frost, static, rust, sand, toxin, bloom) bolted onto "drain" or "sap," with less idiomatic depth than the ghost/psychic/water sets. Rock and Light were the thinnest; no strong element-leaning DRAIN name emerged for either beyond a generic "Stone Sap," and Light produced nothing worth listing at all — draining reads as fundamentally a taking-away action, which sits awkwardly against Light's usual associations (radiance, growth, healing), so that gap is honest rather than a miss.

## Excluded as species-reference (anatomy note)

Leech (as a noun naming the animal) — excluded per brief; retained only as a common English verb ("to leech") and as the general noun sense of the act, never as a name referencing the creature itself.
Lamprey — excluded entirely, real-animal name; the parasitic-mouthpart concept it evokes is captured instead by the anatomy table's "Siphon / Suckle / Tap" entries.
Tick — excluded, real-animal name (parasitic feeding association).
Vampire bat — excluded, real-animal name; "Vampirism" as an abstract noun is retained since it names a behavior/condition, not a species.
Mosquito — excluded, real-animal name.

## Excluded as other-domain

Rot — primarily decay/organic-breakdown domain (closer to a Plant/Chemical rot-and-decay field than direct vitality-draining), listed in nouns/verbs for completeness but not carried into candidate names.
Blight — primarily a disease/affliction-spreading domain, not a direct siphon-style drain; excluded from candidate names.
Corrode / Corrosion — Metal/Chemical material-degradation domain, not vitality-draining; excluded from candidate names.
Malaise — general sickness/mood domain rather than an active draining mechanism; excluded from candidate names.
Infest / Infestation — vermin/disease-spreading domain rather than direct single-target vitality drain; excluded from candidate names.
Blanch — color-change domain (paling), not vitality loss; excluded entirely from candidates.
