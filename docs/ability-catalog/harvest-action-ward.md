# Harvest: Action Field — WARD (protects self or those nearby)

Scope: shielding, bulwarks, aegises, barriers, guarding, fortification, sheltering. Religious-register protection words permitted (sanctuary, benediction) per brief.

## 6. Honest richness assessment

The WARD field is extremely rich — arguably one of the richest of the 16 actions. English has deep, well-populated vocabulary across several registers that all converge on "protects self or those nearby":

- **Military/fortification register** (bulwark, bastion, rampart, redoubt, stockade, citadel, garrison, entrench) is dense and produces many strong, distinct-feeling nouns and verbs with clear architectural imagery — good for heavier, tankier species.
- **Personal/bodily armor register** (shield, buckler, carapace, plating, armor, casing) is dense and pairs naturally with the anatomy table — strong hooks for exoskeletal, scaled, or plant-barked species.
- **Watching/guarding register** (guard, sentinel, warden, vigil, custody, escort) gives good "active protector" flavor, useful for support-role or ally-shielding abilities (matches the "or those nearby" half of the brief) — sentinel/warden/escort read naturally as protecting *others*, not just self.
- **Sheltering/enclosure register** (shelter, sanctuary, refuge, haven, harbor, enclose, envelop) gives softer, more passive-defense flavor and overlaps usefully with the permitted religious-register words.
- **Religious/sacred-protection register** (benediction, blessing, grace, consecrate, sanctify, providence) is present but must stay abstract per the brief — these work best as modifiers or single evocative nouns rather than combat mechanics, so they're comparatively thin as verbs (mostly "bless," "consecrate," "sanctify" — only three strong verbs).
- **Deflection/absorption register** (deflect, parry, block, intercept, absorb, withstand, cushion, buffer, insulate) is the most mechanically distinct sub-concept — it describes *how* the ward works (redirect vs. soak vs. block) rather than just naming a barrier, and is likely the most useful sub-field for differentiating multiple ward-type moves within one element.
- **Weak spot:** genuinely novel single-word nouns beyond the ones listed are scarce once you exclude proper-noun-associated words (Bastille, Aegis-as-mythic-name is borderline) and once fortification jargon gets too technical/obscure to read as natural English (counterscarp, glacis, escarpment were considered and excluded as too technical/unfamiliar to read as natural ability names, not because they're wrong-domain).
- Element-leaning candidates in section 5 came easily for every element checked (Fire, Water, Ice, Rock, Metal, Plant, Sand, Electric, Dark, Ghost, Light, Psychic, Chemical, Air) — WARD is one of the most portable actions across the full 14-element roster, since every element has a natural "wall/veil/shell of X" construction.

## Excluded as other-domain

- Absolution — domain: forgiveness/religious pardon, not protection
- Insurance — domain: financial/contractual risk transfer, not physical protection
- Vanguard — domain: leaning offensive/leading-position (vanguard is usually the forward-most attacking line, not defensive), excluded despite one archaic defensive sense
- Bastille — excluded, too strongly tied to the specific proper-noun Paris fortress in modern English usage
- Custody (family-law sense) — kept only in the abstract "guardianship" sense; the legal-child-custody sense is domain: family law, noted for awareness
- Warranty — domain: commercial guarantee, not physical protection
- Escrow — domain: financial/legal holding, not protection
