1. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The archetype invariant is mathematically impossible: 16 archetypes × 2 favored attributes produces 32 assignments, but 10 attributes × exactly 3 archetypes produces 30. **Concrete suggestion:** Correct the roster or explicitly exempt archetypes such as `balanced`, then enforce assignment totals in schema validation.

2. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Ability intensity is graded 1–10 despite the ratified rule that all graded values use 0–100. Third-party games cannot know which rule is authoritative. **Concrete suggestion:** Store intensity as 0–100 or formally narrow the uniform-scale rule and document conversion semantics.

3. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Seed plus `generatorVersion` does not guarantee reproducibility without a canonical PRNG, byte order, table ordering, numeric rounding, rejection behavior, and random-consumption order. Implementations will generate different creatures from identical provenance. **Concrete suggestion:** Publish a bit-exact generation specification with domain-separated RNG streams per field and golden seed/output fixtures.

4. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Provenance is not proof. Anyone can fabricate a favorable record or grind seeds and claim a desirable serial, origin, or timestamp. **Concrete suggestion:** Canonically serialize and hash each record, sign it with a generator identity, and define serial namespaces plus an issuance or commitment policy.

5. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** `generatorVersion` is an opaque promise to preserve an entire snapshot; no content digest, immutable locator, archival requirement, or schema-compatibility contract exists. A missing or replaced snapshot makes records unverifiable. **Concrete suggestion:** Make it a content-addressed manifest containing hashes for the algorithm and each registry, and guarantee permanent retrieval with explicit compatibility metadata.

6. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Ability generation is not fully specified: regular ability count, signature inclusion, action selection, medium selection, duplicate handling, retry behavior, and empty-pool fallback are absent. Two conforming generators could produce materially different records. **Concrete suggestion:** Specify the complete probability tree and validation/fallback behavior, including whether names may repeat within one creature.

7. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The catalog schema has no field implementing “heavier names go to heavier intensity rolls.” It contains only `minIntensity`, so weighting must either be hidden or inferred from language, contradicting the machine-readable contract. **Concrete suggestion:** Add an explicit heft tier or a ten-value intensity weight curve to every entry.

8. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The dark catalog’s coverage claim is unsupported. It relies on “approx” neutral counts, including “drain 85 (needs dedupe),” before instrument tagging and curation; raw pools contain anatomy-bound, weak, duplicate, and invalid names. **Concrete suggestion:** Do not report coverage until entries are machine-readable, deduplicated, approved, and filtered using exact species instruments and intensity ranges.

9. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** The anomalous affinity rule potentially makes every off-graph medium reachable for every species. If abilities can use that affinity, the supposedly sparse coverage problem expands toward every allowed instrument × action × all 14 media combination. **Concrete suggestion:** Define the anomaly candidate set precisely and make the checker include anomalous reachability—or explicitly prohibit anomalous affinities from composed abilities.

10. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Instrument tags are not using the declared controlled vocabulary. `consolidated-dark.md` contains prose tags such as “grasping limbs” and even `mind`, with no declared mapping to the 25 instrument keys. **Concrete suggestion:** Allow only registry keys or formally defined instrument groups, and validate every tag against both the instrument registry and its allowed-action table.

11. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Signature behavior has no stable machine key. The record’s `signature` field is type-ambiguous, while bespoke game behavior is said to be keyed “to the signature”; using the display name as that key is fragile. **Concrete suggestion:** Add immutable `abilityId` and `signatureId` fields, define `signature` as a boolean only if still needed, and reserve retired IDs and names permanently across versions.

12. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Collision checking only against the current catalog cannot protect future catalogs or historical signatures. At scale, additions can collide with names embedded in older generator snapshots. **Concrete suggestion:** Maintain a normalized, append-only global namespace with tombstones and collision checks across every published version, including case, punctuation, spacing, and inflection rules.

13. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Archetype tilt, attribute rolls, affinity strength, trait draw counts, and temperament weighting lack exact distributions. “Toward band tops” and “tilted by strength” do not define probabilities, clipping, rounding, or correlation strength. **Concrete suggestion:** Specify formulas and fixed-point arithmetic, then publish Monte Carlo acceptance ranges and golden statistical tests for every species/version.

14. **Severity: high. Specific hole:** Independent bands can generate physically incoherent records: implausible height/weight density, inverted climate bounds, respiration incompatible with corporeality, or high flight capability without supporting physiology. No invariant or dependency layer is described. **Concrete suggestion:** Add joint distributions and declarative requirements/exclusions, followed by whole-record validation before issuance.

15. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** The element schema calls `affinities` a map of secondaries while deliberately duplicating the primary there at 100. It is also unclear whether anomalous is a third mutually exclusive outcome, whether multiple secondaries are possible, and where their strengths come from. **Concrete suggestion:** Define one canonical representation and exact cardinalities; preferably keep primary out of `secondaryAffinities` and expose a separate computed all-affinities view.

16. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Favored-only archetype tilt is not power-neutral. Its benefit depends on band width and remaining headroom, so archetypes and species receive unequal total boosts and values may pile up near ceilings. **Concrete suggestion:** Apply tilt in percentile space with a fixed expected budget, and test total-stat distributions by species/archetype.

17. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** “Temperament never power” is unenforceable and likely false in games where aggression, energy, or sociability affects tactical behavior. “Never zeroed” also does not establish how rare gentle-giant outcomes actually are. **Concrete suggestion:** Treat non-power use as guidance rather than contract, publish conditional distributions, and set measurable tail-probability requirements for intended mismatches.

18. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** The nature-versus-rules boundary is unclear. `strike`, `mend`, `terrorize`, passive/reactive classification, and numeric intensity already encode a universal combat ontology rather than purely observed nature. **Concrete suggestion:** Define which fields are physical observations versus universal semantic assertions, and prohibit game outcomes such as damage, cooldown, or target rules from entering the record.

19. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** One-line registry descriptions are too weak for reliable third-party derivation. Developers will independently interpret distinctions such as agility versus reflex, resilience versus vitality, and cloud versus ward. **Concrete suggestion:** Give each key normative semantics, units or anchor examples, invariants, structured relationships, and conformance fixtures—not merely display text.

20. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** The one-action-per-name rule assumes action is inferable from prose, but the dark catalog disproves it: `Gravity Field` could be cloud, ward, or snare; `Quickening` could be mend or a speed effect; `Dark Star` does not inherently mean beam. **Concrete suggestion:** Treat action as authoritative structured data, not something inferred from the name, and allow controlled display-name reuse across actions if IDs remain unique.

21. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** The definition audit is internally inconsistent. `Umbral Pall` and `Smothering Darkness` survive despite dark being defined as gravity/void/time and shadow register being assigned to ghost; `Supernova` is accepted metaphorically while other metaphorical cross-domain terms are rejected literally. **Concrete suggestion:** Publish a medium-ownership matrix and a consistent rule for literal versus metaphorical usage, with per-tag justification stored in catalog data.

22. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Positive-only additive traits make trait count an implicit rarity and power signal, while duplicate handling and dependencies between guaranteed and rolled traits are unspecified. Excluding only “true contradictions” will not catch redundancy or prerequisite failures. **Concrete suggestion:** Define set semantics, deduplication, prerequisites, soft incompatibilities, maximum counts, and whether games must treat extra traits as mechanically neutral.

23. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Immutable records have no response mechanism for corrupt generation, exploitable seeds, illegal combinations, or compromised signing keys. Per-game balance patches do not solve record validity. **Concrete suggestion:** Add an external signed status/errata system supporting invalidation and interpretation corrections without mutating the original record.

24. **Severity: medium. Specific hole:** Stage 1 is unverifiable. Harvest files provide no source URLs or bibliography, and “borrowed name” detection depends on what an agent happens to recognize. Claims of exhaustiveness, definition auditing, and originality cannot be reproduced. **Concrete suggestion:** Record dictionary/corpus provenance per candidate, maintain a competitor-name corpus, and require automated similarity checks plus independent human review.

25. **Severity: low. Specific hole:** The harvest process’s “hard constraints” are demonstrably not enforced: files use forbidden em dashes, and raw candidate pools retain entries such as `Domain of Gloom`, `Hold the Line`, and `Baring of Fangs` despite the two-word rule. This makes prompt compliance an unreliable quality gate. **Concrete suggestion:** Run a mechanical linter at every stage for word count, punctuation, spelling, registry keys, duplicates, unresolved flags, and prohibited vocabulary; reject failing artifacts before consolidation.
