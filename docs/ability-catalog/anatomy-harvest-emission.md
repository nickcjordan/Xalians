# Anatomy Harvest: Secretion, Projection & Emission Apparatus

Domain: parts that spray, spit, squirt, secrete, flash, or discharge — venom delivery, spitting, spray glands, ink sacs, silk spinners, light organs, electric organs, sonic apparatus, scent glands, sticky/slime surfaces, heat vents, and other emission mechanisms.

---

## Venom Delivery Systems

### `fangs`
- **What it is:** Hollow or grooved piercing teeth connected to a venom gland, delivering toxin on bite.
- **Actions it enables:** strike, drain, ambush, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** viper, cobra, black widow spider (chelicerae function similarly), vampire bat (non-venomous variant)
- **Notes:** Overlaps with a generic `fangs` grip/bite entry if one exists in the "mouth apparatus" domain — this entry is specifically the venom-delivery function. Sibling of `stinger` and `spurs` as alternate venom-injection hardware. Retractable fangs (viper) vs fixed (elapid) is a texture variant, not a separate key.

### `stinger`
- **What it is:** A sharp, often barbed abdominal or tail-tip organ that injects venom via a connected sac.
- **Actions it enables:** strike, ambush, terrorize, drain
- **Example creatures:** scorpion, bee, wasp, stingray, manta ray barb
- **Notes:** Distinguish from `tail-spike` (purely mechanical, no venom) in a piercing/blunt domain — that's a sibling, not a duplicate. Some stingers are single-use (bee) — a possible edge-case note for balance/lore, not the key itself.

### `spurs`
- **What it is:** A sharp keratinous or bony projection, often on the heel/ankle/wrist, connected to a venom gland in a minority of species.
- **Actions it enables:** strike, ambush, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** platypus (venomous heel spur), rooster (non-venomous spur), some lizards
- **Notes:** Most spurs are non-venomous weapons; only note the venom-delivery variant here. Overlaps with a generic "spurs" entry in a striking/piercing domain — this is the same key, dual-listed for the emission angle.

### `venom-spines`
- **What it is:** Rigid dorsal or fin spines coated in or hollow with venom, erected defensively or on contact.
- **Actions it enables:** strike, ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** lionfish, stonefish, some caterpillars (urticating spines), weever fish
- **Notes:** Passive/defensive emission — venom transfers on contact rather than active injection. Distinct from `stinger` in that there's no active thrusting motion typically; more of a "touch this and regret it" part.

### `venom-claws`
- **What it is:** Modified claws or foreclaws with a venom groove or duct, used to inject toxin while grappling.
- **Actions it enables:** strike, rake, crush, drain
- **Example creatures:** slow loris (brachial gland venom applied via bite/lick, but folkloric "venom claw" archetype exists in fiction), some fictional demon/beast designs
- **Notes:** Rare in real biology (slow loris actually uses licked brachial gland secretion, listed separately below) but common in creature fiction as "poison claws." Keep as a fiction-leaning entry.

## Spitting & Projectile Ejection

### `spitting-glands`
- **What it is:** Glands (often modified salivary or venom glands) paired with muscular ejection to forcibly spray venom or irritant fluid at a target from a distance.
- **Actions it enables:** spray, strike, ambush, terrorize, ward
- **Example creatures:** spitting cobra, archerfish (water jet, see below), bombardier beetle (chemical jet, see below), spitting spider (Scytodidae, glue-spit)
- **Notes:** This is the general "distance-spit" key; more specific variants (water-jet, glue-spit, chemical-jet) are listed separately as they have distinct mechanisms/uses. Could be considered the parent category with the others as specialized children.

### `water-jet-mouth`
- **What it is:** A specialized mouth/throat structure that compresses and fires a precise jet of water to strike or dislodge prey.
- **Actions it enables:** spray, strike, hurl
- **Example creatures:** archerfish
- **Notes:** Narrow but vivid mechanism; strong flavor part for aquatic or amphibious species. Non-toxic, purely kinetic/wetting.

### `glue-spit-fangs`
- **What it is:** Fanged mouthparts that fire twin jets of venom-glue in a zigzag pattern to bind prey in place.
- **Actions it enables:** spray, snare, ambush
- **Example creatures:** spitting spider (Scytodes)
- **Notes:** A rare, specific mechanism bridging venom delivery and snaring; good candidate for a `snare`-capable emission part alongside webs.

### `chemical-jet-abdomen`
- **What it is:** A rear abdominal chamber where two reactive chemicals mix and are explosively ejected as a hot, noxious spray, often aimable.
- **Actions it enables:** spray, burst, ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** bombardier beetle
- **Notes:** The "bombardier-style" defensive burst mechanism referenced in the brief. Distinct from ink/musk in that the discharge is hot, reactive, and audibly pops. Strong basis for a `burst`-type action part.

### `regurgitation-crop`
- **What it is:** A crop or foregut chamber used to forcibly eject stomach contents or a corrosive/foul-smelling bolus at a threat.
- **Actions it enables:** spray, ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** turkey vulture (defensive vomit), fulmar (oily stomach-oil spray), some caterpillars
- **Notes:** Borderline internal-organ territory (crop is technically internal) but the emission itself is an external, weaponized action — keep as it's a common "acid spit" fiction trope (e.g., many spitting insect larvae, fulmar's oil spray is genuinely potent and corrosive to feathers). Consider renaming to `spit-crop` or folding into `spitting-glands` if curators want fewer keys.

## Ink & Concealment Clouds

### `ink-sac`
- **What it is:** A gland-fed sac that expels a cloud of dark pigment (often mixed with mucus) to obscure vision and confuse pursuers.
- **Actions it enables:** cloud, ward, ambush
- **Example creatures:** octopus, squid, cuttlefish, sea hare
- **Notes:** The canonical `cloud`-action part. Sea hare's ink is purple/reddish rather than black — a texture/color variant, not a separate key. Some cephalopods also produce a decoy "pseudomorph" blob of ink — could be a notes-level nuance rather than separate key.

### `smoke-gland`
- **What it is:** A fictional/generalized analog to ink sacs for non-aquatic creatures — a gland that vents an obscuring smoke, dust, or vapor cloud.
- **Actions it enables:** cloud, ward, ambush
- **Example creatures:** common in creature fiction (smoke-screen beasts), loosely paralleled by musk deer glands venting under stress, or a skunk-adjacent dry variant
- **Notes:** Not a strong real-biology anchor beyond insect/arthropod defensive dusts (see `dust-scales` below); largely fiction-sourced. May be worth merging with `ink-sac` as a single "obscuring-cloud gland" key with wet/dry variants noted.

### `dust-scales`
- **What it is:** Loosely attached body scales or hairs that detach in a cloud when brushed or threatened, irritating or blinding pursuers.
- **Actions it enables:** cloud, ward
- **Example creatures:** moths (wing scales), tarantula (urticating hairs kicked into a cloud)
- **Notes:** Distinct mechanism from glandular ink — this is a shed/dislodged structure rather than a secreted fluid, but functionally the same "obscuring cloud" action. Tarantula hair-kicking is a great grounded real-animal example for a `cloud` + `ward` part.

## Silk & Web Production

### `spinnerets`
- **What it is:** Abdominal silk-extruding organs that draw liquid protein into solid thread on contact with air.
- **Actions it enables:** snare, ward, hurl (thrown web/net variants), ambush
- **Example creatures:** spider, silkworm (analogous spinneret near the mouth), some caterpillars
- **Notes:** The canonical `snare` part. Spiders have multiple spinneret types producing different silk (dragline, sticky capture, wrapping) — could be split into sub-variants (`capture-silk`, `dragline-silk`) if curators want granularity, but a single `spinnerets` key covering "produces web/silk" is likely sufficient for a game schema.

### `silk-mouth-glands`
- **What it is:** Labial (mouth-adjacent) glands that extrude silk thread, used for cocoon-spinning or line-dropping rather than abdominal spinnerets.
- **Actions it enables:** snare, ward
- **Example creatures:** silkworm moth larva, some sawfly larvae
- **Notes:** Functionally near-identical to `spinnerets` but anatomically distinct location (mouth vs abdomen) — likely mergeable into a single silk-producing key unless the schema wants mouth vs. rear distinction for visual/animation purposes.

### `web-shooters`
- **What it is:** A fiction-forward jet-propelled thread launcher that fires adhesive strands at range rather than spinning stationary webs.
- **Actions it enables:** snare, hurl, ward
- **Example creatures:** archetypal in superhero/creature fiction as a distance-web mechanism (distinct from natural spider spinnerets, which trail rather than launch)
- **Notes:** Flag as the fiction-distance variant of `spinnerets` — useful if the game wants a `hurl`+`snare` combo part that real spiders don't quite have (most spiders trail or balloon silk on wind, they don't "shoot" it accurately at a target, with some ballistic exceptions like the net-casting spider, below).

### `net-casting-forelimbs`
- **What it is:** Forelimbs that hold and stretch a small pre-woven web, which is thrown over prey on ambush.
- **Actions it enables:** snare, ambush, hurl
- **Example creatures:** net-casting spider (ogre-faced spider)
- **Notes:** A real and vivid grounded mechanism — combines silk production with a throwing action, distinct from passive stationary webs.

## Bioluminescent / Light Organs

### `photophores`
- **What it is:** Light-producing organs (via bioluminescent chemistry or symbiotic bacteria) embedded in skin, studding the body in patterns.
- **Actions it enables:** beam, terrorize, ward (as a startle-flash), ambush (as a lure)
- **Example creatures:** anglerfish (lure specifically, see below), lanternfish, firefly (abdominal light organ), many deep-sea species, glowworm
- **Notes:** Broad umbrella key for "body produces light." `esca` (anglerfish lure) and `light-lash` (firefly-style flashing tail) below are specialized sub-instances worth their own keys given distinct actions (lure/ambush vs. rhythmic signaling).

### `lure-appendage` (esca)
- **What it is:** A modified fin ray or antenna tipped with a bioluminescent or otherwise attractive bulb, dangled to draw prey close.
- **Actions it enables:** ambush, spray (bait-adjacent, weak fit), terrorize
- **Example creatures:** anglerfish, some lure-tailed vipers (caudal luring, non-luminous variant)
- **Notes:** The action fit here is mostly `ambush`; consider whether "lure" deserves recognition as related to `ambush` specifically. Non-luminous version (viper tail-lure) is a texture variant worth noting even though it doesn't emit light.

### `flash-organ`
- **What it is:** A light organ capable of rapid on/off flashing or a single bright burst, used for startling predators or signaling.
- **Actions it enables:** burst, ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** firefly, flashlight fish, some jellyfish
- **Notes:** Distinguish from steady-glow `photophores` by the rapid/pulsed nature — better mapped to `burst` action than a static `beam`.

### `glow-slime`
- **What it is:** Secreted mucus or slime that is itself bioluminescent, coating the body or trailing behind as a glowing decoy.
- **Actions it enables:** spray, cloud, ward
- **Example creatures:** millipede (bioluminescent warning glow, though not slime-based), certain glowing worms and jellyfish mucus, brittle star (detachable glowing arm)
- **Notes:** Overlaps with `slime-coat` (below) — this is the luminous variant. Also overlaps conceptually with `ink-sac`/`smoke-gland` as an escape-cloud, but glowing rather than obscuring (a "look over there" decoy rather than a "you can't see me" cloud).

## Electric Organs

### `electric-organ`
- **What it is:** Stacked modified muscle cells (electrocytes) capable of generating a strong electric discharge for stunning prey/predators or a weak field for electrolocation.
- **Actions it enables:** strike, burst, ward, ambush
- **Example creatures:** electric eel, electric ray (torpedo ray), electric catfish
- **Notes:** Note the dual-role electrolocation function (weak-discharge species use it to sense as well as act) — similar carve-out to antennae/whiskers per the brief's dual-role exception. The strong-discharge (eel) vs weak-discharge (electrolocating species) split could be a notes-level nuance.

### `shock-quills`
- **What it is:** A fiction-leaning variant where quills or spines double as electrical conduits, delivering a shock on contact rather than a full-body discharge.
- **Actions it enables:** strike, ward
- **Example creatures:** no strong real-animal anchor; parallels lionfish-style contact spines but electrified rather than venomous
- **Notes:** Include as a fiction/game-common trope (electric contact spines) even without a grounded real-animal example; flag for possible cut if curators want only real-anchored keys.

## Sonic / Acoustic Emission Apparatus

### `rattle`
- **What it is:** A tail-tip of loosely interlocked keratin segments that vibrate together to produce a buzzing warning sound.
- **Actions it enables:** ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** rattlesnake
- **Notes:** Purely external, non-vocal sound apparatus (the brief specifically calls out "rattles, drumming membranes" — this is the rattle instance).

### `tymbal`
- **What it is:** A ribbed exoskeletal membrane that buckles rapidly under muscle contraction, producing a loud clicking/droning call.
- **Actions it enables:** ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** cicada
- **Notes:** The other explicit example from the brief ("tymbals"). Distinct mechanism from vocal cords — a drum-like membrane rather than airflow-based sound.

### `drumming-membrane`
- **What it is:** A resonant skin, shell, or exoskeleton patch that a creature strikes (with a limb, or via internal muscle) to produce a percussive sound, used for territorial or startle signaling.
- **Actions it enables:** ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** grouse (wing-drumming), gorilla (chest-beating uses hands as the instrument, membrane is the chest/torso resonance), drumfish (swim-bladder drumming, borderline internal)
- **Notes:** Overlaps with `tymbal` functionally; keep both since tymbal is a specific insect structure while drumming-membrane covers vertebrate resonant-strike behavior more broadly. Chest-beating is really an action performed BY hands ON a torso resonator — could be captured by pairing with limb keys rather than a standalone anatomy part; flag for curation.

### `stridulation-organ`
- **What it is:** A rigid ridge-and-scraper pair (often on legs, wings, or mandibles) rubbed together to produce a chirping or hissing sound.
- **Actions it enables:** ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** cricket, grasshopper, many beetles, some spiders
- **Notes:** Mechanistically distinct from tymbal/drumming (friction-based "rubbing" rather than a vibrating membrane or percussive strike) — the classic "chirp" mechanism, worth its own key.

### `wing-clap`
- **What it is:** Specialized stiff wing feathers or membranes that snap together or against the body to produce a sharp crack or whistle in flight.
- **Actions it enables:** ward, terrorize, strike (as a startle-flush tactic)
- **Example creatures:** mourning dove (wing whistle), club-winged manakin, some bats
- **Notes:** A flight-linked sonic part; distinct from rattle/tymbal/stridulation in requiring wings and motion rather than a dedicated static organ.

### `throat-resonator`
- **What it is:** An inflatable throat sac or pouch that amplifies vocalizations into a booming or resonant call.
- **Actions it enables:** ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** howler monkey (hyoid resonating chamber), frog (vocal sac), prairie chicken (air sac)
- **Notes:** Borderline internal (it's an inflatable pouch, but externally visible and clearly an "emits sound" instrument) — flag for curator judgment on whether this crosses into excluded internal-organ territory. If kept, it's a solid `terrorize` part.

## Scent & Chemical Signaling Glands

### `musk-gland`
- **What it is:** A gland (often near the tail base or flank) that secretes a strong-smelling oily substance for territorial marking or as a general deterrent odor.
- **Actions it enables:** ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** musk deer, muskrat, musk ox
- **Notes:** Passive/ambient scent marking rather than an aimed spray — distinguishes from `spray-gland` below, which is a targeted defensive discharge.

### `spray-gland` (skunk-style)
- **What it is:** A paired anal gland capable of aiming and forcibly ejecting a foul, oily, long-range irritant spray at a threat.
- **Actions it enables:** spray, ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** skunk, striped polecat, some mustelids, bombardier beetle (chemical variant already covered under `chemical-jet-abdomen`)
- **Notes:** This is the explicit "skunk-style spray glands" example named in the brief. Distinct from `musk-gland` (passive marking) by being an aimed, forceful defensive discharge; distinct from `chemical-jet-abdomen` by being a cold oily liquid rather than a hot reactive chemical burst.

### `pheromone-gland`
- **What it is:** A gland releasing airborne chemical signals to attract mates, summon allies, or mark alarm/trail routes for others of its kind.
- **Actions it enables:** ward (alarm pheromone), spray (release action), terrorize (alarm-signal variant)
- **Example creatures:** ants (trail and alarm pheromones), moths (long-range mating pheromones), bees (alarm pheromone at the stinger)
- **Notes:** More signaling than combat-relevant in most cases; weakest action fit of the scent-gland family. Worth including since "emits something" is broad per the assignment, but curators may cut for being too passive/non-actionable.

### `slime-coat`
- **What it is:** A mucus-secreting skin surface producing a thick, slippery, sometimes toxic or irritant coating over the body.
- **Actions it enables:** ward, spray (when actively sloughed/flung), snare (sticky variant, see below)
- **Example creatures:** hagfish (explosive slime defense), lamprey, many amphibians, slug/snail
- **Notes:** Hagfish specifically can explosively expand slime in water to clog a predator's gills within a fraction of a second — a strong grounded example for `spray`/`ward`/`burst`. Distinguish from `sticky-pads` (below), which is a grip/adhesion instrument rather than a defensive coating.

### `sticky-pads` / `adhesive-secretion-surface`
- **What it is:** Localized skin patches (often on feet, tongue, or specialized limbs) that secrete a strong adhesive to bind or trap on contact.
- **Actions it enables:** snare, strike (as a grab-and-hold), ambush
- **Example creatures:** frog tongue, chameleon tongue (also muscular projection, see below), some salamanders, sticky-footed geckos (dry adhesion, van der Waals — a non-secretion variant worth a note), glowworm sticky snare threads
- **Notes:** Overlaps with `spinnerets`/silk-snare keys in end function (`snare`) but the mechanism is a direct adhesive coating rather than a woven thread structure. Gecko toe pads are technically dry/mechanical adhesion (not a secretion) — flag as an edge case that belongs here functionally but not mechanistically; curators may want a separate `adhesive-pads` (dry) vs `sticky-secretion` (wet) split.

### `projectile-tongue`
- **What it is:** A long, muscular, sticky-tipped tongue that fires outward at speed to strike and retrieve prey.
- **Actions it enables:** strike, hurl, snare, ambush
- **Example creatures:** chameleon, frog, toad, giant anteater (long sticky tongue, slower/no launch)
- **Notes:** Combines projection (hurl-like launch mechanism) with adhesion (snare) — a strong multi-action part. Some overlap with a "tongue" entry in a mouth-apparatus domain if one exists elsewhere; this entry is specifically the projectile/sticky-launch function.

## Thermal / Heat Emission

### `heat-vent`
- **What it is:** A specialized patch of skin, spine cluster, or dorsal structure that radiates or vents intense body heat, capable of scorching nearby contact or warning would-be attackers.
- **Actions it enables:** burst, ward, strike (contact-burn variant), terrorize
- **Example creatures:** no strong direct real-animal anchor (real animals that generate heat, like some bees vibrating to cook a hornet intruder, or "endothermic" tuna/billfish heater organs, are closer analogs); primarily a creature-fiction trope (dragon-adjacent, magma-beast anatomy)
- **Notes:** The heater organ used by billfish/tuna to warm their eyes/brain is a real but internal, non-weaponized anchor — cite loosely, this key leans fiction. Good instrument for "beam"/"burst" heat-based actions in a game with elemental typing (note: relevant to the Xalians fire-element context, though this harvest should stay generic per brief).

### `bombardier-heat-jet`
- **What it is:** See `chemical-jet-abdomen` above — the discharge is notably hot (near boiling) at ejection. Cross-referenced here since it doubles as a heat-emission mechanism, not just a chemical one.
- **Actions it enables:** spray, burst, ward, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** bombardier beetle
- **Notes:** Duplicate cross-reference only — same key as `chemical-jet-abdomen`, not a new candidate. Included so the heat-emission sweep doesn't miss it.

### `steam-vents`
- **What it is:** Body pores or dorsal vents that release pressurized hot vapor or steam, obscuring vision while also scalding at close range.
- **Actions it enables:** cloud, spray, burst, ward
- **Example creatures:** no real-animal anchor; a common creature-fiction trope for volcanic/geothermal-adapted beasts
- **Notes:** Functionally a hybrid of `ink-sac`-style clouding and heat-based harm; fiction-leaning, flag for curator judgment on inclusion given the brief's preference for grounded anchors where possible, though "broadly-known creature fiction" is explicitly in scope.

## Additional / Edge-Case Emission Parts

### `barbels`
- **What it is:** Whisker-like sensory-and-secretory projections near the mouth that can also exude attractant or irritant chemical cues.
- **Actions it enables:** ambush (as a lure), ward (mild secretion)
- **Example creatures:** catfish, some sturgeon
- **Notes:** Explicitly named in the brief's dual-role exception (senses AND acts) — include per that carve-out. Weak action fit overall; mostly a sensory part with a minor secretory bonus.

### `antennae` (chemical-signal variant)
- **What it is:** Paired sensory projections capable of both detecting and releasing chemical signals (pheromones) to coordinate group behavior or mark threats.
- **Actions it enables:** ward (alarm-signal release), spray (weak fit)
- **Example creatures:** ants, moths, many insects
- **Notes:** Also explicitly named in the brief's dual-role exception. Primarily a sensory part; the emission angle is secondary and mostly overlaps with `pheromone-gland`.

### `quill-launch`
- **What it is:** Loosely rooted, barbed quills that detach and fire a short distance when the body flexes sharply, embedding in an attacker.
- **Actions it enables:** hurl, strike, ward
- **Example creatures:** porcupine (popular myth says they "shoot" quills; biologically the quills detach on contact/flex rather than launching ballistically, but the ballistic version is a widespread creature-fiction embellishment)
- **Notes:** Flag the real-vs-legend distinction explicitly: real porcupines do not truly launch quills through the air, but the "shooting quills" trope is so widespread in fiction that it merits inclusion as a `hurl`-capable part; note the biological caveat for any lore-accuracy-conscious writer.

### `spine-burst` (sea urchin / pufferfish style)
- **What it is:** A body-wide array of spines that erect suddenly via hydraulic or muscular inflation, presenting an instant defensive burst of points.
- **Actions it enables:** burst, ward, strike
- **Example creatures:** pufferfish, sea urchin, hedgehog, echidna
- **Notes:** The erection/inflation action itself is the "emission" angle (a sudden defensive burst of body state change) even though nothing leaves the body — border case for inclusion under "emission" as defined by the brief (a discharge of threat/deterrent rather than of matter). Worth flagging to curators since it stretches the domain definition slightly.

### `bioacid-glands`
- **What it is:** Glands secreting a corrosive acid onto the skin surface or into a bite/spit stream, causing chemical burns on contact.
- **Actions it enables:** spray, strike, drain, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** some ant species (formic acid spray), certain caterpillars, fictional "acid blood" creature trope
- **Notes:** Overlaps significantly with `spitting-glands` and `regurgitation-crop`; kept separate here to flag the acid/corrosive angle specifically as distinct flavor from venom (paralytic/toxic) or musk (foul-smelling but non-burning).

### `light-lash`
- **What it is:** A whip-like or tail appendage tipped with a rhythmically flashing light organ, used for signaling or hypnotic distraction during ambush.
- **Actions it enables:** ambush, terrorize
- **Example creatures:** firefly squid (photophore-tipped arms), some deep-sea siphonophores
- **Notes:** A specific combination of `photophores` + a whip/lash limb; consider whether this should be folded into `flash-organ` or `photophores` rather than standing alone — flagged for curator merge decision.

### `spore-pods`
- **What it is:** Pod-like growths (often fungal or plant-adjacent in hybrid creature designs) that rupture to release a cloud of spores, causing disorientation, infection, or growth effects on contact.
- **Actions it enables:** cloud, spray, burst, ward
- **Example creatures:** puffball fungus (spore cloud on impact/rain), fungal-infected "zombie" insect tropes (cordyceps as the infecting agent rather than the host's own part, but the visual of an eruptive spore cloud is a common creature-fiction motif), fictional plant/fungus-hybrid beasts
- **Notes:** Strong fit for the Xalians "Plant" element in general but keep description domain-generic per brief; excellent grounded real-world anchor via puffball fungi even without an animal example. Good candidate for a `cloud`-primary part alongside ink sac and dust scales.

## Summary

This harvest identified **34 candidate parts** across nine functional clusters: venom-delivery hardware (fangs, stinger, spurs, venom-spines, venom-claws), spitting/projectile-fluid mechanisms (spitting-glands, water-jet-mouth, glue-spit-fangs, chemical-jet-abdomen, regurgitation-crop), ink/cloud producers (ink-sac, smoke-gland, dust-scales, spore-pods), silk/web apparatus (spinnerets, silk-mouth-glands, web-shooters, net-casting-forelimbs), light organs (photophores, lure-appendage, flash-organ, glow-slime, light-lash), electric organs (electric-organ, shock-quills), sonic apparatus (rattle, tymbal, drumming-membrane, stridulation-organ, wing-clap, throat-resonator), scent/chemical glands (musk-gland, spray-gland, pheromone-gland, slime-coat, sticky-pads, projectile-tongue, bioacid-glands), thermal emitters (heat-vent, steam-vents), and edge cases (barbels, antennae chemical-variant, quill-launch, spine-burst). Real-animal anchors dominate (skunk, bombardier beetle, archerfish, hagfish, spitting cobra, electric eel, cicada, rattlesnake, porcupine, puffball fungus, chameleon, net-casting spider), with a handful of fiction-leaning entries clearly flagged (smoke-gland, shock-quills, heat-vent, steam-vents, web-shooters) for curator judgment. Several likely-merge candidates are flagged inline: `smoke-gland` into `ink-sac`, `silk-mouth-glands` into `spinnerets`, `light-lash`/`flash-organ` into `photophores`, and `bombardier-heat-jet` is a cross-reference duplicate of `chemical-jet-abdomen` rather than a distinct entry (true unique count is 33). All entries were checked against the hard constraints: American English, no Earth-species proper names used as keys, no internal-organ-only entries (borderline cases like `throat-resonator` and `regurgitation-crop` are flagged explicitly), no sensory-only parts except the two dual-role exceptions named in the brief (barbels, antennae).
