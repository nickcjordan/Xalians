# Composition Brief — v5 (2026-09-01)

This is the single authoritative brief handed to element-composition agents. It CONSOLIDATES and SUPERSEDES v1 through v4; there are no amendments to hunt for. Read all of it before composing. If anything here conflicts with your instincts about what makes a "good" curated list, this brief wins.

## 1. What you are doing, and the philosophy

You are composing ability names for ONE element of a creature-collection game's name catalog. Names live in cells keyed by action (16 actions). At creature generation, a name is drawn from the cells matching the creature's element and ability.

The design goal is RELATIVELY EXHAUSTIVE cells. The fun of this game is semi-unique move names: it should be rare that two creatures share a move name. The Pokemon model of a small shared move list is explicitly rejected by the game's designer. Therefore:

- Wide cells are correct. Cells of 40, 60, 100+ names are expected and good.
- Near-duplicates are welcome (Osmosis and Osmotic Drain both stay; Void Slam and Gravity Slam and Mass Slam all stay).
- You are NOT a curator. Do not pick favorites, do not thin for elegance, do not aim for a "tight" list. The bar for inclusion is: logically sound + sounds fine out loud + violates no hard rule below. "Great vs mediocre" is not a distinction you are allowed to make.
- When in doubt, KEEP AND FLAG. A central auditor reviews your output and is the only subtraction step. A wrongly-kept name costs one audit line; a wrongly-cut name is lost forever.

## 2. Inputs

1. Your element's harvest file (named in your assignment): the exhaustive lexicon of element words. Sections 1-3 are your raw material. Any candidate-name lists in it are a starting point only; you go much wider.
2. ALL 14 action harvest files (harvest-action-*.md and harvest-actions-snare-crush-mend.md): each action's synonym vocabulary. These are a MANDATORY second lexicon, not reference material.
3. Any standing rulings named in your assignment (prior cuts and designer decisions). These are law; never reverse them.

## 3. The 16 actions

strike (any direct blow: hit, stab, gore, impale, peck, punch) · lash (flexible whipping) · crush (pressure, constriction, weight) · rake (edged drawing: slash, cut, tear, shred) · shove (force displacement) · drain (siphoning vitality/substance) · ambush (surprise attack from concealment) · beam (directed continuous emission) · hurl (thrown projectile) · spray (directed scatter of substance) · burst (sudden radial eruption) · cloud (lingering area haze) · snare (restraint, entrapment) · ward (protection, barrier) · mend (healing, restoration) · terrorize (fear, intimidation)

A name lives in exactly ONE cell, chosen by the word's DEFINITION, not vibes. A riptide drags, so Riptide is snare, never strike. If a name honestly fits two cells, place it in the definition-honest one and note the tension in your flags.

## 4. The sweep algorithm (mechanical, in this order, per action cell)

**Step 0 — productive-word list (once, before any cell).** List every noun/adjective/verb concept from your element harvest and classify each as PRODUCTIVE (usable as a name component) or NON-PRODUCTIVE with a stated reason. The ONLY valid non-productive reasons are grammatical/formal: it is a multi-word phrase that cannot compose to 2 words, it is a combining form (see rule 6.7), or it cannot grammatically front or follow a partner word. Register, tone, theme, and "abstractness" are NEVER reasons to exclude a word from the productive list. This list is Section 0 of your output.

**Step A — standalone words.** For this action, which productive words are evocative names ALONE (Breaker, Deluge, Undertow, Geyser, Implosion)? Place each in its definition-honest cell.

**Step B — element-internal compositions.** Element word + element word, natural compounds, inversions, real phenomena and real science terms in plain form (Water Hammer, Storm Surge, Surface Tension, Flash Flood, Event Horizon, Whip Tide). Sweep the productive list for these.

**Step C — the full synonym cross (this is the step prior runs failed).** Take EVERY productive element word and cross it against EVERY synonym in this action's harvest pool. Every resulting pair is a candidate name that must be individually judged: kept into the cell, or rejected PER-NAME in the disposition matrix (Section 9). No pair may be skipped, and no group of pairs may be judged in one stroke.

Repeat A-C for all 16 actions.

## 5. Name form rules

- 1-2 words. American English. No possessives, no articles, no hyphens.
- Names containing the literal action word are fine (Tidal Strike); they are the plain tier, not forbidden.
- Bare action synonyms alone (Smash, Slam, Impale) belong to the NEUTRAL pools, a separate deliverable. Never place them uncombined in element cells.

## 6. Hard rules (violations are the ONLY valid grounds for rejection)

1. **Definition honesty (element side):** the name's words must MEAN this element. A water-defined word can never serve another element however good the metaphor. If a word is wind-defined, fire-defined, etc., flag it as a dual/other-element candidate instead of using it.
2. **Definition honesty (action side):** a word serves only cells its definition supports. Drag-words (riptide, undertow) serve snare/ambush/shove, never strike/rake/crush/drain. Froth cannot strike. Containers (reservoir, jar) cannot crush. Sources (wellspring) mend, they do not strike. Currents push, they do not strike.
3. **No borrowed names** from existing creature/monster games or franchises (Hydro Pump, Deep Strike). Unsure = flag, keep it in the cell with the flag.
4. **No real-world weapons or military terms** (Depth Charge, Torpedo).
5. **No Earth fauna/flora proper references** (Python, Kelp, Coral); flag such words to their proper element or cut with the rule cited.
6. **No modern-tech idiom** (Firehose, Laser unless the element harvest sanctions it). Religious/ritual register IS allowed (Baptism, Ablution).
7. **No combining forms standalone or fronting** (no bare Hydro, no Aqua Ward class names; real-word compounds live).
8. **Say it out loud:** cut only if the specific name is innuendo, gibberish, unintentional comedy, or an impossible collocation. This is a PER-NAME test; you must write the name and the failure ("Void Suckle — comic").
9. **Anatomy-evoking names** (implying a body part: Vise, Talon, Jaw, Grip, Clamp, Stare, Roar) are KEPT and must carry instrument tags from this registry: jaws, fangs, beak, tusks, horns, antlers, trunk, tongue, crest, lure, claws, talons, fists, hooves, pincers, blades, spurs, wings, tail, stinger, rattle, coils, hide, shell, spines, tendrils, roots, pseudopods, spinnerets, light-organs, vents, core, antennae, body — or channels: mind, gaze, voice, breath, secretion, swarm, aura. Being anatomical is NEVER a reason to cut: creatures of every element have bodies, claws, jaws, and fists. A dark creature can Void Punch; tag it [fists].
10. **Signature register:** names grander than the 2-plain-word catalog tier (Point of No Return class) are flagged signature-register, listed in flags, not placed in cells. They are released to a signature pool, never lost.
11. **Canon compliance:** nothing implying time reversal, teleportation, creating life, permanent transformation, puppeting, or true invisibility. Element-specific canon constraints come in your assignment.
12. **Tautologies** (Splash Spray) are cut with the rule cited.

## 7. FORBIDDEN rejection reasons (the failure modes that forced this rewrite)

- "Doesn't fit the element's register/theme/framing/fantasy." BANNED. If the definitions compose honestly and it sounds fine, it goes in.
- "These synonyms are too anatomical / too physical / too feeding-specific for this element." BANNED. See rule 6.9.
- Any rejection applied to a CLASS of words in one sentence. Every rejection is one written-out name with one cited rule from Section 6.
- Reversing a standing ruling from your assignment (in either direction: re-cutting ratified keeps, or re-keeping ratified cuts).
- Silently dropping a lexicon word or a synonym pair from the accounting.

## 8. Flags (raise, don't resolve)

Dual-element candidates · borrowed-name suspicions · signature-register names · cross-cell definition tensions · anything you kept under "when in doubt, keep and flag." Flags go in a dedicated section; flagged names STAY in their cells (except signature-register, which moves to the flag list).

## 9. Output format (write to your assigned file)

1. **Section 0:** productive-word list with per-word classification (Step 0).
2. **Cells:** one section per action, every kept name, parenthetical definition notes where fit isn't obvious, instrument tags where rule 6.9 demands.
3. **Flags** (Section 8).
4. **Lexicon disposition table:** EVERY element word mapped to its fate (cells used in / flagged to element X / non-productive with grammatical reason). Nothing silently unaccounted.
5. **Synonym cross matrices:** per action, a matrix of EVERY productive element word (every row from Section 0 marked PRODUCTIVE — no sampling, no "representative subset") × that action's full synonym pool. Each intersection gets K (kept) or a rejection code: D=definition dishonest, G=gibberish/impossible collocation, C=comic/innuendo (say-aloud fail), T=tautology, W=weapon, B=borrowed (flagged), S=signature register (flagged), X=same name already kept in another cell (name the cell). BLANK CELLS ARE FORBIDDEN: a dash, an empty cell, or any "not composed / not attempted" marker is a silent skip and fails the run — every intersection carries K or a code, no third state exists. Codes are per-intersection judgments. If an entire row or column ends up with one uniform code, write out at least three of those names with their individual failures to prove the judgment was per-name.
6. **Counts per cell** and a one-paragraph summary.

## 10. Scale expectation

A compliant run produces cells several times wider than a curated list and a synonym matrix with hundreds of judged intersections. That is the job. An output whose matrices contain sentence-level class rejections, or whose cells look "tastefully tight," will be rejected and re-run.
