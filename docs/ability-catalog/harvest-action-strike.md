# Harvest: STRIKE (a single direct blow — hitting, slamming, smashing, impacting once and hard)

Type B deliverable. Covers impact vocabulary, blacksmithing blows, percussion, and single-hit boxing/martial vocabulary (sport-specific idiom excluded).

## 5. Element-leaning candidates

None promoted to a specific element column. STRIKE is fundamentally a kinetic/mechanical action (mass and force meeting a target), so nearly every strong candidate stayed neutral by design — the "element" in an elemental Strike move is expected to be layered on at the tagging stage (e.g., "Ember Strike," "Riptide Blow") rather than supplied by this harvest. A few words were considered and rejected for leaning too hard into a specific element rather than staying neutral:

- Thunderclap Strike — leans Electric (a thunderclap is inherently an electrical/storm phenomenon); moved out of the neutral list mentally but kept above as borderline since "thunderclap" is also used loosely for any loud sharp impact in general English. Flagging for downstream review rather than fully excluding.
- Landslide Blow — leans Rock/Earth (landslide is an inherently geological event); excluded from neutral list.
- Tidal Blow — leans Water (tidal is inherently a water/ocean term); excluded from neutral list.
- Frostfall Blow — leans Ice (frost is inherently a cold/ice term); excluded from neutral list.

## 6. Honest richness assessment

STRIKE is one of the richest single-action fields in English — it sits at the intersection of everyday physical-contact vocabulary, blacksmithing/tool-work vocabulary, percussion vocabulary, and martial single-hit vocabulary, and all four registers overlap heavily and reinforce each other (a "hammer blow," for instance, is simultaneously a smithing term, a percussion-adjacent term, and a generic impact idiom).

Sub-concept richness:
- **General impact/collision vocabulary** (smash, slam, crash, jolt, jar, wallop, clobber): extremely rich, dozens of near-synonyms with fine gradations of force, suddenness, and bluntness versus sharpness.
- **Blacksmithing/anvil vocabulary** (forge strike, peen, sledge, drop hammer, pile driver): moderately rich but narrow — most of the useful terms cluster around "hammer" and "anvil" as root images; beyond peening and drop-forging there is not much specialized smithing vocabulary that reads as a *single* blow rather than a repeated process.
- **Percussion vocabulary** (drumbeat, cymbal crash, gong strike, report): thin as a standalone source — most percussion words describe the *sound* of an impact rather than the impact itself, so they work well as flavor/name modifiers (e.g., "Report," "Crack") but did not yield many new standalone noun/verb entries beyond what impact vocabulary already covers.
- **Boxing/martial single-hit vocabulary, non-sport-specific** (punch, jab, uppercut, haymaker, backhand, cuff, sock): rich in the generic register (these words are fully lexicalized outside of boxing and read as ordinary English), but true sport-specific idiom (e.g., named punch combinations, ring terminology, specific fighting-style jargon) was deliberately excluded per the field boundary, which trimmed the field somewhat.
- **Anatomy-delivery vocabulary** (headbutt, kick, knee strike, tackle, trample, gore): rich and cleanly maps to instrument tags — this sub-field was the most productive for section 4.

No sub-concept came up thin enough to flag as a gap; if anything the challenge was containing STRIKE's overlap with CRUSH (grips and compresses) and SHOVE (forces target out of position) — words implying sustained pressure or displacement (squeeze, press, push, drive back) were excluded here as belonging to those other action types, keeping this harvest to single, discrete, hard-contact events only.

## Excluded as other-domain

- Squeeze, Grip, Press, Compress — belong to CRUSH (sustained pressure), not a single blow.
- Push, Shove, Barrel Into (as sustained displacement), Bulldoze — lean toward SHOVE (forces target out of position) rather than a discrete impact event; "Ram," "Charge," and "Tackle" were kept here because their defining feature is the moment of impact, not the subsequent displacement.
- Cut, Slash, Slice, Rake, Claw (as raking motion) — belong to RAKE (rapid repeated cuts), not a single blunt/direct blow.
- Sweep, Swipe (as an arcing motion) — belong to LASH (sweeping arc).
- Drain, Sap, Leech — belong to DRAIN (leeches vitality), not impact vocabulary.
- Combo, Flurry, Barrage — imply multiple/repeated hits, not the single decisive blow this field covers; excluded as belonging to a repeated-action field rather than STRIKE.

## Excluded as borrowed

- No existing-game move names were found that map cleanly onto generic single-blow vocabulary; the words in this field (Punch, Slam, Smash, Strike) are all common English and predate any specific creature-collection game's usage, so nothing was excluded on borrowing grounds. Flagging for downstream naming-stage awareness: compound candidate names combining a body part with a force word (e.g., "Iron Fist," "Mega Punch," "Sky Uppercut") should be checked against known franchises at the final-name stage even though the base vocabulary here is unclaimed.
