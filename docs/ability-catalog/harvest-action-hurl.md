# Action Harvest: HURL

**Field:** thrown or launched mass that lands on the target — throwing, lobbing, launching, volleying projectiles of matter.
**Boundary notes from brief:** catapult is fine (siege engine, pre-gunpowder); missile is borderline (kept, flagged); bullet and other gunpowder/firearm vocabulary excluded; no Earth-fauna references; no borrowed game move names.

## 6. Honest richness assessment

The HURL field is extremely rich. English has deep, layered vocabulary here across three registers that all genuinely fit: (1) plain-throw verbs (throw, toss, pitch, fling, lob, heave, hurl — all common, all distinct in connotation of force/arc/effort), (2) siege/artillery vocabulary (catapult, trebuchet, ballista, mangonel, onager, sling, salvo, barrage, bombard, fusillade) which is exactly the pre-gunpowder register the brief wants, and (3) weather-metaphor vocabulary for many-object barrages (hail, rain, shower, deluge, storm) which reads naturally as "many small hurled things landing." The weakest sub-concept is genuinely novel single-word verbs beyond the well-known set — most of the long tail is compounds or niche/dialectal terms (peg, chuck, skim, skip) that sit at register boundaries. Anatomy-mechanism words are moderately rich (sling, catapult-limb, maw/gullet launch, coiled-body torque, prehensile tail/tentacle) giving good instrument-tag variety. The gunpowder-modern boundary is the main hazard: ordnance, shrapnel, fragmentation, cannonade, grapeshot, buckshot, round, and shot put all sit close to or over the modern-military/sports line and are flagged or excluded rather than included outright. "Missile" and "ordnance" are kept but flagged as borderline per the brief's explicit callout. Overall: no shortage of names; the exhaustive list comfortably exceeds what curation will need, and the element-neutral section (3) alone yields 60+ usable candidates before touching element-leaning ones.
