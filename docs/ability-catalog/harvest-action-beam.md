## 6. Honest richness assessment

The BEAM field is very rich in nouns and verbs — English has deep, overlapping vocabulary for narrow directed energy across optics (ray, beam, glare, gleam), weaponry (lance, spear, pike, skewer, needle), and craft/technical registers (bore, drill, etch, trace, vector, trajectory). This is one of the best-supplied action types precisely because "beam" and "lance" sit at the intersection of light-physics language and edged-weapon language, both of which are old and idiomatically dense in English.

Sub-concept richness, honestly assessed:
- **Ray/beam-as-light** (beam, ray, streak, glare, gleam, flare): very rich, easy neutral candidates.
- **Lance/pierce-at-range** (lance, spear, skewer, impale, transfix): very rich, strong anatomy-implying set (horns, tusks, spines), good for physical/organic beam-attackers as opposed to pure energy-emitters.
- **Focused-emission mechanism verbs** (focus, channel, converge, project, discharge): rich and useful for describing the mechanism rather than the visual, good for ability flavor text as well as names.
- **Precision/fine-line vocabulary** (needle, pin, thread, wire, hairline): moderately rich; several of these read a little delicate/small-scale for a combat ability and may skew toward "control" or "status" abilities rather than raw damage — worth flagging downstream since a "Needle Ray" reads different in tone from "Piercing Lance."
- **Weakest sub-concept**: pure single-word neutral nouns that don't need a modifier (i.e., "Beam" or "Ray" alone as a full ability name) are thin — nearly everything usable is a two-word collocation (adjective/verb + Beam/Ray/Lance). This is expected given the one-or-two-word constraint and is not a field weakness, just a naming-pattern observation: BEAM abilities will almost always need a qualifier word to feel like a real ability name rather than a bare noun.
- Nautical/archaic register (broadside, salvo, tracer) contributed few genuinely neutral candidates — most either lean military-technical (feels off-register for a creature-collection game) or are borderline non-linear (salvo, broadside skew toward multi-target/areal, which is arguably ambush/spray territory, not beam). Kept them in nouns for completeness but did not promote them to candidate names.
- No true other-domain or borrowed-name exclusions were needed for pure vocabulary; a few borderline items (Sand Lance as a real fish name, Prick Ray as a weak/informal collocation) are flagged inline above rather than hard-excluded, since neither is a game-move borrow and neither is definitionally other-domain — they're marked for downstream curator judgment instead.
