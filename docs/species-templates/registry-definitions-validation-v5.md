# Registry definitions draft v5: blind validation

Fresh pass, 2026-09-02. The validator did not write the draft and did not read any earlier validation report. Method: read the draft, the ratified design doc, the data-structure doc, the ability grammar, skill sections 2/4/5, RULINGS.md, and the validator script; then read all 29 species descriptions in `species.json` and opened all 29 PNGs in `art/`; then assigned bodyPlan, composition, covering, diet, and lifespan for every species using only the description, the art, and the draft's own rules, recording whether the rule settled it.

**Verdict: NOT READY.** One CONTRADICTS (the exoskeleton double-count, F1) and two SPLITs that a one-sentence rule can close but has not (Scalatto covering, Neph composition). Everything else is CLEAN or a stated FALLBACK. The draft is close: three added sentences and one deleted clause should carry it.

## Findings

| # | Severity | Enum / value | Problem | Proposed wording |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | CONTRADICTS | composition secondary vs covering `chitin` | The two sections double-count the same part. Composition says "A rocky exoskeleton over slime is `slime` primary, `mineral` secondary," but the covering selection rule says "A rigid casing grown or worn over any body is `chitin` when the body grows it as an exoskeleton," and the composition disambiguation says "a chitin crab is `flesh` (chitin is its covering)" — a covering is explicitly not a composition secondary. Under the draft as written Bioflim's one rocky exoskeleton is both `covering: chitin` and `composition.secondary: mineral`, while Graviclaw's one chitin shell is only a covering. Two agents will split on whether an exoskeleton is a covering, a secondary, or both. This also propagates into lifespan cut 3, which uses "armored covering" as its bump trigger. | Replace the composition example with: "An exoskeleton, carapace, shell, or armor plate is a covering, never a secondary, whatever it is made of; the covering value carries the material fact. A rocky exoskeleton over slime is `slime` alone with `covering: chitin`. Declare a secondary only for a structural substance that is not the outer surface: a limb, a core, horns, or a skeleton." Then say once, under covering, that the covering key and the composition keys never describe the same part. |
| F2 | AMBIGUOUS | covering `scales` vs `chitin` | The draft has no tiebreak when a description names two coverings in one phrase. Scalatto is "shielded by a scaly exoskeleton": `scales` is named outright, and `exoskeleton` triggers the chitin rule. Both are defensible from the same six words. The same shape recurs latently for any future "plated hide" or "horny scales" phrasing. | Add to the covering selection rule: "When one phrase names two surfaces, the rigid one wins: `chitin` or `plating` over `scales`, `hide`, `fur`, or `feathers`. A scaly exoskeleton is `chitin`; scaled skin that is not called a casing, shell, or exoskeleton is `scales`." |
| F3 | AMBIGUOUS | composition `flesh` vs `gas` | The draft gives no rule for a living animal body that a source sentence states is made of a gas. Neph is "colossal hydrogen jellyfish" that is "composed almost entirely hydrogen," but it has a bell, eyes, and trailing feeding tentacles, so it reads as flesh under `flesh`'s "living animal tissue" and as gas under the literal composition sentence. The `gas` disambiguation ("a fog creature that is fog at rest") does not reach a jellyfish. | Add to the composition selection rule: "A gas an animal body is filled with or buoyed by is not its composition; `gas` is for a body whose substance is the vapor itself, with no tissue to hold. A hydrogen-filled jellyfish is `flesh`." |
| F4 | AMBIGUOUS | covering `bare` fallback vs the art | The `bare` fallback fires "when neither source names or shows a surface," but 27 of the 29 art files are solid black silhouettes in which a fur or feather read comes from an outline treatment (a shaggy ruff, a spiked back-edge, a plumed tail), not from a rendered surface. Venemist, Voltish, Codazzo, Tizzie, Figzy, and Akinza all sit on this line: an agent that reads a ragged outline as fur and an agent that requires an explicit surface both follow the draft. This is the single largest source of avoidable disagreement across the 29. | Add to the covering selection rule: "A silhouette outline counts as showing a surface only when the outline itself is drawn as that surface across the trunk: a shaggy, spiked, or tufted trunk edge is `fur`, and a trunk edge drawn as separated quills or vanes is `feathers`. A smooth trunk outline with fur or feather detail only on the head, tail, or crest is not the trunk covering; take `bare`." |
| F5 | AMBIGUOUS | bodyPlan `amorphous` vs the fixed-outline clause | "A body whose outline is fixed is never `amorphous` however soft it is" collides with Bioflim, which is a slime with a rigid rocky exoskeleton and therefore a fixed outline, yet has no legs, fins, wings, drift, or units and so fits no other key. The priority list bottoms out at `biped`, which is worse. | Add to the fixed-outline clause: "A body that fits no other key because it has no bearing limbs is `amorphous` even when a casing gives it an outline; the fixed-outline clause only stops a limbed or legged body from being called amorphous." |
| F6 | NIT | lifespan cut 3, `fleeting` | The `fleeting` sub-clause reads "below 20 kg are `fleeting` when the body is a swarm, a conjured-unit body, or a flier the source shows living in numbers." Tetrahive's `swarm` plan comes from its conjured familiars while the mass gauge is its 12 kg central body, so the rule prices a central controller as a mayfly. It is decidable, so not a split, but the outcome reads wrong against the description. | Narrow to: "...when the units themselves are the body being weighed (a true swarm of its own flesh), or a flier the source shows living in numbers. A central body that conjures units is weighed as itself." |
| F7 | NIT | diet `energy-feeder` fallback for plant bodies | The fallback "energy-feeder for a plant body" fires without any source sentence, which sits uneasily beside the same paragraph's rule that "A creature that stores or channels an energy but is not shown feeding on it is not an energy-feeder." It is unambiguous in practice (only Kosanos hits it) but the two sentences read as contradicting one another. | Reword the fallback as: "...for a plant body take `energy-feeder`, because a plant body must draw something in and the sources have not said what; this is the one place the field is authored without a sentence." |
| F8 | NIT | bodyPlan selection rule length | The bodyPlan selection rule is a single 300-word paragraph carrying at least seven distinct sub-rules (pose vs rest, description-names-legs, art-decides, forelimb test, description-overrides-art, priority order, burrow). It is correct and it did settle every one of the 29, but an implementing agent has to hold all of it at once. | Split into a numbered list of the same rules in the same order. No wording change needed. |

Checked and clean: the value sets for corporeality (2), composition (8), bodyPlan (9), covering (9), diet (6), communication (5), phases (3), special senses (6), lifespan (6), chirality (2), descriptionStatus (2), capabilities (7), actions (16), anatomy (34) and channels (7) match `validate-template.js` and the ratified design doc exactly, with no value added, dropped, or renamed. The draft's claims about what the script does are accurate in every case I checked: `traits.phasing` failing on a non-corporeal body without phasing at 100, `corporeality.composition` warning outside spectral/energy/gas, `traits.armored` warning on shell/plating/chitin/crystal, `flight.means` warning without wings/floating/swarm/non-corporeal, `manipulation.means` failing above 40 without grasping anatomy or telekinetic, `anatomy.surface` failing on shell plus hide, and `composition.same` failing on a duplicated secondary. The draft correctly notes that the `shell` expectation for armored coverings is an agent check and not a script check. No definition contradicts a RULINGS.md entry: Tetrahive's `swarm` plan, the conjured-familiar rule, and the one-surface-key rule are all carried faithfully, and the Graviclaw ruling (pincers/shell/body, amphibious) is consistent with the draft's chitin and multiped reads. American English throughout, no em-dashes, no game-mechanics vocabulary in the definitions, no invented lore. The rooted-bodies note correctly reports the declined `sessile` value rather than adding it.

Notes on three rows.

**Scalatto lifespan** is downstream of the F2 split and is therefore contingent, not independent: `chitin` gives standard bumped to long, `scales` gives standard. I have counted the covering as the split and the lifespan as clean-conditional rather than double-counting one disagreement.

**Chromocat diet** is where I disagree with my own first read and the rule held. "Harassing their opponents" is explicitly named as not-feeding by the selection rule, and no sentence shows it consuming anything, so the flesh fallback applies and the value is `omnivore`, not `carnivore`. Recording it as F, not C. A second agent following the rule lands in the same place; an agent reading "wicked speed and ionic blades" as predator flavor lands on carnivore. The rule is doing real work here, which is a point in the draft's favor.

**Smokat and Tizzie bodyPlan** both come out `biped` from the forelimb test on rearing art of animals the descriptions call feline and (implicitly) small mammalian. The rule settles them, so they are not splits, but they are the two rows most likely to make Nick wince, and F8's restructuring would at least make the reasoning legible in a walkthrough.

Fallback rate is 29 percent, concentrated almost entirely in covering (18 of 29) and diet (19 of 29). That is expected and acceptable: most of the 29 legacy descriptions are one- or two-sentence stubs that say nothing about surface or feeding, and the draft's `bare` and `omnivore`/`energy-feeder` defaults exist precisely for that. The concern is not the rate but F4: roughly seven of those 18 covering fallbacks are one silhouette-reading judgment away from `fur`, and the draft does not say how much outline detail counts as showing a surface.

## Blocking items

1. **F1 (CONTRADICTS)** — the exoskeleton is simultaneously a covering and a composition secondary. Must be resolved before any agent runs Bioflim.
2. **F2 (SPLIT)** — Scalatto covering, `scales` vs `chitin`.
3. **F3 (SPLIT)** — Neph composition, `flesh` vs `gas`.

F4 is not formally a split under the draft's wording, but it is the highest-volume risk in the document and should be closed in the same pass.
