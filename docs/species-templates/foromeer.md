# Foromeer (metal, Veridium) migration walkthrough

## Art reading

The render shows a single tall body standing upright on two long digitigrade legs, weight fully on the feet, with a narrow deer-like or antelope-like head carried high on a slim neck. The muzzle tapers to a small closed mouth; one pale eye is visible; the ears sweep back along the skull; from the crown rise two long, thin, backswept filaments that fork near their tips. The torso is a solid unbroken silhouette, broad at the shoulders and narrow at the waist, with no drawn surface texture. Both forelimbs are raised and held clear of the ground; each ends in a plated gauntlet-like hand and forearm assembly drawn with panel highlights, and out of each of those hands rises a very long, tapering, helically threaded shaft angled up and outward, unmistakably a screw or drill bit. The two shafts are longer than the creature is tall. The hind limbs and feet are drawn as segmented, jointed, plated mechanical assemblies with highlight panels at thigh, shin, and foot; the head, neck, and trunk are not drawn as plated. There is no tail and there are no wings. One body, one central mind, two bearing legs, two raised arms terminating in drills.

## Notes

2026-09-08, `insulated` line call. Veridium's hazard list carries discharge arcs, and the hazard rule of iteration three names `insulated` for continuous lightning or static discharge. I left the key off. Discharge arcs on Veridium come from industrial machinery running without regard to proximity, which is an intermittent injury hazard from equipment rather than the ambient charge the key was added for on Zolton and Stonera, and the report's own output priorities name plated chassis, metallurgical manipulation and self-repair rather than insulation. Reopen it if Nick reads any arc hazard as ambient charge, in which case the entry belongs at 100 and the expected count goes to 5.70.

Per operating rule 7, one rule strained against this species: the composition secondary. The registry states that a casing of any material is always a covering and never a composition secondary, and it also states that a second substance forming a limb, a core, or a skeleton is a secondary. The Foromeer is the case where those two sentences point opposite ways, because its metal is both the casing on its limbs and the limbs themselves, drill shafts included. I declared `metal` as the secondary on the limb clause and `plating` as the covering on the casing clause, which records the same visible material in two fields. The smallest fix would be one added sentence in the composition section: when the only named second substance is the same material as the covering, declare the secondary only if a source names a part made of it that is not the casing. For the Foromeer the drill horns are exactly that part, so the call would stand, but it would stand on a stated rule instead of on my reading.
