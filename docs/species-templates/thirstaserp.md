# Thirstaserp: migration walkthrough

## Art reading

The artwork shows a single limbless serpent drawn in flat black silhouette, coiled on the ground with the front third of its body reared vertically. The head is a viper's wedge, mouth open wide with two long curved fangs in the upper jaw and a forked tongue extended; a single eye is visible in profile. Behind the head a broad fan-shaped hood flares out and is drawn with radiating internal ribs, the classic cobra spread. The neck and body carry heavy transverse banding, drawn as overlapping crosswise plates along the underside and flank, which reads as a scaled belly. The body loops twice on the ground in flat coils, and a second, thinner tail section rises separately on the left side of the frame ending in a splayed rosette of narrow pointed blades arranged like petals. Behind that rosette the artist has drawn a large field of concentric ripple rings radiating outward, which reads as the vibration coming off that tail organ rather than as any solid part of the body. There are no limbs of any kind, no wings, and no fins. One body, one head, one tail.

### Physiology

- `corporeality: corporeal`: a body that bites and is bitten. art shows a solid outlined body.
- `composition.primary: flesh`, no secondary: living animal tissue with venom and a bite; no second structural substance is named or shown. species: "attack with a unique venom".
- `bodyPlan: serpentine`: art shows a long limbless body that loops on the ground with no limbs at all, and species calls it "cobra-like". Serpentine is the ratified key for `a long body that moves by undulation, with no limbs or with limbs too small to bear it`. The reared pose in the art is a pose, not a stance at rest; the selection rule takes the body plan, and the body has nothing else to be borne on.
- `covering: scales`: art draws the underside and flank as heavy overlapping transverse bands, which is the registry's `overlapping plates of skin or horn, reptile or fish style, flexible as a whole`, and the outline itself is drawn as that surface. Not `hide`, because no source shows a thick, leathery, or rugged surface, and the drawn bands are plates rather than a rugged skin.
- `anatomy: jaws, fangs, tail, rattle`. `jaws` from species: "when bitten", plus art (a wide-open biting mechanism). `fangs` from art (two long curved piercing teeth) and species: "a unique venom", which is delivered by the bite and so lives in the piercing teeth. `tail` from species: "the rattles on their tails" and art (a separate rising tail section). `rattle` from species, same quote, and it is exactly the registry's `sound-warning tail organ`; the art's rosette with radiating ripple rings is the organ and its output. No `hide` and no `shell`: the one-surface rule allows at most one, the body has no armored aspect, and nothing in either source shows the skin used defensively, so the minimum honest set omits it. No `coils`: the art shows the body looped, but neither source shows it wrapping or crushing anything, and its named kill is the bite. That omission is listed under Authored fields as a deliberate exclusion.
- `size: heightCm [190, 250], weightKg [95, 145]`: the legacy gauge is 218 cm and 121 kg, used only as a relative reading against the other 29. A large cobra-like serpent whose `height` on a reared serpent is best read as reared length gets a band around that gauge; the weight band is a realistic mass for a heavy-bodied constrictor-scale serpent at that length. Authored absolutes.
- `lifespan: standard`: cut 1 and 2 do not apply (flesh). Cut 3 by mass, midpoint 120 kg, which falls in `20 kg up to and including 200 kg are standard`. The adjustment does not fire: no source calls it cold, slow, or long-lived, and `scales` is not an armored covering. Cut 4 does not fire: no source sentence says the environment shortens its life.
- `genome.chirality: rolled`: the default; nothing about a flesh body with venom chemistry warrants `achiral`.
- `diet: carnivore`: the selection rule takes `a sentence showing the creature consuming, dissolving, paralyzing to consume, or draining a victim`. species: "a unique venom that drains the victim’s water when bitten, causing them to suffer and die from extreme dehydration" is a draining-a-victim sentence and it uses the word victim, so `carnivore` is settled rather than authored.
- `communication: ["vibration"]`: the registry's `signals by tremor, drumming, or percussion through ground, water, or air` matches species: "emit a subsonic vibration from the rattles on their tails". This is the one hard call. The lure-and-mesmerize effect is aimed at a target, and the registry says an effect aimed at a target is an ability, not communication. But the vibration itself is a broadcast tremor the body makes, and a body that produces a subsonic tremor produces one whether a target is there or not, so `vibration` is listed as the outward signaling channel and the mesmerizing is carried separately by the `hypnotic` trait and by the `rattle` instrument. No `vocal`: no source shows a call or cry, and the vibration comes from the tail rather than a throat.
- `breathes: ["gas"]`, `ambientMedia: ["gas"]`: a flesh serpent on a desert world with a cloudless sky. planet: "Endessa’s twin suns, which once kept its deep oceans warm enough to sustain life, now blazed through a cloudless sky, scorching the earth". `breathes` is a subset of `ambientMedia`. Solid is never a medium, so hiding beneath sand is carried in `burrow`, not here.
- `temperatureC: {min: 10, max: 55}`: the current planet source gives Endessa a habitable band of -10 to 55 C against extremes of -60 to 195 C and a mean of 60 C, and this band sits inside it, topping out at the survivable ceiling. The sub-band is narrowed from the history and the body: planet describes "arid, sweltering heat" and a cloudless sky over "vast seas of endless rolling dunes", so this is a creature that works at the hot end, while the floor is lifted off the habitable minimum because a burrower in sun-loaded sand is never operating at the cold extreme. See the Script denials section: my first band was 5 to 70, written against the older data-block rule.

`tail` is in anatomy but not in the instrument list, because the working organ on it is the `rattle` and the list caps at three; the bite and the rattle are what the description actually fights with.

## Script denials

Run 1 raised 2 FAIL. Both came from a single sentence in the thin-combo section, and both were legitimate.

- `md.quote`: I had quoted the sand catalog file's own header note about its mend cell inside double quotes. The script correctly refused it: the quotation convention reserves double quotes for verbatim text from species.json, the planet history, or a registry definition in the skill, and the ability catalog is none of those. Changed to an unquoted restatement in my own words. The original was not better; the script caught a real convention breach.
- `md.emdash`: the same quoted fragment carried the catalog file's em-dash. Removed with the quotation. Legitimate denial, and the rule is right: the walkthrough must be free of em-dashes regardless of where the character came from, which is exactly why quoting an out-of-scope file was the wrong move.

Run 2 raised 2 FAIL.

- `temperature.habitable`: my band was 10 to 55 written as 5 to 70, denied because it fell outside Endessa's habitable band of -10 to 55 C. This is a legitimate denial and worth flagging as a skill drift, per operating rule 7. The skill's section 5.5 instructs that the temperature band must lie inside the planet's `data` block range from planets.json, which for Endessa is -50 to 173 C, and 5 to 70 sits well inside that. The script checks against a different and newer source: `planetRecords.json` `environment.habitableBandC`, rebuilt 2026-09-02, which narrows Endessa to -10 to 55 C and marks the extremes as not survivable. The script is right and the skill is stale. Changed to 10 to 55. My original was not better on the merits, since a sustained-activity band should be a survivable band, but the skill sent me to the wrong source. The smallest fix: update section 5.5 of the skill to name `planetRecords.json` `environment.habitableBandC` as the range the band must lie inside, and retire the `data` block sentence, which now describes a legacy file the script keeps only for the quotation corpus.
- `md.emdash`: one em-dash of my own, in the `senses.special` line, that survived the run 1 edit. Replaced with a colon. Legitimate denial, my error.

Run 3 raised 0 FAIL and 4 WARN, each answered here.

- `traits.expected` (expected count 4.95 above 3.5): confirmed deliberate. The pool is long because this species is described by a chain of four separate mechanisms in one sentence, each of which lands on a different trait: it hides (`stealthy`), it mesmerizes (`hypnotic`), it envenoms (`toxic`), and it lives buried on a scorched world (`resistant`). Three of those are at 60 or above on their own evidence, and `toxic` is body-demanded at 100, so the sum is high without any padding. I would rather carry a high expected count that every entry can defend than shave percents to hit a target number.
- `instruments.predicate.source` (`secretion` predicate is source-text): the sentence is species: "attack with a unique venom that drains the victim’s water when bitten". Venom is an emitted substance, which is the predicate exactly.
- `signature.description.elementkey` and `enc.definition.elementkey` (the word `water`): in both places `water` is ordinary English for the liquid the venom takes out of a body, never a type label. The signature line reads 'takes the water out of what it has bitten' and the encyclopedia line reads 'drains the water from what it bites'. Both trace to species: "drains the victim’s water when bitten". There is no substitute that is not a circumlocution, and the sources use the plain word.

One rule strained, recorded per operating rule 7 (levers, not stone), with no denial attached because the rule did not force a different value, only a lossy one:

- The `communication` registry says that `an effect aimed at a target (hypnotizing it, terrorizing it) is an ability, not communication, even when it uses light or sound`. This species' only outward emission is the subsonic vibration, and that emission is aimed at a target, so a strict reading empties `communication` to `[]` and the species becomes mute despite the fact that the single most distinctive thing its body does is broadcast a tremor. I listed `vibration` because the tremor is a broadcast the body makes rather than a contact effect, and the targeting is carried separately by the `hypnotic` trait and the `rattle` instrument. The smallest fix: let the `communication` rule distinguish a broadcast emission that happens to have an effect on whoever is in range from an effect delivered at a chosen target, so a rattling, drumming, or displaying body is not classified mute merely because its signal also works as a lure.

## Open questions for Nick

Should the Thirstaserp declare an affinity-odds override toward `water`? The baseline 75/25 is what the template carries now, and I did not override it, because an override wants a lore reason about the species and the sources give a mechanism rather than a stated affinity. But the case is unusually strong for this one creature: its venom takes water specifically, it lives on the one world in the setting whose oceans were deliberately destroyed, and `water` is on the sand graph. My recommendation is to leave the baseline as written and revisit it if the games ever want a creature whose secondary reads as thematically inevitable, because raising the odds here would make water-affinity Thirstaserps the common case and quietly turn a sand species into a water one.
