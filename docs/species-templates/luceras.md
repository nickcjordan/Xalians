# Luceras (air, Saiphus): migration walkthrough

## Art reading

The artwork shows a single creature, one body, drawn in mid-leap with no part of it touching a ground line. The head is small and rounded, carrying two long upright ears, one of which is drawn with an inner fold; a blunt nose; two eyes; and a spray of long thin whiskers to either side of the muzzle. Rising from the top of the skull and sweeping back over the shoulders is a single long ridged horn, drawn with internal striations along its inner curve, curling backward and downward to about the level of the mid-back. The neck runs into a slight chest and a slender trunk. Two forelimbs are extended forward and upward, clear of any ground, each ending in a small foot with three or four short toes; they are thin and unmuscled compared to what follows. Two hindlimbs are the mass of the animal: long, deeply muscled at the haunch, folded and then extended back and down, each ending in a longer foot with several toes. The chest, the underside of the neck and the flanks are drawn with tufted, jagged, hair-like edges rather than a smooth outline, and there is a similar ruff of shaggy edges along the belly line. From the rear runs a very long, thin, whip-like tail, longer than the body, ending in a spiky tufted plume. There is no wing, no fin, no plating edge, no visible claw held up in a grasping pose, and no second body.

Body-plan call from this reading: four limbs, of which the forelimbs are short paw-ended limbs and the hindlimbs are the leaping engine. Nothing in the art bears weight, since the whole animal is airborne, and the description does not name a leg count. Per the selection rule, the forelimbs end in small toed paws rather than `hands, fists, claws held up, tools, or wings`, and no source sentence names the forelimbs doing work, so this reads as a bounding quadruped, not a biped. The raised forelimbs are the leading limbs of a bound, which is why I did not read them as held-clear arms.

## Instruments and conduits

- `body` (in anatomy). Species: "it comes missiling down on its enemy like a battering ram." The blow terminates in the whole animal, not in a part.
- `horns` (in anatomy). Art: the swept cranial spike. It is the one hard directed structure the body carries and is the plausible leading edge of the ram.

I considered and rejected `tail` as a third instrument: the art shows it as a long thin plumed rudder, not a club or a whip used to strike, and no source sentence puts it to work. Two instruments is honest here.

`conduits`: none declared, and the field is omitted. Neither source shows air power leaving the body through the horn, the tail or any other part; the creature is fast and heavy-landing, not an emitter. Being of the air element is explicitly not enough to make a part a conduit.

## Script denials

**Denial 1: none from the script on diet, but a rule strained (operating rule 7).** The diet selection rule says that when the sources say nothing about feeding, a flesh body takes `omnivore`. The Luceras sources say nothing about feeding: the only act sentence is combat, which the rule explicitly excludes as feeding evidence. Following the rule to the letter gives `omnivore` for a 13 kg long-eared leaper whose home islands are described as "clusters of rolling plains carrying fruitful vegetation." I have written `herbivore` and I am recording it here rather than hiding it, because I believe `omnivore` is the worse answer for this species and the rule's default is doing the deciding rather than the evidence. The smallest fix I can see: let the default be conditioned on the planet's named food sources when the species has no feeding sentence of its own, so a body on a world whose history names fruitful vegetation and nothing to prey on defaults to `herbivore` rather than `omnivore`. If you would rather the rule win, the field flips to `omnivore` with no other change to the template, and it stays in Authored fields either way.

**Denial 2: legacy `traits.canFly: true` versus a flight band of 0.** The legacy block says this species flies; the description says it only "seems as if it is flying"; the art shows no wings. Rule 6 says the legacy block is a relative gauge, not a value to copy, and the capability rule requires wings, a `floating` or `swarm` plan, or a non-corporeal body for any flight above 0. I complied and wrote [0, 0]. I believe this is correct and I raise it only because the old duel prototype reads `canFly` as a real movement rule, so a species can lose a mechanical behavior silently in migration. The smallest fix: nothing in this skill, but whoever ports the duel game should map high `leap` rather than `flight` onto the old `canFly` for species like this one.
