Design system, version 4

Character

Three ways to give the chrome tier a character of its own without touching the palette, the square corners or the low depth. Each column applies one device to the same five things: a catalog tile, the three ranks of key, a record card, and an input. Hover and press them.

Direction a

Registration

The Generator prints a record. Every plate and card wears crop marks at its corners, the way a printed specimen sheet does, and every record carries a number set in the data face. The chamfer stays the signature; the marks are its small cousins. Nothing changes color.

From the chamfer we already own. Corner marks, printed numbers, a faint layout grid on the room.

Direction b

Screen print

The art is ink on paper. Element washes carry a halftone screen in a deeper tone of their own hue, so the black silhouettes sit on a printed field rather than a flat fill. Secondary keys and card heads take a lighter screen on hover. The room gains a faint paper grain.

From the dotted yellow button. Halftone on washes, dot screen on hover, grain on the room.

Direction c

Plate offset

Surfaces are cut plates stacked on the hull. Anything you can press casts a hard offset shadow in the surface below it, lifts a step on hover, and drops flat when pressed. The primary key's shadow is the darker viable; a tile's is its own element, dimmed.

From the white lifted button. Hard offset shadows, lift on hover, press flat.

A, site-wide

  • Corner marks on every plate, card and secondary key; the chamfer stays on glass and the primary.
  • A record number in the data face on every record and tile (the registry already assigns ids).
  • The faint layout grid on level-0 surfaces only, never behind prose.
  • Risk: it is quiet. It gives order more than character, and could read as a wireframe if the marks are too many.

B, site-wide

  • Every element wash is a halftone: tiles, plates, chips, the world tiles, the duel piece badges. The silhouettes stay solid black on a printed field.
  • Level-0 surfaces get the paper grain; secondary keys and card heads take the light screen on hover.
  • Fits the art: bold black silhouettes are a screen-print idiom, and a field guide is what the encyclopedia is.
  • Risk: the screen must stay coarse enough to read as ink and fine enough not to moire on phones; one size, tested at 390.

C, site-wide

  • Hard offset shadows on everything pressable: tiles, keys, toggles, tabs. Static cards get a smaller one.
  • Lift on hover, press flat, all inside the 120 ms rule.
  • Risk: it is the current neo-brutalist trend and reads as a template look; it also contradicts section 5 (depth is low, one float shadow) and would need that lever moved.

Recommendation

B, screen print, as the site's character. It answers the actual complaint (flat gray) with texture instead of chrome, it makes the creature art look intentional rather than pasted on a swatch, and it fits the world: the Generator prints records, the encyclopedia is a field guide, the Scrambler Token is an encrypted genome pressed into a chip. It costs nothing in the contract: colors, corners and depth stay as ratified.

Take the record number from A as a free companion: a data-face id on tiles and records is information, not decoration, and the registry already has the ids.

Leave C. The keycap and lifted-plate buttons are pleasant on their own but they are a trend, they need the depth lever moved, and the site would look like a template that used them.
