# Xalians platform lifecycle, generation, and economy

Status: current platform design of record, reconciled with Nick's explicit decisions through 2026-09-23. This is design direction, not a claim that the platform has been implemented. It supersedes older economy, progression, trading, release, and access proposals wherever they conflict. The ratified creature genesis schema and lore remain authoritative for their own subjects. Read `generation-economy-open-threads.md` first for a compact handoff and the unresolved work, then this document for the full policy.

## 1. Authority and working rules

- **Agreed direction** means Nick explicitly accepted the rule in discussion. It is the setting to design against, not an irreversible law. Concrete evidence from implementation or play may reopen it.
- **Provisional** means the shape is accepted but depends on a first integrated game's actual needs or on playtest evidence.
- **Deferred** means the possibility is preserved but should not be built or treated as a launch promise.
- **Implemented** refers only to code verified in the current repository. A documented future rule does not make today's API enforce it.
- The creature's immutable genesis and published lore follow `xalian-creature-system-redesign.md`, `xalian-creature-data-structure.md`, and the current generation release contract. For platform lifecycle and economy, this document wins over older platform-vision and generation proposals. If a future implementation exposes a real conflict, report it with the smallest proposed change rather than silently adapting either side.
- Keep game-specific catalogs, numerical thresholds, exact prices, and restoration missions out of this document until real games and content support them. Do not turn examples into registry keys.
- A new agent must not treat the six questions in the 2026-09-11 handoff as still open. Their current dispositions are in the companion brief.

## 2. The platform boundary

Xalians is a shared creature identity platform with multiple games as consumers. A creature has several deliberately different layers:

1. **Immutable genesis:** the generated individual, including species, physiology, capabilities, abilities, appearance, seed, origin, and generation release. Existing genesis never changes to accommodate a later game or balance patch.
2. **Portable creature life state:** completed, currently active game-specific development and append-only factual history. Completed development stays active when the creature changes owners, but is a bounded sidegrade, not a growing power total.
3. **Owner relationship state:** an active or ready development intention, partial checklist progress, nicknames, notes, favorites, squad presets, and relationship information. This does not transfer with custody.
4. **Platform account state:** credits, Scrambler Tokens, qualification progress, Generator attunement, restoration projects, and account-owned purchases.

Games derive their own mechanics from genesis and, if that game has a development system, its own active development entries in a versioned session snapshot. Games never interpret raw history as a mechanics API. An integrated game may ignore development entirely. There is no universal creature level, XP total, cross-game power rating, or automatic cohesion bonus. A newly generated or newly acquired creature must be viable before development. A game's roster or battle budget prices everything mechanically relevant within that game.

No game result can destroy a creature or involuntarily release, transfer, permanently injure, or otherwise weaken it across the platform. Permanent custody transitions are separate explicit platform transactions.

## 3. Scrambler Tokens and qualification

A Scrambler Token is a unique, account-bound pre-creature seed, not ordinary currency or a coupon purchased with credits. Its unique ID, securely generated hidden seed or commitment, issuance provenance, and issued/consumed status are recorded. Tokens cannot be traded, gifted, sold, surrendered, or converted into credits. They do not expire, and an account may hold any number without pausing qualification or being forced to redeem them. A grouped count may be shown for convenience while the individual token records remain authoritative. Only one standard token type is in the initial policy. New worlds, species pools, and qualification pacing must account for demand from legitimately saved tokens rather than confiscating, expiring, or capping previously earned tokens.

The token's entropy is fixed when it is issued, but it is **not** tied to a planet, species pool, or generation-policy version. Genesis is resolved only when the owner uses the token at an attuned planet's Generator. The generation transaction records the then-current pinned generation release and all inputs needed to reproduce the resulting creature. The player cannot inspect alternate planet outcomes, reroll, or retry after seeing the creature. Credit amount and credit provenance never affect the roll. A defect in a generation release requires an explicit audited remedy, not a silent rewrite of existing creatures.

Tokens are earned through visible, deterministic qualification, never random drops from ordinary completion. The guided first qualification may be completed through any offered opening expedition. **The first such world completed on the account issues one ordinary token and attunes that world together.** Completing another opening expedition later attunes its world but cannot issue another first-qualification token. This is an account-level one-time entitlement, not a token per planet or per easy first achievement. The first completion's effort should be broadly comparable to later renewable cycles so new-account creation is not an efficient token source. Individual first-time achievements may award modest account-bound credits, badges, cosmetics, lore, or other access; they do not separately grant an opening world attunement. Do not require a player trade or irreversible creature release for that first token.

Thereafter, renewable qualification cycles issue one token each. One active cycle receives contribution at a time. Completing it atomically records the completed cycle, issues its token, and opens a new empty active cycle. The player does not need to claim the token manually, start the next cycle, or spend previously earned tokens before progressing again. The receipt that completes one cycle applies only to that cycle; excess contribution does not overflow into the new cycle or count twice. Lifetime history remains after a cycle completes and its counters reset. Incomplete progress does not expire merely because a day or season ends. The base effort should remain broadly comparable across cycles rather than increasing forever. Registered accomplishment and activity families have contribution ceilings and breadth requirements, so one trivial repeat cannot fill a cycle. The exact accomplishments, thresholds, and rewards are deferred until reward-bearing games exist.

**Launch feasibility revises an earlier blanket rule:** a single game may be the only integrated, free reward-bearing game at first. Every active cycle must therefore be completable through the eligible free activities actually available. Variety can come from that game's naturally emitted facts and scenarios. Do not force mechanics or artificial proofs into a game merely to manufacture cross-game breadth. Additional games later provide alternative routes and exploration incentives, not mandatory prerequisites to earn any token.

A verified activity may advance a planet campaign, the player's one active token cycle, credits, and a participating creature's active game-specific intention under their separate policies. A credit balance or ledger event is never itself a qualification input: holding, earning, purchasing, or spending credits does not advance a token cycle. When one verified activity legitimately produces both credits and qualification contribution, each result is evaluated independently from the factual receipt rather than converting one reward into the other. An activity cannot count in two token cycles at once. Easy one-time site achievements do not directly issue extra tokens. Paid arcade activity may make a small, real, clearly disclosed, capped contribution to qualification because of its verified play, not because credits were spent, but must never be the fastest, exclusive, or unbounded path.

## 4. Starter allocation and the playable-roster floor

New accounts receive fully owned starter creatures so they can play before earning a token. They are authentic, developable creatures with `starter-allocation` provenance, not loaners. Every account is offered the same five starter species, with one individual candidate from each species, and manually chooses exactly three. Every starter candidate's species homeworld must have an account-attuned Generator from the beginning, including the two candidates the player may not choose. The individual rolls may differ across accounts within a versioned, capped common-band profile, but each account's five exact previews are stable and non-refreshable once presented. Before confirmation, the player can inspect every candidate's meaningful player-facing immutable genesis information, appearance, species lore, and plain-language capabilities. No meaningful choice trait is hidden, and the platform presents no score, ranking, recommended trio, automatic selection, or default selection that could imply a preferred combination. Internal security material such as generation entropy need not be exposed. The player may revise the selection before explicit confirmation; confirmation atomically creates only the three chosen owned records and permanently closes the other two previews. Unchosen previews were never owned creatures, cannot later be claimed or transferred, and create no custody or release history. Their species remain normally obtainable through the already-attuned homeworld Generators. Every permitted choice of three must support the introductory experience. Cards, comparison tools, expanded details, and other presentation patterns remain interface decisions to iterate later. The fixed species slate supports predictable onboarding balance and a disjoint pool at every offered first-expedition world, while individual variation and world choice keep openings from being identical. Exact species identities, starting worlds, and planet spread are confirmed against the first integrated game's needs.

Starter generation excludes rare finishes, mutations, exceptional trait combinations, and premium or strategically exceptional rolls by validating the complete outcome, not merely capping each independent trait. Starters are viable, not deliberately handicapped. They may remain useful after full generation opens, but cannot begin with scarce or premium qualities. They retain normal capacity for development and normal lifecycle rights.

The account has a protected **playable, player-owned roster floor of three**. This is a platform custody rule, not a requirement that every game use three creatures. A game may require one creature, three creatures, or another roster size according to its own design. A voluntary trade, release, or NPC surrender cannot leave the account below the platform floor, evaluated against the final atomic transaction state. A one-for-one starter trade may be allowed immediately because the roster count stays intact. There is no token-per-starter exit counter. A formal trade hold still counts as owned until execution. There is no platform-wide maximum collection size and no paid core creature-storage capacity.

**Opening journey direction (agreed 2026-09-17 through 2026-09-22):** After the starter choice, the first major earned payoff is qualifying for a Scrambler Token and generating the player's first token-born creature. The player can pursue an expedition to any of three offered surveyed, not-yet-attuned worlds, pause it, and work on another without erasing the first world's progress. The first expedition they complete earns the one-time guided token and attunes its world. Later completion of another opening expedition attunes that world without another guided token. Every offered destination's eligible species pool must exclude the species of all five starter candidates, so every permitted trio and destination offer a new-species option under ordinary uniform selection. The token is not bound to the completed world: players may instead save it or choose any other attuned world, accepting its disclosed chance of a familiar species. This preserves normal odds without a hidden first-roll exception or duplicate-protection rule. The account's first successful token-based generation has no credit activation fee, ensuring that the earned token can be used without a purchase or a required win. The opening must not require a trade or irreversible release. Exact game steps, thresholds, starter species, destination identities, and later generation fee remain to be designed against real content.

## 5. Generation and planet discovery

The Mercurius Machine issues seeds; a planet-bound Generator expresses one seed as a creature. At redemption, the owner chooses any planet to which their account is attuned and presents one earned token. The account's first successful token-based generation has a zero credit activation fee; subsequent standard generations charge one flat, disclosed credit fee. The standard fee does not vary by account, selected planet, eligible species pool, token balance, creature collection, or current demand. Its exact amount remains a later economy-tuning decision and should ordinarily be affordable by the time a player earns a token. The transaction atomically consumes the token, deducts any applicable credits, generates the creature from the fixed seed, selected planet, and pinned current generation release, registers ownership, and records use of the one-time fee waiver. On failure, it consumes nothing, including the waiver. The waiver belongs to the account, is not a separate balance or item, is not tied to the guided token, and remains available until the first successful token-based generation. A future Generator mode may receive a separately reviewed fee, but no alternate mode or price structure is part of the initial policy.

The chosen planet determines primary element and the eligible native species pool. Initial species selection is uniform among that pool, with no per-species rarity weight or stamped rarity tier. Individual genesis rolls remain random under the published generation policy. No named-species purchase, paid reroll, personalized duplicate tilt, hidden pity adjustment based on holdings, or paid improvement to rarity or mechanical potential is part of the initial system. Duplicates remain distinct individuals with trade, NPC surrender, and restoration uses, not fusion fodder. Generated signatures exist at birth; development must not become a straight vertical signature unlock.

Planet attunement is a permanent, account-bound discovery campaign. The encyclopedia and all canonical worlds may be visible from the beginning, while Generator access begins with a small viable subset that includes every starter candidate's species homeworld and expands through clear, achievable chapters. This initial attunement does not issue a token. The exact starting world count and identities are open. The first additional world unlocks when the player completes any one of three offered opening expeditions. These three charted worlds are not yet attuned to the account. Every candidate offers the disjoint starter-species pool described above, with distinct lore, elements, and creature possibilities rather than a stronger or weaker reward. Other opening expeditions remain available to finish afterward, with their saved progress and their own world-attunement reward but no repeat of the guided token. The opening choice should not force a game to change its mechanics or invent destination-specific proofs just to equalize difficulty. Credits cannot buy initial attunement. Required introductory game play is free and completion-based rather than win-based, with a guided mode and a permanent fallback if a game is retired. Campaign branching should keep one disliked game from blocking nearly the entire galaxy. Every canonical planet needs a permanent guaranteed access route; limited events may add an alternate or faster route, never the only route. Later planets provide different species, elements, stories, and strategies, not a vertical power ladder.

The opening expeditions use the same structure, objectives, qualification facts, first-token opportunity, and intended effort for every offered destination. Planet-specific authored pieces are swapped into that structure so the chosen world feels present. These may include the briefing, map and environmental presentation, sites, inhabitants, and other content a chosen game already supports. Such pieces may create ordinary tactical variation, but must not create a superior reward route or a destination-specific proof requirement. If a game cannot naturally support a proposed swap, use presentation and narrative context rather than changing its mechanics to satisfy the campaign. The exact content slots and parity checks wait for the first integrated game.

Each opening expedition keeps its own account progress. Only the actively selected expedition advances from a session, using the selection captured when that economy-eligible session begins; switching destinations between sessions saves the old progress and never copies or retroactively redirects it. Completion is recorded once per world. The first completion atomically marks the guided token entitlement claimed while issuing that token and attuning the completed world. Later completions attune their worlds only. Eligible verified play after the first completion can also contribute to the ordinary renewable token cycle under its normal policy, including play used to finish another expedition. Earlier saved expedition progress is not retroactively credited to that cycle, and the later expedition completion itself neither grants nor shortcuts another token.

Some attunement chapters may offer a **restoration milestone as an alternative route** to game exploration. A project on an already reachable world can help open a neighboring world, avoiding a requirement to release a creature from a locked world first. Restoration is not mandatory for every planet. A creature acquired by trade from an unattuned planet may still be owned and played, but it does not grant Generator attunement. Unused tokens can be saved for later worlds or new content because they have no planet or policy lock at issuance.

Before redemption, the UI should disclose the selected planet, current eligible pool, species-selection rule, material odds and trait ranges, fee, and irreversibility. Exact starting planets, chapter order, mission requirements, and generation pricing await the first integrated content. Higher token grades, Generator modes, site-specific generation, and account-dependent species weighting are deferred, not launch assumptions.

## 6. Ordinary credits and paid access

Ordinary fungible currency needs a distinct final name; **credits** is a placeholder. The player sees one balance, while an auditable ledger distinguishes earned, purchased, promotional, support-adjusted, refunded, and reversed entries. The ledger records reason, source receipt, policy version, and idempotency. Credits remain account-bound, have no cash-out or peer transfer, and ordinarily do not expire. Purchased and earned credits have the same visible spending power unless future legal or storefront review requires otherwise.

Verified completed games may award credits on a mode-specific policy. Completed losses or failures can earn them, with eligible wins generally earning more. Abandoned or unresolved games earn nothing. There is no account-wide daily cap after which all activities stop awarding credits. A particular reward source may instead use clearly disclosed diminishing returns or a source-specific ceiling when evidence shows a farming risk. A player may continue earning from other eligible sources under their own policies. Participation cadence for a puzzle, event, or other piece of content is a separate design decision and does not create a hidden global currency cap. NPC surrender pays a modest fixed credit amount initially; it does not scale with rarity, finish, development, or history, and it does not grant Scrambler progress. There is no automatic standing penalty initially. Credit scarcity may diminish for veteran players. Do not add upkeep, repair, storage, or other artificial recurring taxes to force spending.

Credits can pay the activation fee for generations after the account's first successful token-based generation, including when purchased, because the token remains the earned generation entitlement. Credits cannot be converted into tokens or qualification contribution through balances, earnings, purchases, or spending. Do not issue special opening credits or an earmarked credit balance merely to simulate the first-generation waiver. Future credit sinks may include cosmetics, wearables, optional content, saved configurations, showcase layouts, and genuinely useful convenience. Paid access may allow a player to keep enjoying a preferred arcade mode and receive modest, transparent, bounded progress from verified play. It must never buy token qualification directly, bypass attunement, increase reward rates, improve a creature, generate more creatures without earned tokens, or create an exclusive or best-efficiency progression route. Consumable does not imply a gameplay boost. If repetition reduces credit awards, show the applicable window and next-session outcome before play or payment. Scrambler qualification needs harder family ceilings, not merely a short time throttle. Match duration or time spent in a status never measures development.

Purchases should give an honestly described benefit, not manufacture frustration or imply a larger advantage than exists. Account-owned paid wearables and cosmetics unequip when a creature changes custody. Purchased content access, decorations, and presentation entitlements stay with the account. Player trades initially include creatures only, and NPCs do not repurchase credit-bought cosmetics for credits. Permanent paid cosmetic treatments that travel with a creature are deferred because they could become indirect purchased trade value. Chargebacks or fraud handling must not silently rewrite a traded creature's history.

## 7. Game-specific creature development

Mechanical development is initially **specific to the game that defines it**. A game may have a versioned catalog of mutually exclusive, bounded sidegrades. A creature starts viable with no development in each game. Initially there is at most one active specialization for that game per creature. No platform-wide practice or environmental conditioning slot is active. In particular, environmental tolerance in immutable genesis is not edited by play. If a future real game demonstrates the need for another axis, add it deliberately to that game's policy. Other games do not reinterpret its specialty. Restoration initially does not inspect development or gameplay history.

Only verified receipts from the defining game may advance its development. The owner declares one active development intention for the creature before play, including the target and the active entry it would replace. Requirements are a small registered checklist, not generic XP. A receipt is evaluated against the intention in the session's starting snapshot and cannot be applied retroactively to a new target. Applicable participating creatures may each advance their own intentions from one match. Absent or benched creatures do not. Completed losses may contribute when the target's policy says so; abandoned sessions never do. Raw elapsed play time is not a criterion, though a game-authored semantic choice such as short versus extended expedition may be a registered fact.

The current specialty stays active during retraining. When the checklist is complete, the new entry becomes **ready to activate**, not automatic. While ready, its target and replaced slot are fixed, it gains no more progress, and the creature cannot begin another intention. Outside an active authoritative session or locked roster, the owner explicitly activates the replacement or discards it. Both actions are final. There is no expiration clock, bank of alternate loadouts, free switch-back, credit fee, or reusable partial progress. Changing or canceling an unfinished intention discards that partial checklist. Reacquiring an archived entry requires developing it again under current requirements.

Completed active development and archived history travel with the creature through trade. Active entries, not archived entries, affect games. A new session uses the current game balance for an entry, not its historical numeric patch, while old sessions remain reproducible under their pinned rules. An entry's conceptual meaning cannot silently change; a materially different specialty needs a new key. Partial intentions and ready-but-uncommitted entries are owner relationship state and are discarded only when custody actually changes. Listing a creature does not discard them. The interface warns before a transaction that would discard unfinished work.

Squad cohesion is not a universal score or power bonus. Factual co-play may be retained in relationship history. A concrete game may later interpret its own coordination choices, but no platform mechanic is required before that game needs it.

## 8. Game receipts and explicit integration cutover

Local game prototypes may iterate freely. **Do not extract, restructure, instrument, or enforce a deterministic platform engine during gameplay development unless Nick explicitly requests the cutover.** The phases are local prototype, gameplay stabilization, explicit requested cutover, then platform integration. A game not intended to award permanent platform value may stay local or nondeterministic.

At cutover, a reward-bearing game must be deterministically replayable: the same initial versioned snapshot, rules and content release, server seed, and ordered player commands produce the same final state and facts. The server, not the client, verifies the submitted command transcript and derives results. Determinism does not itself prove an authentic player, so account-integrity controls remain separate. Game code emits a factual receipt and never declares its own credit, token, or development payout.

Receipts use a small global envelope and closed, versioned, namespaced per-game fact manifests. Each fact key defines its nature, scope, value type, exact emission rule, and version. Published meanings are append-only; changed meanings get new keys. The fact registry defines what happened, while policy maps facts to account, credit, or development consequences. Keep three principal version surfaces: `receiptVersion`, `gameRulesVersion` (including fact semantics), and `developmentPolicyVersion` (including reward mappings). Ordered receipts, prior state, and pinned versions must reproduce the same transition. Use receipt IDs, idempotency, transactional updates, and a policy-owned economic circuit breaker for compromised game versions.

Before the player commits to an integrated economy-bearing session, the platform presents the applicable reward policy in player-facing terms. The preview identifies every system the session can advance, any entry cost and refund rule, the completion, win, loss, or other relevant outcome conditions, the corresponding amounts or ranges, and current source-specific ceilings or diminishing returns. When the exact result depends on play, the preview shows the conditional schedule rather than promising an outcome. The policy shown is the policy pinned when the session begins and cannot change underneath that session. After resolution, the platform shows the verified outcome and a breakdown of the credits, qualification contribution, campaign progress, and creature-development progress actually applied, including a clear reason for any zero or reduced award. Security controls and hidden generation entropy need not be exposed. Local prototypes and non-economy modes have no such requirement before explicit cutover.

An account may have only one active platform game session at a time across integrated games and modes, including sessions that award no economy progress. A paused or disconnected session remains active and resumable; disconnection alone does not abandon it or forfeit its potential rewards. Another tab or device may resume that same session, but cannot create a parallel one. Starting a different game requires finishing or explicitly abandoning the active session. Canceling before a paid session starts charges no credits. Once the paid session starts, its disclosed entry fee is charged and a voluntary abandonment does not refund it. If a verified platform failure prevents completion, the platform voids the session, releases its slot, and refunds any entry credits charged through the account ledger. A transient failure that leaves the session resumable does not itself trigger a refund. The same entry charge cannot be refunded twice. This is a standing platform rule, not merely a launch restriction. It does not impose session management on a game's local prototype before Nick requests integration.

Resolved completed sessions may earn rewards whether won or lost. Voluntarily abandoned, platform-voided, and unresolved sessions earn no permanent rewards or development. A creature in an unresolved economy-eligible session cannot execute a trade, release, or NPC surrender until it resolves, is explicitly abandoned, or is voided for a verified platform failure. A formal trade may be drafted but not executed meanwhile. An abandoned or voided session rejects later results. Local non-economy play creates no platform custody lock. A compromised game version may continue in clearly labeled non-economy mode while its receipts are quarantined and reviewed.

## 9. Trades, custody, and creature presentation

Player trades are bilateral creature-for-creature exchanges, including bundles. Each side offers at least one creature. No direct gifts, credits, tokens, wearables, auctions, price-denominated marketplace, or ordinary trade fee initially. NPC and platform transactions may pay or charge credits under policy. Trade never creates credits. Every completed trade is atomic and final, with permanent custody events; infrastructure or account-compromise remediation is a separate audited correction, not silent history rewriting.

A public listing is nonbinding and does not lock a creature. A formal offer places only creatures voluntarily offered by their current owners on a trade hold. An unsolicited offer cannot lock someone else's creature. While held, a creature remains owned and viewable, with its completed development unchanged, but cannot enter economy-eligible play, advance or activate development, enter another formal offer, release, or surrender. Pending owner-specific intentions are preserved if the offer is canceled and discarded only if custody changes. Owners can cancel before execution; offers should expire and stranded holds must be recoverable. A creature must resolve or abandon relevant sessions before entering the hold. This hold stabilizes transferable state without requiring a broad monotonic creature-revision scheme.

A player may send a nonbinding trade proposal for a currently owned creature discovered on another player's public profile, whether or not that creature has a public listing. A proposal may describe the creature-for-creature exchange the sender wants, but it holds no creature on either side, promises no availability, and cannot itself execute a trade. The recipient may ignore or decline it. Proceeding requires each owner to voluntarily place their own offered creatures into a formal offer, followed by both parties' approval of the exact bundle under the ordinary trade rules. Proposal delivery and abuse controls can be designed with the trade interface; they do not change custody or create an unsolicited hold.

Both players review the exact bundle, active game-specific development, nontransferring state, cosmetics, and post-trade roster counts before final approval. Changing the bundle clears approvals. Execution rechecks ownership, hold, session eligibility, and both roster floors atomically. After transfer, each received creature is immediately usable. Genesis, active completed development, archived development history, and verified provenance travel. Partial or ready intentions, owner-specific relationships, nicknames, biographies, private notes, tags, favorites, squad presets, and account-owned cosmetics do not.

The platform keeps an authoritative internal ledger, an owner view, and a privacy-aware public provenance projection. Verified creature history survives ownership change, but former account identifiers, private matches, and owner-written claims are not automatically public. A previous keeper may opt into attribution; removing attribution does not erase the custody fact. Naming and renaming are free. Creature history is not a permanent public dossier of every person who owned it.

Profiles are public in the initial design. The entire currently player-owned creature collection is browsable so other players can discover potential trade partners. The owner cannot selectively hide individual owned creatures. A featured or active squad may appear first, but it is only a presentation choice, not the extent of the visible collection. The player's released-creature reserve is also publicly browsable as a separate gallery; those residents are no longer owned or tradable. NPC-surrendered creatures are neither in the owned collection nor the reserve gallery. Public creature views show appropriate creature information and privacy-filtered verified provenance, not private notes, owner relationship state, unfinished development intentions, hidden generation entropy, or private match details. Neither a public collection nor a featured squad implies that a creature is listed for trade or can be taken through an unsolicited hold. There is no launch private-profile switch or per-creature visibility control. A future account-wide private option may protect the whole profile while allowing carefully scoped friend or explicit trade discovery; its access exceptions and messaging behavior require later design rather than premature scaffolding.

For both currently owned creatures and released reserve residents, the public creature page shows meaningful player-facing immutable genesis and completed, currently active game-specific specialties. This lets visitors evaluate a potential trade and understand a released creature's developed identity without exposing owner-specific work in progress. Archived specialties remain preserved in the creature's history and available in the owner view while player-owned, but are not part of the initial public page because they no longer affect play. A public archive may be considered later if it proves valuable as creature history, clearly distinguished from current capabilities. Unfinished or ready-but-uncommitted intentions, partial checklist progress, private notes, hidden generation entropy, and private match details remain nonpublic. A nonbinding proposal does not freeze these public details; before a trade executes, both owners review the creature's then-current state in the formal offer held under the ordinary trade rules. Exact public history presentation beyond the privacy-aware provenance projection remains a later interface decision.

## 10. Release, NPC surrender, and restoration

Leaving player circulation is a lifecycle and custody transition, never routine database erasure. Player trade transfers playable ownership. Release moves a creature to a planet's reserve and advances the player's long-term restoration effort. NPC surrender is the simple sell-off route for a player who wants to remove a creature from their collection and receive credits. Before confirmation, the player sees the fixed payout and that the transfer is final. Surrender atomically transfers the creature to NPC custody and pays the credits; it preserves genesis and history but offers no ordinary buyback, player resale, or return to play in the initial policy. The creature does not enter the personal reserve. Additional NPC buyers, special appearances, and variable offers are possible later but are not part of the initial payout policy. There is no player-facing creature-destruction action or platform destruction state; NPC surrender serves the discard use case. Neither release nor NPC surrender returns a Scrambler Token directly.

Restoration is personal effort inside one shared, authored galaxy. The same fourteen canonical planets, lore, and story chapters exist for everyone; player actions do not automatically solve global canon. Each player has a personal reserve and finishable authored restoration projects on canonical worlds, not duplicate private planets or one catch-all personal world. Released creatures normally go to their species homeworld, initially the same as Generator origin. Their genesis and history remain preserved and browsable. Release permanently ends playable ownership, so a reserve resident cannot be reclaimed for games, trades, or NPC surrender and does not count toward the protected roster floor. The release transaction respects that floor before it executes.

A release always makes the creature a resident of its homeworld reserve. Before confirmation, the player sees that release is permanent, along with currently open project requirements that the creature could satisfy, and may select **one** permanent project contribution or choose reserve-only release. A release cannot retroactively supply a new permanent contribution to a later project. Projects use broadly satisfiable composition predicates and multiple valid solutions, initially grounded in immutable genesis and project context, not a generic rarity or development total. A project cannot demand a rare premium outcome as its only route. Completing a project leaves a lasting reserve, story, or presentation change. Later authored chapters can request new releases without undoing earlier work; avoid a meaningless infinite progress meter.

Some completed projects may be alternate routes to planet attunement. Such a route must be achievable from already accessible worlds, while a permanent free game-exploration route remains available. Individual releases do not grant repeatable Scrambler progress. A one-time restoration milestone may contribute a capped amount to a qualification policy if later designed and disclosed. No release fee is agreed; do not assume a credit toll. NPC surrender remains the immediate credit route; it creates no restoration progress.

**Provisional extension, not MVP:** reserve residents may staff a limited number of reversible active posts. One creature occupies at most one post; moving it loses the previous active benefit and never returns it to playable ownership. Assignments never make old residents count as fresh permanent contributions, and initially confer no direct competitive squad power. Defer the layer if it becomes chore-like, has one obvious configuration, or demands too much interface/content complexity. Limited seasonal projects may be added later through data-driven manifests, without making them the only permanent path or granting exclusive competitive power.

## 11. Ownership, privacy, and account integrity

Ordinary ownership and long-term preservation have no paid storage cap. Games may limit active roster size and restoration may limit active assignments. Purchased saved configurations or showcase layouts are presentation conveniences, not core storage. An account closure does not fabricate a release, surrender, reward, or redistribution: remaining creatures enter an administrative dormant-custody state with preserved genesis and history and anonymized public attribution. Suspension freezes an account without rewriting custody. Exact legal retention, purchase, and refund handling require later review.

The economy should not rely on proving that one human owns exactly one account. The starter floor, reciprocal trades, account-bound credits and tokens, full-effort first qualification, family contribution limits, and verified game receipts reduce farming incentives. Account policy should prohibit using secondary accounts to evade limits or automate play, with graduated review rather than automatic punishment of households or friends. Real suspicious trade or gameplay patterns may inform later integrity controls. Do not require invasive identity verification for the base economy without evidence.

## 12. Current implementation and migration boundary

As of this reconciliation, the repository does **not** implement this economy or lifecycle. Signed-in `POST /xalians` currently generates an owned creature for free and may accept an exact species; `DELETE /xalians/{id}` physically removes the registry item. The anonymous showroom is unowned. The existing Arcade endpoint verifies a submitted win and awards numeric credits under a daily cap, with an automatic credit-to-token conversion. That account-wide cap and conversion, the free signed-in generation route, the current release deletion, and the exact-species parameter are implementation facts, **not** approved final economy rules. The current games and local modes are not thereby required to adopt this policy during gameplay iteration.

This is permission for a clean launch, **not an instruction to delete them now** and not permission to remove unrelated content, source files, or lore.
