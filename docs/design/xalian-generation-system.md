# Xalians platform lifecycle, generation, and economy

Status: current platform design of record, reconciled with Nick's explicit decisions through 2026-09-17. This is design direction, not a claim that the platform has been implemented. It supersedes older economy, progression, trading, release, and access proposals wherever they conflict. The ratified creature genesis schema and lore remain authoritative for their own subjects. Read `generation-economy-open-threads.md` first for a compact handoff and the unresolved work, then this document for the full policy.

## 1. Authority and working rules

- **Agreed direction** means Nick explicitly accepted the rule in discussion. It is the setting to design against, not an irreversible law. Concrete evidence from implementation or play may reopen it.
- **Provisional** means the shape is accepted but depends on a first integrated game's actual needs or on playtest evidence.
- **Deferred** means the possibility is preserved but should not be built or treated as a launch promise.
- **Implemented** refers only to code verified in the current repository. A documented future rule does not make today's API enforce it.
- The creature's immutable genesis and published lore follow `xalian-creature-system-redesign.md`, `xalian-creature-data-structure.md`, and the current generation release contract. For platform lifecycle and economy, this document wins over older platform-vision and generation proposals. If a future implementation exposes a real conflict, report it with the smallest proposed change rather than silently adapting either side.
- Keep game-specific catalogs, numerical thresholds, exact prices, and restoration missions out of this document until real games and content support them. Do not turn examples into registry keys.
- A new agent must not treat the six questions in the 2026-09-11 handoff as still open. Their current dispositions are in the companion brief.

## 2. The platform boundary

Xalians is a shared creature identity platform with multiple games as consumers. A creature has several deliberately different layers:

1. **Immutable genesis:** the generated individual, including species, physiology, capabilities, abilities, appearance, seed, origin, and generation release. Existing genesis never changes to accommodate a later game or balance patch.
2. **Portable creature life state:** completed, currently active game-specific development and append-only factual history. Completed development stays active when the creature changes owners, but is a bounded sidegrade, not a growing power total.
3. **Owner relationship state:** an active or ready development intention, partial checklist progress, nicknames, notes, favorites, squad presets, and relationship information. This does not transfer with custody.
4. **Platform account state:** credits, Scrambler Tokens, qualification progress, Generator attunement, restoration projects, and account-owned purchases.

Games derive their own mechanics from genesis and, if that game has a development system, its own active development entries in a versioned session snapshot. Games never interpret raw history as a mechanics API. An integrated game may ignore development entirely. There is no universal creature level, XP total, cross-game power rating, or automatic cohesion bonus. A newly generated or newly acquired creature must be viable before development. A game's roster or battle budget prices everything mechanically relevant within that game.

An ordinary game result cannot involuntarily destroy, release, transfer, permanently injure, or otherwise weaken a creature across the platform. Permanent custody transitions are separate explicit platform transactions.

## 3. Scrambler Tokens and qualification

A Scrambler Token is a unique, account-bound pre-creature seed, not ordinary currency or a coupon purchased with credits. Its unique ID, securely generated hidden seed or commitment, issuance provenance, and issued/consumed status are recorded. Tokens cannot be traded, gifted, sold, surrendered, or converted into credits. They do not expire. A grouped count may be shown for convenience. Only one standard token type is in the initial policy.

The token's entropy is fixed when it is issued, but it is **not** tied to a planet, species pool, or generation-policy version. Genesis is resolved only when the owner uses the token at an attuned planet's Generator. The generation transaction records the then-current pinned generation release and all inputs needed to reproduce the resulting creature. The player cannot inspect alternate planet outcomes, reroll, or retry after seeing the creature. Credit amount and credit provenance never affect the roll. A defect in a generation release requires an explicit audited remedy, not a silent rewrite of existing creatures.

Tokens are earned through visible, deterministic qualification, never random drops from ordinary completion. A one-time guided first qualification encourages exploration and issues **one ordinary token and attunes the first additional world together on completion**, not a token for each easy first achievement. Its total effort should be broadly comparable to later cycles so new-account creation is not an efficient token source. Individual first-time achievements may award modest account-bound credits, badges, cosmetics, lore, or other access; they do not separately grant that first world attunement. Do not require a player trade or irreversible creature release for that first token.

Thereafter, renewable qualification cycles issue one token each. One active cycle receives contribution at a time; lifetime history remains after a cycle completes and its counters reset. Incomplete progress does not expire merely because a day or season ends. The base effort should remain broadly comparable across cycles rather than increasing forever. Registered accomplishment and activity families have contribution ceilings and breadth requirements, so one trivial repeat cannot fill a cycle. The exact accomplishments, thresholds, and rewards are deferred until reward-bearing games exist.

**Launch feasibility revises an earlier blanket rule:** a single game may be the only integrated, free reward-bearing game at first. Every active cycle must therefore be completable through the eligible free activities actually available. Variety can come from that game's naturally emitted facts and scenarios. Do not force mechanics or artificial proofs into a game merely to manufacture cross-game breadth. Additional games later provide alternative routes and exploration incentives, not mandatory prerequisites to earn any token.

A verified activity may advance a planet campaign, the player's one active token cycle, credits, and a participating creature's active game-specific intention under their separate policies. It cannot count in two token cycles at once. Easy one-time site achievements do not directly issue extra tokens. Paid arcade activity may make a small, real, clearly disclosed, capped contribution to qualification, but must never be the fastest, exclusive, or unbounded path.

## 4. Starter allocation and the playable-roster floor

New accounts receive fully owned starter creatures so they can play before earning a token. They are authentic, developable creatures with `starter-allocation` provenance, not loaners. The provisional starting policy is one stable, non-refreshable slate of five exact individual previews from a versioned, capped common-band profile. The player chooses three; only the chosen three become owned creature records. An optional automatic selection may choose a viable trio. Any permitted choice of three must support the introductory experience. Exact candidates, planet spread, and final roster size are confirmed against the first integrated game's needs.

Starter generation excludes rare finishes, mutations, exceptional trait combinations, and premium or strategically exceptional rolls by validating the complete outcome, not merely capping each independent trait. Starters are viable, not deliberately handicapped. They may remain useful after full generation opens, but cannot begin with scarce or premium qualities. They retain normal capacity for development and normal lifecycle rights.

The account has a protected **playable, player-owned roster floor**, provisionally three. A voluntary trade, release, or NPC surrender cannot leave the account below that floor, evaluated against the final atomic transaction state. A one-for-one starter trade may be allowed immediately because the roster count stays intact. There is no token-per-starter exit counter. A formal trade hold still counts as owned until execution. There is no platform-wide maximum collection size and no paid core creature-storage capacity.

**Opening journey direction (agreed 2026-09-17):** After the starter choice, the first major earned payoff is qualifying for a Scrambler Token and generating the player's first token-born creature. The same guided qualification that issues that token also attunes the first additional world. Its eligible species pool must exclude the species of all five starter candidates, so every permitted trio has a newly available Generator that can produce a new species under ordinary uniform selection. The token is not bound to that world: players may instead save it or choose any other attuned world, accepting its disclosed chance of a familiar species. This preserves normal odds without a hidden first-roll exception or duplicate-protection rule. The opening must not require a trade or irreversible release. Exact game steps, thresholds, starter species, world selection, and credit fee remain to be designed against real content.

## 5. Generation and planet discovery

The Mercurius Machine issues seeds; a planet-bound Generator expresses one seed as a creature. At redemption, the owner chooses any planet to which their account is attuned, presents one earned token, and pays a modest, predictable credit activation fee. The transaction atomically consumes the token, deducts credits, generates the creature from the fixed seed, selected planet, and pinned current generation release, and registers ownership. On failure, it consumes nothing. The fee should ordinarily be affordable by the time a player earns a token.

The chosen planet determines primary element and the eligible native species pool. Initial species selection is uniform among that pool, with no per-species rarity weight or stamped rarity tier. Individual genesis rolls remain random under the published generation policy. No named-species purchase, paid reroll, personalized duplicate tilt, hidden pity adjustment based on holdings, or paid improvement to rarity or mechanical potential is part of the initial system. Duplicates remain distinct individuals with trade, NPC surrender, and restoration uses, not fusion fodder. Generated signatures exist at birth; development must not become a straight vertical signature unlock.

Planet attunement is a permanent, account-bound discovery campaign. The encyclopedia and all canonical worlds may be visible from the beginning, while Generator access begins with a small viable subset and expands through clear, achievable chapters. The first additional world unlocks with the first guided token qualification and offers the disjoint starter-species pool described above. Credits cannot buy initial attunement. Required introductory game play is free and completion-based rather than win-based, with a guided mode and a permanent fallback if a game is retired. Campaign branching should keep one disliked game from blocking nearly the entire galaxy. Every canonical planet needs a permanent guaranteed access route; limited events may add an alternate or faster route, never the only route. Later planets provide different species, elements, stories, and strategies, not a vertical power ladder.

Some attunement chapters may offer a **restoration milestone as an alternative route** to game exploration. A project on an already reachable world can help open a neighboring world, avoiding a requirement to release a creature from a locked world first. Restoration is not mandatory for every planet. A creature acquired by trade from an unattuned planet may still be owned and played, but it does not grant Generator attunement. Unused tokens can be saved for later worlds or new content because they have no planet or policy lock at issuance.

Before redemption, the UI should disclose the selected planet, current eligible pool, species-selection rule, material odds and trait ranges, fee, and irreversibility. Exact starting planets, chapter order, mission requirements, and generation pricing await the first integrated content. Higher token grades, Generator modes, site-specific generation, and account-dependent species weighting are deferred, not launch assumptions.

## 6. Ordinary credits and paid access

Ordinary fungible currency needs a distinct final name; **credits** is a placeholder. The player sees one balance, while an auditable ledger distinguishes earned, purchased, promotional, support-adjusted, refunded, and reversed entries. The ledger records reason, source receipt, policy version, and idempotency. Credits remain account-bound, have no cash-out or peer transfer, and ordinarily do not expire. Purchased and earned credits have the same visible spending power unless future legal or storefront review requires otherwise.

Verified completed games may award credits on a mode-specific policy. Completed losses or failures can earn them, with eligible wins generally earning more. Abandoned or unresolved games earn nothing. NPC surrender pays a modest fixed credit floor initially; it does not automatically scale with rarity, finish, development, or history, and it does not grant Scrambler progress. There is no automatic standing penalty initially. Credit scarcity may diminish for veteran players. Do not add upkeep, repair, storage, or other artificial recurring taxes to force spending.

Credits can pay a Generator activation fee, including when purchased, because the token remains the earned generation entitlement. Future credit sinks may include cosmetics, wearables, optional content, saved configurations, showcase layouts, and genuinely useful convenience. Paid access may allow a player to keep enjoying a preferred arcade mode and receive modest, transparent, bounded progress. It must never buy token qualification directly, bypass attunement, increase reward rates, improve a creature, generate more creatures without earned tokens, or create an exclusive or best-efficiency progression route. Consumable does not imply a gameplay boost. If repetition reduces credit awards, show the applicable window and next-session outcome before play or payment. Scrambler qualification needs harder family ceilings, not merely a short time throttle. Match duration or time spent in a status never measures development.

Purchases should give an honestly described benefit, not manufacture frustration or imply a larger advantage than exists. Account-owned paid wearables and cosmetics unequip when a creature changes custody. Purchased content access, decorations, and presentation entitlements stay with the account. Player trades initially include creatures only, and NPCs do not repurchase credit-bought cosmetics for credits. Permanent paid cosmetic treatments that travel with a creature are deferred because they could become indirect purchased trade value. Chargebacks or fraud handling must not silently rewrite a traded creature's history.

## 7. Game-specific creature development

Mechanical development is initially **specific to the game that defines it**. A game may have a versioned catalog of mutually exclusive, bounded sidegrades. A creature starts viable with no development in each game. Initially there is at most one active specialization for that game per creature. No platform-wide practice or environmental conditioning slot is active. In particular, environmental tolerance in immutable genesis is not edited by play. If a future real game demonstrates the need for another axis, add it deliberately to that game's policy. Other games do not reinterpret its specialty. Restoration initially does not inspect development or gameplay history.

Only verified receipts from the defining game may advance its development. The owner declares one active development intention for the creature before play, including the target and the active entry it would replace. Requirements are a small registered checklist, not generic XP. A receipt is evaluated against the intention in the session's starting snapshot and cannot be applied retroactively to a new target. Applicable participating creatures may each advance their own intentions from one match. Absent or benched creatures do not. Completed losses may contribute when the target's policy says so; abandoned sessions never do. Raw elapsed play time is not a criterion, though a game-authored semantic choice such as short versus extended expedition may be a registered fact.

The current specialty stays active during retraining. When the checklist is complete, the new entry becomes **ready to activate**, not automatic. While ready, its target and replaced slot are fixed, it gains no more progress, and the creature cannot begin another intention. Outside an active authoritative session or locked roster, the owner explicitly activates the replacement or discards it. Both actions are final. There is no expiration clock, bank of alternate loadouts, free switch-back, credit fee, or reusable partial progress. Changing or canceling an unfinished intention discards that partial checklist. Reacquiring an archived entry requires developing it again under current requirements.

Completed active development and archived history travel with the creature through trade. Active entries, not archived entries, affect games. A new session uses the current game balance for an entry, not its historical numeric patch, while old sessions remain reproducible under their pinned rules. An entry's conceptual meaning cannot silently change; a materially different specialty needs a new key. Partial intentions and ready-but-uncommitted entries are owner relationship state and are discarded only when custody actually changes. Listing a creature does not discard them. The interface warns before a transaction that would discard unfinished work.

Squad cohesion is not a universal score or power bonus. Factual co-play may be retained in relationship history. A concrete game may later interpret its own coordination choices, but no platform mechanic is required before that game needs it.

## 8. Game receipts and explicit integration cutover

Local game prototypes may iterate freely. **Do not extract, restructure, instrument, or enforce a deterministic platform engine during gameplay development unless Nick explicitly requests the cutover.** The phases are local prototype, gameplay stabilization, explicit requested cutover, then platform integration. A game not intended to award permanent platform value may stay local or nondeterministic.

At cutover, a reward-bearing game must be deterministically replayable: the same initial versioned snapshot, rules and content release, server seed, and ordered player commands produce the same final state and facts. The server, not the client, verifies the submitted command transcript and derives results. Determinism does not itself prove an authentic player, so account-integrity controls remain separate. Game code emits a factual receipt and never declares its own credit, token, or development payout.

Receipts use a small global envelope and closed, versioned, namespaced per-game fact manifests. Each fact key defines its nature, scope, value type, exact emission rule, and version. Published meanings are append-only; changed meanings get new keys. The fact registry defines what happened, while policy maps facts to account, credit, or development consequences. Keep three principal version surfaces: `receiptVersion`, `gameRulesVersion` (including fact semantics), and `developmentPolicyVersion` (including reward mappings). Ordered receipts, prior state, and pinned versions must reproduce the same transition. Use receipt IDs, idempotency, transactional updates, and a policy-owned economic circuit breaker for compromised game versions.

Resolved completed sessions may earn rewards whether won or lost. Abandoned and unresolved sessions earn no permanent rewards or development. A creature in an unresolved economy-eligible session cannot execute a trade, release, or NPC surrender until it resolves or is explicitly abandoned. A formal trade may be drafted but not executed meanwhile. An abandoned session rejects later results. Local non-economy play creates no platform custody lock. There is no universal rule yet for simultaneous sessions, beyond resolving all relevant sessions before custody changes. A compromised game version may continue in clearly labeled non-economy mode while its receipts are quarantined and reviewed.

## 9. Trades, custody, and creature presentation

Player trades are bilateral creature-for-creature exchanges, including bundles. Each side offers at least one creature. No direct gifts, credits, tokens, wearables, auctions, price-denominated marketplace, or ordinary trade fee initially. NPC and platform transactions may pay or charge credits under policy. Trade never creates credits. Every completed trade is atomic and final, with permanent custody events; infrastructure or account-compromise remediation is a separate audited correction, not silent history rewriting.

A public listing is nonbinding and does not lock a creature. A formal offer places only creatures voluntarily offered by their current owners on a trade hold. An unsolicited offer cannot lock someone else's creature. While held, a creature remains owned and viewable, with its completed development unchanged, but cannot enter economy-eligible play, advance or activate development, enter another formal offer, release, or surrender. Pending owner-specific intentions are preserved if the offer is canceled and discarded only if custody changes. Owners can cancel before execution; offers should expire and stranded holds must be recoverable. A creature must resolve or abandon relevant sessions before entering the hold. This hold stabilizes transferable state without requiring a broad monotonic creature-revision scheme.

Both players review the exact bundle, active game-specific development, nontransferring state, cosmetics, and post-trade roster counts before final approval. Changing the bundle clears approvals. Execution rechecks ownership, hold, session eligibility, and both roster floors atomically. After transfer, each received creature is immediately usable. Genesis, active completed development, archived development history, and verified provenance travel. Partial or ready intentions, owner-specific relationships, nicknames, biographies, private notes, tags, favorites, squad presets, and account-owned cosmetics do not.

The platform keeps an authoritative internal ledger, an owner view, and a privacy-aware public provenance projection. Verified creature history survives ownership change, but former account identifiers, private matches, and owner-written claims are not automatically public. A previous keeper may opt into attribution; removing attribution does not erase the custody fact. Naming and renaming are free. Creature history is not a permanent public dossier of every person who owned it.

## 10. Release, NPC surrender, and restoration

Leaving player circulation is a lifecycle and custody transition, never routine database erasure. Player trade transfers playable ownership. Release moves a creature to a planet's reserve and advances the player's long-term restoration effort. NPC surrender moves it to NPC custody for the fixed initial credit payout. True destruction is exceptional narrative state, not a routine materials or token reward. Neither release nor NPC surrender returns a Scrambler Token directly.

Restoration is personal effort inside one shared, authored galaxy. The same fourteen canonical planets, lore, and story chapters exist for everyone; player actions do not automatically solve global canon. Each player has a personal reserve and finishable authored restoration projects on canonical worlds, not duplicate private planets or one catch-all personal world. Released creatures normally go to their species homeworld, initially the same as Generator origin. Their genesis and history remain preserved and browsable. Release removes playable ownership and respects the protected roster floor.

A release always makes the creature a resident of its homeworld reserve. Before confirmation, the player sees currently open project requirements that the creature could satisfy and may select **one** permanent project contribution, or choose reserve-only release. A release cannot retroactively supply a new permanent contribution to a later project. Projects use broadly satisfiable composition predicates and multiple valid solutions, initially grounded in immutable genesis and project context, not a generic rarity or development total. A project cannot demand a rare premium outcome as its only route. Completing a project leaves a lasting reserve, story, or presentation change. Later authored chapters can request new releases without undoing earlier work; avoid a meaningless infinite progress meter.

Some completed projects may be alternate routes to planet attunement. Such a route must be achievable from already accessible worlds, while a permanent free game-exploration route remains available. Individual releases do not grant repeatable Scrambler progress. A one-time restoration milestone may contribute a capped amount to a qualification policy if later designed and disclosed. No release fee is agreed; do not assume a credit toll. NPC surrender remains the immediate credit route; it creates no restoration progress.

**Provisional extension, not MVP:** reserve residents may staff a limited number of reversible active posts. One creature occupies at most one post; moving it loses the previous active benefit. Assignments never make old residents count as fresh permanent contributions, and initially confer no direct competitive squad power. Defer the layer if it becomes chore-like, has one obvious configuration, or demands too much interface/content complexity. Limited seasonal projects may be added later through data-driven manifests, without making them the only permanent path or granting exclusive competitive power.

## 11. Ownership, privacy, and account integrity

Ordinary ownership and long-term preservation have no paid storage cap. Games may limit active roster size and restoration may limit active assignments. Purchased saved configurations or showcase layouts are presentation conveniences, not core storage. An account closure does not fabricate a release, surrender, reward, or redistribution: remaining creatures enter an administrative dormant-custody state with preserved genesis and history and anonymized public attribution. Suspension freezes an account without rewriting custody. Exact legal retention, purchase, and refund handling require later review.

The economy should not rely on proving that one human owns exactly one account. The starter floor, reciprocal trades, account-bound credits and tokens, full-effort first qualification, family contribution limits, and verified game receipts reduce farming incentives. Account policy should prohibit using secondary accounts to evade limits or automate play, with graduated review rather than automatic punishment of households or friends. Real suspicious trade or gameplay patterns may inform later integrity controls. Do not require invasive identity verification for the base economy without evidence.

## 12. Current implementation and migration boundary

As of this reconciliation, the repository does **not** implement this economy or lifecycle. Signed-in `POST /xalians` currently generates an owned creature for free and may accept an exact species; `DELETE /xalians/{id}` physically removes the registry item. The anonymous showroom is unowned. The existing Arcade endpoint verifies a submitted win and awards numeric credits under a daily cap, with an automatic credit-to-token conversion. That conversion, the free signed-in generation route, the current release deletion, and the exact-species parameter are implementation facts, **not** approved final economy rules. The current games and local modes are not thereby required to adopt this policy during gameplay iteration.

Nick confirmed on 2026-09-17 that the existing creature and economy records are disposable prototype data. At an explicit future cutover, those in-scope test records may be cleared rather than migrated or grandfathered. This is permission for a clean launch, **not an instruction to delete them now** and not permission to remove unrelated content, source files, or lore. Before any actual reset, identify the environment and exact records, verify scope, and perform a deliberate launch transaction or runbook. Official new accounts then enter the starter allocation. Keep test and official reward state clearly separated before public trading or paid credits open.

## 13. Deferred decisions and evidence needed

- First integrated game's natural fact manifest, development catalog, reward families, and the actual first-token and renewable qualification requirements.
- Final starter slate, allowed common band, initial planet set, campaign chapter paths, and the roster size needed for a satisfying first game. Three-of-five and floor three are strong provisional defaults.
- Credit name, generation fee, game payouts, NPC payout, repetition windows, paid-service catalog, and financial or storefront terms.
- Exact restoration project manifests, composition predicates, milestones, reserve presentation, and whether the optional active-assignment layer provides enough value to build.
- Generator modes, site-specific conditions, higher token grades, species-pool additions, and any explicit duplicate protection. None is silently active now.
- Mechanical squad cohesion, platform-wide environmental conditioning, cross-game development mappings, development-aware restoration, and transferable paid items. Add only after a concrete game or restoration need demonstrates value.

The next implementation order is intentionally conditional: stabilize a game's fun local mode, request its explicit platform cutover, define and verify its natural facts and replay, then connect policy and durable economy. Documentation of this future contract must not become friction during current gameplay development.
