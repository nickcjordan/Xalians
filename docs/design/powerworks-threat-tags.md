# Powerworks: an enemy's next hit shows where it lands

## Context

Nick, 2026-09-30, on the first turn of a run, before he had made a move: "why is it saying Graviclaw and Crystorn have attacked for 14 each?" Each enemy plate carried an intent chip (#770) that led with the target's portrait and name, then "HITS ✺14". Read top to bottom it says "Graviclaw hits 14": the companion as the subject, the hit as something already done. A word fix ("NEXT HITS") was tried and dropped; Nick asked for a redesign instead of a bandaid.

The root problem is where the fact sits. An enemy's plate is about that enemy; the hit it has committed to is about the companion it will land on. Putting a companion's name and portrait inside an enemy's plate makes the plate say two things, and the reader picks the wrong subject. The page already has the right rule for the player's own moves: a previewed result shows on the plate it lands on (intents contract, decision 3). This applies the same rule to the enemies: a committed hit shows on the plate it will land on, tagged with the letter of the enemy that will throw it. The genre's clearest example is Into the Breach, which marks the threatened tile, not the attacker.

## Storyboard (the frames the judges read)

1. First turn, at rest (the frame Nick sent): the enemy plates show name, health, matchup; Graviclaw's plate carries an "A ✺14" tag and Crystorn's a "B ✺14" tag. Nothing names a companion on an enemy plate.
2. Hover enemy A: a line from A's figure to Graviclaw's plate; both ringed.
3. Choose a hinder move, hover B: Crystorn's tag reads 14 struck, then the lower number.
4. Choose a strong move whose preview finishes A: Graviclaw's "A" tag is struck through and stepped back.
5. A lethal intent: the tag with the skull and danger border on the companion.
6. A support intent: a heal tag on the enemy it will heal.
7. Enemy turns: the acting enemy's tag lit, the line of the strike, the tag gone after the impact.
8. Phone, 844x390: the tags on the companion plates, readable.

Deviations and additions the build needed:
- A tag that would run into the element tag rides one row higher (above it); the plate carries the height the tags take (`--tags-h`) so a previewed number rises over them. An enemy plate that carries tags puts its preview number at the top right of its figure area.
- At rest, when a committed target has already fallen, the tag shows on the companion the engine's own redirect will choose (not on the fallen plate, which would hide it). During playback the tag moves at the redirect beat as specified, with the number read at the state the run holds.
- A hit's number leaves out the enemy's own persistent hinder as a struck form (the enemy's hinder chip already says it); only previews strike a number.
- Phone only, where the short stage cannot hold two things: a previewed enemy's own tags step aside; the head pointer of a companion that carries tags is not drawn (its green plaque still marks it); the tags of the plate a blow is landing on step aside for the landing number; a struck tag drops its glyph; the enemy row takes 50% and the squad row 48% of the stage (was 58 and 40, sized for the old chip). No tags show while the stage holds on a fall.
