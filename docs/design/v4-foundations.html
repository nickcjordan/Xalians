Design system v4, foundations

The viable signal

The accent is the color a Generator shows when a genome is alive and printable. It is the logo's own mint, carried into the interface with one meaning: this is living, or this is the next step. Everything else on the page stays quiet so that the signal, and the creatures, can be loud.

The existing wordmark and the DNA form it morphs into on the home splash. Both stay. Their mint, #86ffb5, is the source of the accent.

1. Accent one hue, three tiers

The logo mint at full strength is an emitter: it looks like light. Used large it turns the site into a green terminal, which is the Pip-Boy signal we agreed to avoid. So the hue is carried in three tiers, and the bright one is only ever small.

#86ffb5 viable-hi
Emitter
The logo, live values on glass, status dots, the focus ring, the active toggle knob. Small, on dark, never a fill wider than a line of text.
#4fc98d viable
Fill
The one primary button on a screen, the selected segment, the active tab underline. Calm enough to sit on a matte surface without glowing.
#2f8f63 viable-lo
Pressed and border
Pressed state of the primary button, the on-state of a toggle track, a selected row's border. Plus a 10% tint of the emitter for selected backgrounds.
The test any agent can applyIs this thing alive, or is it the one action that moves the person forward? If neither, it is not accent-colored. Headings, icons, dividers, decorative rules and section markers never wear it. One primary button per screen.
Why it is groundedXalians are engineered life; the Generators, the Scrambler Tokens, the tournament loop and the Plague are all about genomes. The helix in the logo already says so. The accent is the Generator's signal that a genome is viable, so a living Xalian and a forward action share one color for one reason.

2. Semantic colors status only, never content

Status colors say what state something is in. They are separate from the accent and separate from the element hues, and they appear only on interface state: badges, borders, toasts, validation. Content never borrows them.

Viable

Success, alive, connected, saved. Same hue as the accent on purpose: the good outcome and the forward action are one idea.

Saved to your account.
Plague

Error, failure, destructive. A sick raspberry, kept away from the Fire element's orange-red. Never used as a highlight, so the two ends of the lore's conflict are the two ends of the palette.

Could not save. Sign in and try again.
Caution

Warning, unsaved, expiring. A dusty amber. Rare by design; most of the site has nothing to warn about.

This Xalian is not kept. Sign in before you leave.
Neutral

Information, idle, offline, not set. A cool slate that is clearly not a verdict.

Method: every color measured in OKLCH, the perceptual color space, and scored two ways: contrast against a dark foreground (floor 4.5:1, since these are backgrounds under dark text) and distance to its nearest neighbor. Yours had three contrast failures (Dark, Ghost, Rock) and five near-identical pairs (Electric and Sand, Air and Light, Dark and Ghost, Plant and Rock, Water and Metal). The proposed set has no contrast failures and its closest pair is 60 percent further apart than yours was. The hues now sit roughly 26 degrees apart around the wheel, which is as far as fourteen colors can be spread. Element hues appear only on element-tagged content, interface state never uses them, and collisions with the accent or status colors are resolved by role.

3. Neutrals warm room, bone ink

The room is a warm near-black and the ink is bone rather than white. The warmth is deliberate: mint against a cool grey reads as software; mint against a warm dark reads as a light in a real room. Surfaces step up in four tones. Glass is darker than the room and holds only live data.

5. Space, radius, depth 4 point grid, low relief

Spacing is a 4 point scale. Corners are 3 on controls and 6 on panels; nothing is a pill except a dot or a toggle. Depth is low: surfaces are told apart by tone and a hairline edge, with a single inset highlight on the top edge. Drop shadows exist only on things that float over the page.

Level 0, s0Navbar, page footer, sidebars. Sits on the room.
Level 1, s1Cards and panels. The default container for content.
Level 2, s2Controls resting on a panel. Buttons, segments, inputs.
GlassLive data only: a fresh record, a match summary, a status figure. Darker than the room.
FloatingModal, popover, toast, menu. The only things with a drop shadow.

7. Motion quick, physical, optional

Durations120 ms for state changes on controls, 200 ms for things that appear or move within a panel, 320 ms for a panel or modal entering. Nothing on the chrome is longer. Enter eases out, exit eases in.
What may moveA control changing state, a panel entering, a value updating on glass, the home splash once on arrival. Nothing loops, nothing pulses, nothing glows on a timer. Under reduced motion every transition is instant and the splash shows its resting frame.

8b. Wordmark, round four real faces, scanned

Agreed: hand-applied cuts were the wrong direction. I scanned the Google Fonts catalog by its own tags (Stencil, Techno, Futuristic, and the Nova family which is untagged) and pulled every free face that either has designer-made cuts or breaks in the letters, or has the squared engineered character without them. Google Fonts only, because they are the one font host the mockup page can load; Fontshare and Velvetyne have a few more candidates and I can render those as images in a second pass if nothing here lands. Every candidate is shown at splash size and at navbar size, in mint, as the real word.

The morph works with any of them. The X of whichever face wins is converted to an SVG path once (opentype.js does this), and GSAP's MorphSVG morphs that path into the helix's strands the same way it morphs your three-piece X today. If the helix should stay three pieces, the X path is split at its crossing. So the choice is on looks alone.

8c. Where this still reads as a software site an honest pass

You asked me to check where I am leaning on the corporate half of my training. Here is what I see, what it would become instead, and what I am proposing to do about it. The interface staying quiet is still right; the question is whether the world gets to show up in it. Right now it mostly does not.

9. What a complete system includes scope of the v4 contract

FoundationsEverything on this page as tokens in three layers: primitive (the hex), semantic (surface, ink, accent, status, focus), component (button-primary-bg and so on). CSS and JavaScript mirrors stay paired by test.
Component inventoryButtons by rank, links, inputs, select, segmented, checkbox, toggle, range, form field with label and help and error; cards, lists, tables, tabs, badges, chips, meters, progress; navbar, masthead, footer, breadcrumb; modal, drawer, toast, tooltip, menu; empty, loading, error and skeleton states; pagination.
Patterns and templatesIndex page, record page, setup form, results screen, reading page. Form validation, destructive confirmation, responsive collapse at 1024 and 640, the way a featured component sits inside the chrome.
Content and accessibilityPlain copy on controls, sentence case except legends, numbers and units formatting, where in-world voice is allowed (content) and where it is not (controls). Contrast floor 4.5:1, visible focus, keyboard order, 44 pixel touch targets, reduced motion.
GovernanceThe classification rule (chrome, featured component, immersive experience) with the test for each; how to add a token; the paint-check harness; a styleguide page that renders every token and every component state; the build-ui skill as a decision tree that ends in an existing component or a written justification for a custom one.
What leavesTerminal objects and their props, per-area faces and accents, fake readouts, in-world button copy, hazard livery, the orange. The world lives in the content. The interface stays quiet so the content can be loud.
