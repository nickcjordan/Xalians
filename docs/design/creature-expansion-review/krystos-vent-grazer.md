# Krystos vent-grazer concept

Status: first concept pass after Nick accepted Krystos as the next target on 2026-09-26. No species name, template, signature, appearance, or canon has been approved. This is not yet a creature record or an ability audit. The [target brief](../creature-expansion-target-briefs-02.md) and the current v5 contract govern the next passes.

## Source boundary

Krystos's planetary history establishes a frozen ocean and oxygen-producing algae deep beneath the ice, fed by geothermal vents. The current environmental report establishes vent-warmed subsurface water, optimal burrowing through ice and drift, insulated high-mass tunneling forms, and other forms capable of full vital arrest through a storm. It does **not** establish that one species combines all those features or specify what any of those forms eats. Assigning the algae, tunneling, and suspension to this new creature is a proposed synthesis, not a recovered fact.

The current v5 model explicitly defers mechanical dormancy. If full vital arrest remains part of this species, it is ecological lore only. It gives no status, passive, signature, reaction, protection, or game-state shortcut. Its active abilities must work when it is awake on ordinary solid ground.

## Proposed ecological loop

The creature spends active periods along vents and reachable cavities beneath the ice, where it grazes the established algal growth. It also crosses the frozen surface and cuts shelter passages through drifts and ice. If it takes preserved organic remains exposed along those routes, that is opportunistic scavenging rather than its only food supply; the dead of ancient battlefields are finite, so an exclusively carrion-fed high-mass population would not be a sound baseline. During a severe blizzard it shelters and can suspend its vital activity, as the report says some Krystos fauna do. It resumes ordinary movement when conditions allow.

This life cycle makes swimming, digging, land support, and storm survival parts of one animal. It does not require a vent, blizzard, snow, water, or carrion for the creature to stand, move, defend itself, and use its eventual complete moveset in another game.

## Posture correction and body architecture to test

- The first low, broad quadruped sketch is withdrawn. Sonalloy, Shuntara, and Fathomaw already use low bodies for individually valid reasons. Repeating that posture here would be a design habit, not a Krystos requirement. A body carried above the snow has better clearance through drifts and loses less heat by continuous contact with ice; broad feet can distribute its weight without dragging its belly.
- Test a high-shouldered quadruped with a visibly arched but comparatively narrow trunk, long powerful forelimbs that widen only at the digging hands, and large spreading hind feet. It normally stands with clear space under its chest. It lowers its forequarters only to excavate or enter a passage; the digging pose is not its permanent outline.
- The forelimbs carry broad, clawed digging hands that chip and clear ice. The rear limbs carry wider webbing for swimming and counterstrokes. In a tunnel the limbs fold back near the trunk after the claws open a wide enough passage. These functions should remain legible in one standing pose and one swimming pose; fins do not replace its land-bearing limbs.
- A single forward-projecting, spade-shaped brow of tough living tissue helps lift ice already opened by the foreclaws and shelters small eyes from blowing debris. It is part of the head, not a detached tool, extra jaw, or ice horn. An algae-rasping mouth sits beneath it. The brow and mouth must be drawn so the creature can visibly feed and see. The elevated shoulders should not force the head to remain out of reach of the ice it works.
- A thick, flexible insulating hide covers the trunk. It is not a rigid shell, automatic armor, or an ice projector. Broad hide folds around the neck could give the creature character and protect exposed seams, but their final shape should be chosen with the whole silhouette, not appended as decoration.

This architecture deliberately contrasts Akinza's slender feline biped and Yetimoth's shaggy upright guard without repeating the low silhouettes of the three recent designs. It also avoids Foromeer's horn bore, Frackworm's injected slurry, and Drilltail's tail drill. The digging edge is a forelimb-and-head operation; it is not a rotating drill or an emitted substance. A four-limbed form is easier to read and animate than adding extra legs solely for numerical novelty. These are design reasons, not a rule against extra limbs if a better mechanism emerges. The raised posture still needs a tunnel-clearance and swimming check before acceptance.

### Why this posture is a candidate, not a new formula

| Resting posture | Snow travel | Tunnel and water cost | Visual contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Belly-gliding, first sketch | Belly drags through drifts and stays against ice | Fits a shallow passage and swims cleanly | Repeats the recent low-bodied silhouettes |
| Raised shoulders on four spreading feet | Clears irregular snow with weight carried above it | Must fold limbs and excavate a taller entrance; limbs add drag until tucked | Gives a visible arch and open negative space beneath the chest |
| Permanently upright/rearing | Can reach down into cracks | Narrow balance on pack ice and weak swimming posture | Crowds Akinza and Yetimoth's upright lane |

The middle option currently wins the combined test, but not because the roster now needs a tall creature by quota. It wins only if the folded-limb tunnel and swimming poses remain convincing. If either fails, revisit body architecture rather than force the opposite of the previous low-body habit.

## Active capability hypothesis

The strongest first signature candidate is a contacted scoop-and-turn: the forelimbs hook around a target while the broad brow supports it from below, then the creature turns and thrusts to drive it away. This is not a remote throw or a mandatory restraint status. Rear feet brace on land; in water the rear limbs paddle and the body recoils. Counterstroking can orient the maneuver, but does not create a rigid anchor or justify equal throwing power against every target in both media. The defining action remains available without ice, a vent, or a wall; games resolve relative mass, footing, and displacement. On Krystos the same anatomy lifts already broken ice from a passage. This can be a fixed signature assembled from ordinary physical capabilities; signature status does not require inventing a new effect or a supernatural power. Exact effect structure and name remain open. No current v5 species has a displacement signature, which is a useful secondary contrast, not a reason to force this outcome.

The foreclaws could strike, rake, crush, and shove under the shared derived-act table; the body and jaws also grant candidate families. None can be dropped merely to make the list tidy. A later ability audit must inspect all table-granted acts and any supported extensions, including protection or restraint if the final brow, hide, or mouth actually supplies them. Cold survival by itself grants no elemental conduit or attack resistance.

## Checks before a template

1. Test the feeding path physically: where the under-ice algae grows, how the animal reaches it, and how the mouth collects it. If that requires an invented aperture or unbelievable excavation through a full ocean sheet, redesign the habitat or feeding role.
2. Trace the four load-bearing limbs and the brow from body to contact in a raised neutral stance, a lowered digging pose, and a swimming pose. Test whether the folded limbs fit a plausible excavated passage and whether broad foreclaws can dig and still function in water.
3. Decide whether the rotational levering action truly deserves signature status or is just `body/shove` with dramatic prose. Its counterstroke must work without ground support. Find a source-supported distinguishing effect before assigning a name or output band.
4. Review all anatomy, channels, conduits, and effect families with the formal v5 ability audit. Do not represent deferred dormancy by a renamed status.
5. Only after the body and action are stable, choose a memorable but anatomically coherent character feature, then a pronounceable name with documented roots and a sound distinct from the roster.

## Construction and ecological pass, 2026-09-30

The resting posture is an elevated, forward-heavy quadruped, not a permanently crouched digger. Its trunk slopes from high shoulders toward a smaller rounded rump. The neck is short but can pitch downward with the shoulders. A broad flattened brow extends beyond the mouth like a short scoop, with a rounded leading edge rather than a piercing horn. The eyes sit behind and above that edge. Four limbs remain separate: forehands combine blunt weight-bearing pads with three stout digging claws; hind feet have a weight-bearing central pad and spreading, webbed toes. Webbing folds when walking, rather than becoming the sole surface carrying body mass. These are proposed anatomy facts, not approved canon.

### What the construction study establishes

A side-view diagram traces two forelimbs and two hind limbs in standing, digging, and tucked swimming poses. Forelimb segments stay at 52 and 58 drawing units; hindlimb segments stay at 44 and 50. Joint bends are solved from those lengths rather than shortening limbs to fit a different pose. Units are construction ratios, not creature measurements. The standing pose leaves chest clearance, and pitching the trunk while bending the limbs brings the scoop close to the surface. The tucked pose shows that long limbs need not remain extended while swimming.

This is a joint-reach sketch, not a biomechanical validation or an official portrait. It does not establish strength, buoyancy, joint limits, comfortable gait, or front-view tunnel width. A folded pose does not prove the creature can travel through a body-tight tunnel: active tunneling requires clearance for repeated forelimb strokes and removing spoil. Shelter passages must exceed that working envelope, not merely the folded body envelope. It is not assigned an implausibly narrow bore to make the raised stance look efficient.

### Feeding, breathing, and excavation limits

- Proposed feeding habitat: vent-warmed cavities, water-filled fissures, and their connected edges beneath the frozen ocean. Algae growing there is the canonical resource; its exact accessible distribution is a species-design inference. The creature grazes submerged surfaces with blunt rasping dental plates. It does not need to drill from the surface through the full ocean ice sheet for every meal.
- Excavation is through snow, drift, fractured ice, and thin ice partitions. Foreclaws open seams and clear fragments; the brow lifts loosened pieces. Continuous boring through massive intact glacier ice is not claimed. The route between habitats follows existing openings where possible.
- Air-breathing lungs are proposed as its ordinary respiration, including normal land encounters. It can swim and dive while holding its breath and return to air pockets or open water to breathe. No auxiliary gills, oxygen absorption through hide, portable tank, or indefinite underwater life is invented merely to fill a gap. Reliable access to air constrains its native routes.
- Insulation and internal metabolism support active cold tolerance, but neither implies cold immunity or unlimited heat. Full vital arrest remains only the proposed severe-storm shelter behavior, outside the current mechanical model. Exact active tolerance bands are not assigned until the habitat and dimensions are agreed.

### Character integrated with anatomy

Give the short scoop brow a broad, gently upturned leading rim and a shallow central notch. It should look like an expressive muzzle, not a tool strapped to its face or two tusks. A single broad pale cheek-to-throat patch follows the jaw and neck, rather than tracing every muscle or joint. The shoulder outline has one rounded rise, not a stack of industrial plates. These character candidates preserve the feeding opening, eye placement, digging reach, and limb attachment paths. No extra tendrils or ornamental appendages are needed to differentiate it. Final markings remain subject to portrait review.
