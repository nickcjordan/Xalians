# Creature appeal: the floor every Xalian clears

Ruled by Nick on 2026-10-25, after the Luceras slice. Next to Akinza, a kid would never pick Luceras, even though Luceras was built faithfully. Nick's words: "I think the minimum floor is the important part. The appeal needs to have the minimum. I think some creatures can probably have more appeal. But your example of Toothless from How to Train Your Dragon is the exact concept I was thinking about. I want to make sure that everything we create has the correct appeal here."

## Context

Animators call this quality **appeal**. Its known basis is the baby schema (Kindchenschema): a small set of proportions that people, children most of all, read as safe and likable without thinking about it. The target is not a cartoon. The target is Toothless, or the furry creatures in *Detective Pikachu*: fully realistic surfaces (scales, strand fur, wet eyes) on proportions tuned for appeal. The realism stays in the materials, and the appeal comes from the proportions, above all the eyes.

Every species clears the **floor** below. A species may go above it; a therapy creature from Telypso can sit far higher than a war beast from Magmuth. No species goes below it, whatever its lore. Menace comes from posture, motion, color and the signature act, never from failing the floor.

## The floor (measured, every species)

Measured on the rest pose, head in profile and from straight ahead. Ratios use the skull's own height (crown to jaw line, excluding horns, ears and crests).

1. **Eye size.** Eye opening height is at least 16 percent of head height. Real goats sit near 10 percent; Akinza sits far above the floor.
2. **Eyes facing forward.** Both eyes are visible from straight ahead: each eye's axis turns no more than 35 degrees from the snout line.
3. **Eyes low on the head.** The eye center sits at or below the midpoint between crown and jaw line, which leaves a rounded forehead above.
4. **Readable eyes.** A round pupil at rest, no more than half the iris width, with the iris ring visible all the way round and a highlight in every render. The eye reads as an eye, not a dot, at sprite size (192 px body height).
5. **Muzzle.** Snout length (eye center to nose tip) is at most 1.2 times the eye-to-crown distance. A longer face needs a compensating gain elsewhere (larger eyes, a rounder skull) and the reader test below.
6. **Face contrast.** The eye area reads lighter than the surrounding coat, or framed by a high-contrast rim, so the eyes stay the first thing seen at sprite size.
7. **Threat features near the face.** Horns, crests, teeth and spikes are allowed, but none crosses in front of the eyes in the rest pose, and points near the face are blunted or curved away from it.
8. **Soft forms around the head.** Cheeks and the forehead read rounded. Hard planes and sharp angles belong on the body and limbs, not framing the face.

## The reader test (every species, every gate that shows the face)

At sprite size, a lineup is shown to a blind Opus reader: the candidate, the appeal reference (Akinza when current, or the species with the highest approved appeal) and one creature known to fail the floor (the Luceras headpass1 render). The question is: "Which of these would a ten-year-old pick as a favorite, and which would they find unfriendly? Why?" The candidate passes when the reader ranks it above the failing reference and gives a reason that is not about color alone. Nick never sees a creature that fails it.

## Where it applies in the pipeline

- **Stage 1, intake:** the reading records the species' appeal target (floor, or how far above it) and how the lore's menace will be expressed without breaking the floor.
- **Stage 3, model sheet:** the sheet's head view is measured against items 1 to 8, and the reader test runs on the sheet. A sheet that fails is regenerated before anything is built.
- **Stage 5, sculpt:** the head part is measured against items 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 and 8, so the sculpt cannot drift from the sheet the way Luceras did.
- **Stage 7, surface:** items 4 and 6, with the reader test at sprite size.
- **Stage 9, animate:** the resting eyes stay round; slit pupils, narrowed lids and bared teeth belong only to acts and expressions.

## Luceras against the floor (2026-10-25)

Luceras headpass1 fails items 1, 2 and 4: the eyes are real-goat size, turned about 46 degrees off the snout line, and the pupil is horizontal. Item 5 is borderline because of the long goat muzzle. The sheet already drew round, forward-facing, larger eyes. An appeal pass brings the eyes to the floor: rounder and larger, turned forward, a round pupil with a highlight, and possibly a slightly shorter muzzle. The horns, coat, body and motion stay as approved.
