## What can wait, and what cannot

Fine hair strands, tiny fur grooves, material roughness variation, eye reflections and final lighting can wait. Production topology, UVs, rigging and animation remain downstream. The facial volumes, eye integration, ear envelope, coat masses, body transitions, joints, paws and tail fusion listed above cannot wait. Most of the visible shortfall is in those forms.

The lowered-arm inspection pose is not itself a defect. Front and back images mirror a single object, and the tail can legitimately foreshorten in profile. Neither fact excuses poor sculptural quality. Do not invent new anatomy failures merely to make the critique longer, or undo the accepted tail architecture while improving its execution.

## Why the review failed

1. The agent and earlier reviewer tested whether named components existed and obvious seams were reduced. They did not enforce comparable form quality against the preferred references.
2. Improvement over rejected 0008 became the implicit benchmark. That is the wrong comparison; the reference remains the benchmark.
3. Technical success was described beside a readiness verdict in a way that gave it artistic weight. Closed surfaces, matching cameras, hashes and a portable ZIP prove reproducibility, not likeness.
4. Repeated parameter changes remained within a limited authoring vocabulary. Calling surfaces "authored" did not make their output sufficiently sculptural. The resulting leaves, tubes and section bands are visible evidence of that limitation.
5. The generated head study was more developed than the model and appeared near the delivery without sufficiently prominent labeling. The user could reasonably mistake the attractive reference for the evaluated output.
6. Current documents contradicted each other: the brief and reference map still named unresolved likeness failures while the package and handoff claimed readiness. No final consistency pass reconciled those statements.
7. The agent treated "no obvious catastrophic defect" as enough to ask Nick to approve. Nick should evaluate meaningful design choices and a sufficiently developed result, not perform the first comprehensive art-quality inspection.
