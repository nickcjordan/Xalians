## 3. Cross-sections and surface

- **Upper arm, round.** Sections perpendicular to the axis are near round: lateral .041 to .043, depth .046 to .047 (sheet: lateral .037 to .044 in the front and back views, measured across the projected axis; depth about .048 in the left view). The model's upper arm is the other way round, wider than deep (.047 to .051 lateral, .043 to .046 deep), and above the armpit, at y .32 to .38, its outer face is flat: in horizontal slices the lateral surface stays within .003 world of its outermost point over .036 to .048 world of depth, where a round arm of this size gives about .031. Target: at every row from .32 to .43 the lateral face stays within .003 world of its maximum over at most .032 world (.017 fit) of depth.
- **No inset tube.** The flat face ends at hard creases where the arm meets the chest in front and the back behind (at y .33 to .38 the slice outline jumps by .03 to .10 world between neighboring .012 world samples). The arm must leave the trunk through one fillet of radius at least .012 fit (.022 world) on the front, back and armpit sides, so that in profile no edge line or bright strip runs down the outer upper arm.
- **Convex and concave.** Front view: outer edge one convex curve over the deltoid, straight down the upper arm, a slight convex corner at the elbow, convex over the forearm swell, a long straight taper to the wrist, then convex over the paw. Inner edge: concave at the armpit fillet, straight down the upper arm, a soft concave turn at the inner elbow, straight to the wrist. Profile: back edge convex over the triceps, a small convex point at the olecranon, convex over the forearm swell; front edge convex over the biceps, a soft concave fold at the inner elbow.
- **Paw.** Dorsum gently domed across, the four lobes reading as one mitten with shallow grooves; palm side flat to slightly cupped; in edge view the paw is as thick as the wrist or thicker for three quarters of its length and then rounds off.
- **Surface.** Smooth short fur over soft muscle, the same idiom as the legs (R08): no ridges, facets, tendon cords, rings or creases; field blends only, no Boolean seams. Claws are the only hard, glossy parts.

## 4. What it must not look like

There is no history card for R07 under this checklist. The baseline critique (assembled-0291, unchanged since 0290) and the gap audit name these looks; the geometric reason for each:

- **A bud, finger or thumb from the front** (R07.5 fail): the edge-on paw thins from .056 to .018 over its last 45 percent (palm ellipsoid ending at s .16 world, lobes smaller than the palm and ending in points), and the claws hide under the lobes.
- **Small pins under the lobes** (R07.3 partial): claw bases .010 behind and .008 palmward of the lobe tips. A claw must leave the end of its lobe.
- **A flat lit slab on the outer upper arm, with a hard lower edge** (R07.1 fail): the lateral face is flat over .036 to .048 world of depth at y .32 to .38 and the arm meets the trunk at creases; the section is wider than deep. It ends at about y .43, where the arm separates from the trunk, which is the hard lower edge.
- **A noodle with no elbow** (R07.2 partial): the arm's only change of direction is spread from .40 to .56, the forearm is .05 to .07 long and as thick as the upper arm, and there is no olecranon or wrist taper.
- **Arms too long** (R12.4): the arm is .366 from the shoulder joint to the paw tip against about .29 on the sheet.
- **Overcorrections to avoid.** Sausage segments: no carved rings at the elbow or the wrist; the wrist is a smooth taper over the lower forearm and the elbow a soft point. Human hand: no separate fingers longer than .025, no thumb or dewclaw, no splayed digits; the lobes stay joined. Oversized paw: the dorsum at most .046 (1.35 wrist widths) and the paw at most .070 long. Needle forearm: the forearm stays at least .042 wide below the elbow. Claws as spikes stuck on the front face (the hind-paw fault in gap-audit rank 6): claws come only out of lobe tips.

Friction for the orchestrator:

1. **`rig_core.derive_joints` cannot find this arm's joints, so `proportions.json` and the posed fit read the arm wrongly.** It searches for the elbow only between z -.24 and .02 (y .475 to .615), so an elbow at the target y .431 (z .101) cannot be found; it sets the wrist at 58 percent of the way from the elbow to the claw tips instead of at the narrowest section; and it puts the shoulder .08 below the neck-base landmark, at y .37, which is .045 below the cap center and below the sheet's own armpit (.35). On the current mesh it put the elbow at y .539, inside the true wrist region, so the reported upperArm .191, forearm .066 and hand .048 are the visual upper arm plus forearm, half the paw, and the other half. Smallest change: search the elbow over z -.24 to .20, take the wrist as the narrowest arm section between the elbow and the claws, and take the shoulder from the arm's own top section; or let the body record its analytic S, E, W, T in its fairing record and have `cmd_posed` use them when present (the builder knows them exactly after this rebuild).
2. **R07.6 measures mostly the trunk and the pose, not the arm.** Its band (.30 to .58 of the tail-free halves) holds the trunk and the hip; the sheet's arm and paw are about 28 percent of the band's pixels in front, 26 percent in back and 12 percent in the side view. The stored sheet pose (`rig/akinza-sheet-pose.json`) was fitted to an older body: refitting it alone moves the current mesh from .830 to .862. Simulations with the packet's vertex dump and `rig_fit` (scripts in the spec-r07 folder): the current mesh with corrected arm joints, refit, .865; a capsule arm built to this spec on the current trunk, refit, .858 and .867 for two runs; the sheet's own arm pixels pasted onto the model's posed trunk, .900 with the stored pose and .93 with the refit poses. So .90 needs a near-perfect arm plus R06's trunk and hip work and a refit. Proposed smallest change: refit the sheet pose once after this rebuild (with fixed joints, friction 1) and lower R07.6 to .86 until R06 lands, or score the arm on arm-only pixels (posed arm-bone vertices against a traced sheet arm region) so the criterion moves with the arm.
3. **Ownership with R05.** The arm root S sets both the cap and the armpit. This spec places S and hangs the arm from it; the cap's outline (R05.5, R05.6) and the armpit height are R05's. Proposed: R05's builder centers the cap on S as given here. The shoulder-width row (R05.3, central maximum over .26 to .34) bounds the cap's outer edge at .152 per side (width .304, ratio 1.03 against the 1.07 limit).
4. **R06 moves the trunk in profile** (its station table brings the chest forward by about .02 to .03 and the upper back forward by up to .05 at .32 to .40). S's depth is therefore given as the trunk's mid depth at y .325 plus .010, not as a fixed world y, so the arm rides with whichever trunk the assembly uses.
