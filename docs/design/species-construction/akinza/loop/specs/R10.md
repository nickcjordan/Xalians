Rules the table encodes:

- **One column, stacked roots.** The three root controls sit at world x -.004 to -.025 (across the midline, so the root covers both rear sides, R11.2) and world z -.09, -.138 and -.135 to -.256. Root radii of .045 to .058 fall inside the body silhouette, cost nothing in the fit, and give the sheath R11 asks for. Do not move the three roots back to one point.
- **Crescent, not leaf.** From root to peak the top edge of each tail must rise or run level (tails 1 and 2) or descend less steeply than the bottom edge (tail 3). The peak radius falls at 40 to 50 % of length. The radius is still at least .63 of the peak at 12 % and .9 of it at 25 %, then tapers evenly: about .63 at 70 %, .35 at 85 % and a point at 100 %.
- **Shingles.** Each lower tail's hidden top edge reaches .011 fit units (.02 world) up under the tail above, over the first 45 % of its length (already in the controls: z and radius each raised .01 there). In depth the upper tail is in front wherever they overlap (99 % and 100 % of overlap pixels with these controls). The first background gap opens at x -.13 between tails 1 and 2 and at x -.14 between tails 2 and 3 (black ticks in panel A). Nearer the root there is no gap.

## 3. Cross-sections and surface

- **Round, full tubes.** r03 shades each tail as a plump round volume with a highlight band along the upper third and a soft core shadow below. The accepted tail probe (`tail-shape-acceptance.json`, probe 0003) was round. Use sweepFlatten .9: depth is .9 of width. The baseline's .62 makes vertical plates that read as leaves and blades from every angle but square-on.
- **Convex top edge, concave bottom edge** in the back view along the outer 60 %. Over the inner 40 % the bottom edge is hidden (tails 1 and 2) or runs straight along the column and tail below.
- **Tip:** a smooth continuation of the arc to a sharp point, radius .034 at 85 % and .001 at 100 %. No change of curvature in the last 15 %.
- **Surface:** smooth, no fur locks, no ridges, no facets. r03's tails are smooth-skinned volumes; the coat idiom (R12) is a separate question. Keep sweep blur (sigma about .009) so the cone joints leave no ribs.
- **Tail 3 lies on the body.** Its column segment and proximal third keep their inner surface within .01 of the buttock surface. If the union forms a lump there, raise that segment's y by .01 to .02 rather than shrinking its radius.

## 4. What it must not look like

There is no history card for R10 under this checklist. The baseline critique (assembled-0205) names these looks. The geometric reason for each:

- **Fingers splayed from one point** (R10.5 partial): the three baseline roots sit together at world z -.03 to -.085, which is fit y .50 to .53, so every tail radiates from one point and gaps open right at the root. Fix: the stacked column above.
- **Leaf or blade tails, narrow at the root** (R10.6 fail): the top edges fall away from the root because the roots are too high (tail 3's top edge is .107 too high at x -.02). sweepFlatten .62 flattens every tail into a plate. Note that r03's back-view width also peaks at 40 to 50 % of length, as the baseline's does. Fullness at the root comes from round sections, stacked roots and no gaps, not from moving the widest point.
- **Downward hook at the tip** (R10.4 fail): in the baseline's last segment the centerline drops 54 degrees over the final 8 % while the radius collapses from .04 to zero. The bottom edge then kinks into a beak. Fix: even curvature, and the taper schedule above.
- **Reversed shingles:** in the baseline the lower tails reach further back than the upper ones at the same x (tail 3 y .31, tail 2 .25, tail 1 .22 at world x .19). From behind, the lower tail therefore sits over the upper one, the reverse of r03. Fix: the depth schedule above.
- **Tail 3 short and high:** its tip at (-.179, .784) sits .03 short of r03.

Friction for the orchestrator:

1. **R10.1 and R10.2 cannot pass on the tails alone.** Replacing the model with r03 inside the whole tail zone gives trunk .700 and legs .709. These controls, with the body under the old tails estimated by mirroring the model's right side, give trunk .680 and legs .705. With that body left bare they give .645 and .704. The rest is body silhouette in the same bands: the arms and torso are wider than the study (trunk extra .283) and the legs are thicker and placed differently. Proposed smallest change: score R10 on the tail footprint IoU (at least .90), and move the band thresholds to the torso, arm and leg regions, or lower them to .69 and .70 for R10.
2. **R10.4's wording contradicts r03.** In the study every tip points out and down (47, 75 and 79 degrees below horizontal), and so do the first sheet's plumes. What rises "up and out" is the tail body leaving the root. A builder who bends the tips upward will move away from r03 and lose overlap. Proposed wording: "Each tail is a full crescent that rises or runs level out of the root and arcs over to a sharp tip continuing the arc outward and down, as in the back study; no hook or kink at the tip."
3. **R10.6's "full near the root" is not a back-view width fact.** r03's widths peak at 40 to 50 % of length, as the baseline's do. The measurable version is round sections plus the stacked-root and no-gap rules above.
