## 4. What it must not look like

There is no history card for R06 under this checklist. The baseline critique (assembled-0265) and the gap audit name these looks; the geometric reason for each:

- **A tube or slab in profile, no waist** (R06.4, R06.9 fail; audit rank 5): front and back edges nearly parallel (front -.078 from .42 to .46, back descending evenly from +.073 to +.032), so the depth is .111 to .144 everywhere. Fix: the back hollow (-.048 at .44) and the forward chest.
- **Turtle posture, chest behind the chin** (R06.8 fail; audit rank 7): the chest front at .36 is -.070, two thousandths ahead of the chin (-.068); the sheet's is .027 ahead. The whole front from .26 to .44 is .017 to .028 too far back.
- **"Shorts" pelvis with a belt line** (R06.3 fail; audit rank 12): the flank holds .067 to .070 from .43 to .47 and steps out .028 between .48 and .50, and the crease at .47 to .48 marks the join. Fix: start the flare at .45, cap it at .015 per .02, blur the join.
- **Athletic back, trapezius hump** (R06.5 partial; audit rank 18): the back edge stands at +.073 at .34 to .36, .036 to .042 behind the sheet's.
- **Overcorrection: wasp waist.** Do not go under .078 deep or .122 wide at .44; the sheet is slim, not pinched.
- **Overcorrection: pigeon chest or a keel.** The chest front must not pass -.102 at .34 to .38, and the forward move must be spread over the full chest width (the lateral fades above), or the midline comes forward as a ridge.
- **Overcorrection: a bustle or a flat rump.** If the lower back comes forward and the rump does not, the back edge steps at .50 to .54; if the rump is cut back too far, the rear reads flat. Keep the rump back edge between +.045 and +.065 at .58 to .60 and rising no faster than .012 per .02 from .50.
- **Faking the posture by moving the head or the shoulders.** The chin and the shoulder joint already sit where the sheet's do; the trunk moves, not the parts on either end of it.

Friction for the orchestrator:

1. **R06.6 and R06.7 measure the arm, not the waist, once the waist is right.** They read the leftmost run of the arms-down left silhouette. With the arms down, the hanging arm covers fit x -.033 to +.030 at .42 to .46 in profile (the sheet's shoulder joint is at the same place, so the arm should not move). Proposed smallest change: compute the model side of R06.6 and R06.7 from the mesh sections in `render/torso.json` (the harness already does this for R06.2, for this reason), or from a left render with the arms hidden. R06.8 reads the front edge and is unaffected.
2. **R06.2 compares against the sheet at .40, not at the waist.** Its sheet value (.102) is the central-run minimum over .36 to .48, and at .41 to .46 the sheet's trunk run no longer contains the frame center, so only .40 and .48 count. A model with the sheet's waist (.082) scores .80 and fails its minimum of .90 while R06.6 passes. Proposed: retire R06.2 (R06.6 measures the same thing correctly), or give it pick `left` over .40 to .48.
3. **R06.10 is capped at about .87 for a symmetric trunk.** Simulated on the posed masks: the side profile of this spec raises the left trunk band from .848 to about .956, but the front band stays at about .78, because the sheet's front drawing places the trunk .019 further to the character's right (relative to the fan) than its back drawing does, the same front-and-back disagreement R08 met with the legs. Proposed: score the posed trunk band after a best horizontal shift within plus or minus .02 per view, or refit the pose's pelvis and spine after this rebuild (`posed --refit`; pelvis and spine are 0 in the current pose), or lower the bar to .87.
4. **The rump depth is hidden by the tails in every sheet view.** The rump numbers (.52 to .62 back edge) are estimates anchored on the visible +.016 to +.021 at .49 to .51 and the back-view buttock cleft and folds. If Nick wants a fuller rear, it is the lever at .56 to .62, and R06.5 should say so.
