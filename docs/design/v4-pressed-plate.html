Design system, version 4 · depth, round two

The pressed plate

One rule, applied everywhere: thickness means pressable. A thing you can press is a plate with visible mass, in its own color one step darker, sitting proud of the surface. It lifts a step under the pointer and drops flat when pressed. Nothing is lit, nothing emits but the viable signal. Flat things stay flat. Hover and press everything on this page.

Keys

Four ranks, one rule. The primary keeps the chamfer and carries the most mass; its thickness is the darker viable. Secondary sits on a darker step of the room. Quiet has no mass because it is a link, not a key. Destructive stands proud in plague and only fills when pressed.

Selected means pressed

A segmented control is a row of keys. The chosen one is already pushed in: flat, on the room tone, with the viable underline it has today. The others stand proud. A filter chip is a small key; a content chip on a record is flat because you cannot press it. A switch's knob is a key that slides.

Tiles are plates you can lift

A catalog tile is pressable, so it has mass: its thickness is its own element, dimmed. The art is the whole face. The record number sits in the data face on the plate; the registry already assigns it.

Flat things stay flat, slots sink

A record card is not pressable, so it has no mass; the keys inside it do. An input is a slot: it sinks into the surface with an inset lip instead of standing proud, so keys and slots are opposites you can tell apart before you touch them. Level-0 surfaces carry the faint layout grid.

The alternative: diagonal mass

The same rule with the thickness offset down and to the right, the way your examples carry it. It reads as a sticker or a stacked card more than a key; I prefer the straight-down version because a key on a panel only has one direction to go. Shown so you can choose.

What changes in the contract

Section 5, depth (replaces "Depth is low")

Depth is thickness, not light. Nothing is lit, nothing glows but the viable signal, nothing is bevelled or glossy. A thing you can press is a plate with visible mass: a solid thickness in its own color one step darker (primary on viable-lo, secondary on the room's darker step, a tile on its element dimmed), 4px on keys and tiles, 3px on small keys, 2px on filter chips. It lifts one pixel under the pointer and drops flat when pressed, in 120 ms. Thickness means pressable: a selected segment is already pressed and sits flat; a card, a panel, a plate, a badge and a content chip have no mass because you cannot press them. A slot (input, textarea, select) sinks with an inset lip. Level-0 surfaces carry a faint 32px layout grid; nothing else does. The one float shadow remains for things that float over the page.

Section 13, deliberately avoided (amended)

  • Remove "drop shadows on things that do not float"; replace with "lit, glossy or bevelled surfaces; mass on anything that cannot be pressed."
  • Keep glow, scanlines, noise overlays and pulsing as they are.

Descriptors (new, section 0)

Instrument. Square, legible, tabular, every control says what it does. Warm. Near-black room, bone ink, the mint as a light in it. Printed. Records, plates and numbers in the data face; the layout grid. Physical. Mass means pressable; flat light. Quiet. One accent, one primary, the world in the content.

Tokens

One new token family, --thick 4 / 3 / 2, and the darker steps derived per color. No new hues.
