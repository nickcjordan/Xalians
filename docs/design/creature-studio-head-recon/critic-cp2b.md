# Hypnopet head, CP2 round 6: independent critic report (cp2b)

Images reviewed: cp2-round6.png, cp2-round6-extra.png, voxel-check.png, targets/flesh-front.png, flesh-side.png, turnaround.png. Only the head and face were judged. Ears, horn and body were not.

## Overall verdict

**Mostly.** The layout is right: big eyes set wide and high, a small nose, an open mouth, and a thin neck. But the mouth and muzzle read as a hissing cat, not a happy bunny, and that flips the character's expression.

## Score

**5 / 10.** The mouth is the main thing holding it below 6. The sheet's wide, upturned, open smile with a dropped jaw has become a small mouth with two fang-like upper-lip points, and the muzzle pads are too broad and catlike.

## Shape defects, ranked by damage to likeness

1. **Mouth shape and expression (front and three-quarter, mouth).** The sheet has a wide open U or D smile. The corners curl up into the cheeks, the lower jaw drops, and the tongue fills the bottom of the opening. In R1 to R3 the opening is short and shallow. The two upper-lip lobes hang down at the center like fangs, and the corners turn down and back. In voxel-check this reads as a snarl. Fix: widen the opening by about 1.5x, lift the corners up and outward toward the cheeks, and drop the lower lip and jaw so the opening is roughly as tall as the nose-to-lip distance. Round off the upper-lip points so the lip reads as a soft inverted-Y split, not two teeth. Real shape defect. Fur will not hide it.

2. **Muzzle pads too broad and puffy (front, whisker pads and nose).** R has a wide, flat-topped nose wedge sitting on two big swollen whisker pads that span almost the full gap between the eyes. That is a cat muzzle. The sheet's nose is a small rounded triangle with a short philtrum line, and its muzzle is narrow and only gently swollen. Fix: narrow the whisker pads by about 30 percent and reduce how far they project. Shrink the nose to about two thirds of its current width and round its top edge. Real shape defect. Fur on the painted turnaround softens the muzzle, but the bare sheet shows it small.

3. **Eyes bulge out of the face silhouette (three-quarter and top views).** In the three-quarter view the far eye sits half outside the head contour, past the nose bridge, like a frog's. In the top view, amber shows beyond the skull outline on both sides. On the sheet, the far eye in three-quarter stays inside the face, with the bridge of the snout overlapping its inner edge. Fix: rotate the eyes about 10 to 15 degrees more toward the front, sink them deeper (or build up the brow and outer cheek around them) so their outer edge sits inside the skull silhouette from above. Real shape defect.

4. **Side profile: pointed, sloping snout and no stop (side view).** The sheet's profile is a round cranium. A clear stop sits just in front of the eye, then a short, blunt snout ends in a round nose bump. In R the forehead runs in one straight slope from the horn base down to a pointed nose, which reads as fox or beak. Fix: raise the brow and forehead in front of the eye so there is a concave stop. Then make the snout tip blunter and rounder, with the nose as a distinct small bump at the front. Real shape defect.

5. **Cranium too low and pinched under the horn (front view, top of skull).** The sheet has a clear dome of forehead between the eye tops and the horn base, roughly half an eye height. R's skull rises to a narrow peak where the horn sits, and the bulging ear roots on each side flatten the top. The head looks short-crowned and triangular. Fix: raise and widen the crown so it reads as a smooth dome from ear root to ear root, with the horn sitting on a broad top, not a point. Real shape defect. Crown fur on the painting exaggerates the height, but the bare sheet also has the dome.

6. **Ridge between the eyes (front view, brow and bridge).** R has a raised vertical ridge running from between the eyes down to the nose, with grooves on each side. This gives a frowning, beaky look. The sheet's bridge is broad, flat and smooth. Fix: flatten and widen the bridge and fill the grooves beside it. Real shape defect.

7. **No jaw or chin undercut at the neck join (side and front views).** On the sheet the dropped lower jaw and chin form a separate rounded mass, with a visible turn under the chin into the throat. In R the throat runs in one straight line from the lower lip down to the chest, and the head reads as a continuation of the neck. Fix: build a rounded chin and lower-jaw mass and carve a soft undercut where the jaw meets the throat. Mostly a real shape defect. Chest fur would partly hide the throat line, but not the chin.

8. **Thin eye lids and sockets (front, around the eyes).** The sheet's eyes sit in soft lids: an upper-lid fold and a lower-lid shelf that wrap about 10 percent over the eyeball. R's eyes sit almost bare on the surface, with only a dark contact groove. Fix: add a soft upper lid that overlaps the top of the eye and a low lower-lid shelf. Real shape defect, and it also feeds defect 3.

## Construction defects

- **Dark contact groove around each eye (voxel-check, both eyes, strongest at the upper rim and lower outer edge).** A thin black ring separates the eyeball from the skin. It reads as a gap or a cut edge, not a lid crease. Either the eye is not seated against the surface or the surface is cut away around it.
- **Surface grain on the cheek (voxel-check right image, right cheek and lower right of the face).** There is a faint stippled or blotchy pattern. It looks like grid or voxel resolution showing through the smoothing.
- **Upper-lip points (voxel-check and front row, mouth center).** The two lip lobes meet at a sharp crease and hang down into the opening as hard points. This is a modeling artifact as well as a shape problem.
- **Shallow mouth cavity (voxel-check, mouth).** The tongue sits nearly flush with the lips, and the dark interior shows only at the two corners. The opening reads as a shallow dent, not a mouth.
- **Eyes poke past the skull outline (top view in extra sheet, both eyes; three-quarter row, far eye).** See defect 3. From above this also reads as a construction fault.
- No holes or seams were visible at the head-to-neck join in the back or top views.

## Eye size

R3 is closest: the sheet's eye is about a third of head height in the side view, and only R3 reaches that. R2 is a close second and matches the front-view width ratio about as well, but R1 is clearly too small.
